## Introduction

UFO, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) and alternative-energy research often appear to form a single technological field because the same small set of terms keeps circulating between them: **antigravity, electrogravitics, zero-point energy, vacuum energy, free energy, [cold fusion]({{ 'cold-fusion/' | relative_url }}), exotic propulsion and breakthrough physics**. The overlap is real at the level of publishing, conferences and speculation, but it does not establish scientific equivalence.

That distinction matters when examining narratives about allegedly suppressed or mysteriously deceased scientists. Once “zero-point energy” can lead to “antigravity”, antigravity to UFO propulsion, and “free energy” to cold fusion, a researcher need not have investigated UFOs at all to be retrospectively placed in a UFO-related suppression story. Alternative-technology sources provide unusually clear examples of this vocabulary-driven convergence, while NASA's own advanced-propulsion work shows why some of the terms genuinely can coexist without implying that all the underlying claims belong to one validated science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980201240.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerNASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program - NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)June 1, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 1998</span></span></span>

## The recurring vocabulary across alternative-technology publishing

The clearest way to see the mechanism is not to start with any individual scientist, but with the way alternative-technology material is organised. *Infinite Energy*, founded by cold-fusion advocate Eugene Mallove, explicitly defines its broad category of “New Energy” to include both cold fusion or low-energy nuclear reactions (LENR) and vacuum or zero-point energy. Its FAQ then discusses “free energy” devices in the same conceptual space as attempts to obtain energy from the quantum vacuum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.infinite-energy.com/resources/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: infinite-energy.com">[Infinite Energy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">infinite-energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Infinite Energy New Energy FAQ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infinite Energy New Energy FAQ</span></span></span>

The magazine's contents show the same boundary-crossing in practice. A 2000 issue could contain technical or speculative pieces on calorimetry, cold fusion, dark matter and a zero-point-energy experiment, while its news section also reported “antigravity claims”. Mallove's own publication list stretches from cold-fusion patents and excess heat to an article entitled “The ‘Lifter’ Phenomenon: Electrogravitics, Antigravity and More” and another on “Tesla and the Aether”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://infinite-energy.com/iemagazine/issue34/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: infinite-energy.com">[Infinite Energy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">infinite-energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Infinite Energy Issue 34 Table of Contents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infinite Energy Issue 34 Table of Contents</span></span></span>

This is important because it demonstrates how a person's **publishing environment** can become broader than his or her principal research identity. Mallove is remembered primarily for championing cold fusion, yet readers encountering his work through this alternative-technology ecosystem also meet antigravity, electrogravitics, Tesla-related ideas and zero-point energy. A later researcher compiling names associated with “suppressed advanced technology” can therefore cross from one subject to another without encountering a sharp editorial boundary.

Integrity Research Institute provides an even more explicit example. Its material places books and presentations on “Free Energy and Antigravity Propulsion”, “The Energy Technology of UFOs”, zero-point energy, electrogravitics and future energy alongside one another. Its catalogue has offered an *Antigravity Report*, material on T. Townsend Brown's electrogravity work, a zero-point-energy introduction and even a publication concerning the Betty Hill UFO story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integrityresearchinstitute.org/orderAll.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integrityresearchinstitute.org">[Integrity Research Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integrityresearchinstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on integrityresearchinstitute.org.</span></span></span>

Conference programming can produce the same effect. The Institute's 2020 Conference on Future Energy included presentations on zero-point energy, gravity effects, unconventional propulsion and cold fusion, as well as a presentation described as moving “Toward a Ufology Textbook”. The subjects were not being presented as one experimentally demonstrated theory, but their appearance within the same event gives them a shared social and intellectual setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integrityresearchinstitute.org/cofe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integrityresearchinstitute.org">[Integrity Research Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integrityresearchinstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Integrity Research Institute COFE: Conference on Future Energy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Integrity Research InstituteCOFE: Conference on Future EnergyAugust 14, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 14, 2020</span></span></span>

That setting is one of the main engines of [field drift]({{ 'field-drift/' | relative_url }}). A reader repeatedly encounters the same researchers, publishers, conferences and vocabulary until the distinctions between *energy source*, *propulsion mechanism*, *gravitational effect* and *observed aerial phenomenon* begin to recede.

## Why shared terminology can imply false scientific equivalence

There is a reason the vocabulary overlaps: some advanced-propulsion questions genuinely touch fundamental physics and energy.

NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics programme, established in 1996, is a useful control case because it shows legitimate proximity without equivalence. NASA described goals including propulsion without propellant mass, extremely rapid space travel and breakthrough energy production. Its 1997 workshop discussed gravity-electromagnetism coupling, vacuum-fluctuation energy, warp drives, wormholes and quantum effects. Crucially, NASA framed these as highly speculative possibilities requiring affordable, credible research capable of producing measurable progress — not as established technologies or interchangeable discoveries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980201240.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerNASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program - NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)June 1, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 1998</span></span></span>

The distinction becomes clearer if the recurring labels are unpacked:

* **Cold fusion/LENR** concerns reported anomalous heat or nuclear effects in particular material systems. The US Department of Energy's 2004 review revisited evidence for LENR after the controversial 1989 cold-fusion episode; it was a question about anomalous nuclear-scale processes, not gravity control or UFO propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ADOE_Report_of_the_Review_of_Low_Energy_Nuclear_Reactions.djvu/1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:DOE Report of the Review of Low Energy Nuclear Reactions.djvu/1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:DOE Report of the Review of Low Energy Nuclear Reactions.djvu/1 - Wikisource, the free online libraryDecember 1, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2004</span></span></span>
* **Zero-point energy** refers, in physics, to the non-zero ground-state energy associated with quantum systems. Observable phenomena associated with quantum vacuum physics do not automatically demonstrate that useful energy can be extracted from the vacuum. A technical assessment of proposed extraction methods found that none had been reliably demonstrated and that major proposed classes faced thermodynamic objections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5893" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Extraction of Zero-Point Energy from the Vacuum: Assessment of Stochastic Electrodynamics-Based Approach as Compared to Other Method...</span></span></span>
* **Antigravity or gravity modification** describes a different ambition: changing gravitational or inertial behaviour, or producing effects interpreted in those terms. It does not follow from the existence of zero-point fluctuations or anomalous heat.
* **UFO/UAP research** begins with observations or reports of unidentified phenomena. An object can remain unidentified without its energy source, propulsion system or even its physical nature being known.

The blurring occurs when a term that is legitimate in one context acquires a stronger meaning in another. “Vacuum energy”, for example, is a recognisable subject in theoretical physics. “Extracting unlimited usable energy from the vacuum” is an additional technological claim. “Using that energy to control gravity” adds another claim, and “therefore explaining UFO propulsion” adds another still.

Each transition requires independent evidence. Vocabulary can conceal those evidentiary steps because the words sound as though they belong to one continuous theory.

### The Casimir effect illustrates the problem

Zero-point energy is particularly effective as a linguistic bridge because quantum-vacuum phenomena have serious scientific standing. The Casimir effect — forces observed between closely spaced bodies — is routinely discussed in relation to quantum-field fluctuations. But even within physics there are different formulations of how best to interpret the effect, including close connections to van der Waals forces. Its observation therefore does not establish the much stronger proposition that an engineering system can extract limitless net power from a vacuum ground state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9901011" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv The Casimir Effect: Physical Manifestations of Zero Point Energy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv The Casimir Effect: Physical Manifestations of Zero Point Energy</span></span></span>

Yet alternative-energy discussions can compress these propositions into a much shorter chain: *zero-point energy is physically real; therefore vacuum energy can be tapped; therefore it could provide enormous power; therefore it might enable antigravity or UFO-like propulsion*. The first proposition does not scientifically validate the later ones.

The terminology nevertheless makes the chain intuitive. That is precisely why vocabulary is such an effective mechanism for expanding a narrative without an obvious moment at which the subject appears to change.

## From “advanced propulsion” to UFO technology

Antigravity provides perhaps the strongest bridge because it already sounds like a description of how a spectacular unidentified craft might fly. Thomas Townsend Brown's electrogravitics work illustrates the historical connection. Brown pursued high-voltage devices that he associated with gravitational effects and also became involved with UFO investigation, helping to establish the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP). Later UFO literature explicitly presented him as someone who had discovered how flying saucers might be propelled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Townsend_Brown" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Thomas Townsend Brown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thomas Townsend Brown</span></span></span>

That history gives later alternative-technology publishing a ready-made vocabulary: **electrogravity → antigravity → advanced propulsion → flying saucers**. Once zero-point energy is proposed as the power source for such propulsion, the energy literature enters the same chain.

A revealing example is journalist Nick Cook's *The Hunt for Zero Point*. Public discussions of Cook's work repeatedly combine zero-point energy with antigravity and secret aerospace research; a Wisconsin Public Radio interview summarised the proposition that zero-point energy could solve energy problems while antigravity might become an early application. Specialist alternative-technology outlets similarly folded Cook's work into their existing coverage of electrogravitics and unconventional energy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ttbook.org/interview/nick-cook-anti-gravity" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ttbook.org">[The Thoughtful Counselor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ttbook.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ttbook.org.</span></span></span>

The resulting narrative structure is powerful because it can run in either direction. A UFO discussion can ask what revolutionary energy source would be needed to power extraordinary flight. A free-energy discussion can ask what revolutionary propulsion technology its proposed energy source might enable. Both routes arrive at the same vocabulary.

But neither route establishes that an unexplained aerial observation was powered by zero-point energy, or that a laboratory claim about unconventional energy has anything to do with UFOs.

## Repetition turns proximity into an apparent research field

The most consequential effect is cumulative. A single publication placing cold fusion beside antigravity would not erase their differences. Repeated association across books, magazines, conferences, radio programmes and websites can.

A book such as *Infinite Energy Technologies*, for example, advertises a range including Tesla, cold fusion, zero-point energy, “power from water”, antigravity and free-[energy claims]({{ 'energy-claims/' | relative_url }}). Its subtitle itself joins “Tesla, Cold Fusion, Antigravity, and the Future of Sustainability”. That is not evidence that the subjects share one physical mechanism; it is evidence that they are being packaged for readers as members of the same **alternative technological frontier**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Infinite-Energy-Technologies/Finley-Eversole/9781594773808" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: simonandschuster.com">[Simon &amp; Schuster]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">simonandschuster.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on simonandschuster.com.</span></span></span>

Integrity Research Institute's archive makes the pattern especially visible over time. Its newsletters and materials move between zero-point energy, fusion, electrogravitics, antigravity, triangular craft and UFO-related material, while its presentations explicitly discuss possible “UFO energy and propulsion systems”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integrityresearchinstitute.org/videos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integrityresearchinstitute.org">[Integrity Research Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integrityresearchinstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Integrity Research InstituteIntegrity Research Institute - Home...</span></span></span>

This produces a kind of semantic network:

**unconventional energy → free energy → zero-point energy → exotic physics → gravity control → antigravity → advanced propulsion → UFO propulsion → secret aerospace technology**

Cold fusion can enter at the first end of that chain even though it has no necessary connection to its last end. A UFO investigator can enter at the other end without ever having conducted an energy experiment. Once both are described as investigating “breakthrough”, “suppressed” or “exotic” technology, however, their original disciplinary distance becomes much less visible.

The important unit is therefore not a shared scientific theory but a **shared vocabulary of technological promise**.

## How repeated association expands suppressed-scientist lists

This semantic network becomes especially consequential when people rather than ideas are being catalogued. Suppose a list begins with researchers directly associated with gravity modification or unconventional aerospace propulsion. If zero-point energy is treated as the likely power source for antigravity, researchers working on vacuum-energy concepts become eligible. If “new energy” is then treated as one broad suppressed field, cold-fusion advocates become eligible too. If Tesla-related electrical inventions or unconventional motors are included under “free energy”, the pool expands again.

No explicit rule has to announce that the category has changed. Each addition can be justified by one neighbouring term.

Mallove demonstrates the mechanism unusually well. His own bibliography contains extensive cold-fusion work but also electrogravitics and antigravity material. *Infinite Energy* itself crossed between cold fusion, zero-point energy and antigravity. Consequently, later narratives need only emphasise the broad label “new-energy researcher” rather than ask the narrower question: **what was the person's actual relationship to UFO or antigravity research?**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://infinite-energy.com/iemagazine/issue34/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: infinite-energy.com">[Infinite Energy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">infinite-energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Infinite Energy Issue 34 Table of Contents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infinite Energy Issue 34 Table of Contents</span></span></span>

That change in classification matters statistically as well as rhetorically. Every expansion of the eligible population supplies additional biographies and therefore additional deaths, [accidents]({{ 'accidents/' | relative_url }}), disappearances, disputes and career setbacks from which a pattern can be constructed. The resulting list may look increasingly impressive while becoming progressively less coherent as a scientific sample.

It can also become circular. Researchers are grouped together because they are said to belong to a suppressed technological field; their misfortunes are then presented as evidence that such a field was being suppressed. The shared vocabulary that created the group disappears from view, making the category look more objective than it was.

## A practical test for spotting field drift

The safest way to read these lists is to replace broad labels with specific propositions. Instead of asking whether someone worked on “advanced energy”, “new physics” or “antigravity”, ask what the documented research actually concerned and what evidence connects it to the next field in the chain.

NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics work shows why this discipline is necessary. Serious researchers can examine gravity, vacuum physics and advanced propulsion in one programme precisely because they are testing whether useful relationships exist. The co-location of hypotheses is not itself the result.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980201240.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerNASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program - NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)June 1, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 1998</span></span></span>

The same caution applies on the UFO side. The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office reported in its 2024 historical review that it found no evidence that a US government investigation had confirmed a UAP sighting as extraterrestrial technology and no empirical evidence that the government or private companies had reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology. Whatever disputes remain around particular UAP cases, the existence of speculative research into exotic propulsion cannot simply be used to fill that evidentiary gap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

For suppressed-scientist narratives, three questions therefore keep the categories clear: **What did this person actually research? What evidence connects that work specifically to UFOs or gravity modification? And is the connection technical, documentary and contemporary, or merely a later association through terms such as “free energy”, “zero point” or “exotic technology”?**

Those questions do not require dismissing unconventional research. They simply prevent vocabulary from doing evidentiary work that experiments and documents have not done.

## The key distinction is association, not equivalence

The UFO–energy blur is best understood as a network of repeated associations rather than evidence for one concealed scientific discipline. There are genuine points of contact: advanced spacecraft require energy; speculative propulsion research can involve fundamental physics; vacuum phenomena are legitimate subjects of physics; and institutions such as NASA have deliberately examined several frontier concepts together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19980017317" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerBreakthrough Propulsion Physics Workshop Preliminary Results - NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)...</span></span></span>

But the stronger synthesis — cold fusion, zero-point-energy extraction, antigravity and UFO propulsion as different manifestations of the same suppressed technology — does not follow from those overlaps.

Alternative-technology publishing shows how easily that stronger impression can arise. The same outlets, conferences and personalities repeatedly place these concepts beside one another, until shared vocabulary begins to substitute for shared evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.infinite-energy.com/resources/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: infinite-energy.com">[infinite-energy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">infinite-energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Infinite Energy New Energy FAQ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infinite Energy New Energy FAQ</span></span></span>

That is the mechanism by which research-field drift enlarges suppressed-scientist narratives. A scientist does not have to cross from cold fusion into UFO research personally. The vocabulary can make the crossing on the scientist's behalf.
