## Introduction

Huntsville attracts secret-propulsion theories for a reason that is less exotic, but in some ways more interesting, than the theories themselves. Few American cities place so much genuine rocket engineering, missile research, space intelligence, university science and cleared defence work in one tightly connected community. NASA’s Marshall Space Flight Center sits on Redstone Arsenal alongside Army missile organisations, the Missile Defense Agency and the Defense Intelligence Agency’s Missile and Space Intelligence Center. Nearby, the University of Alabama in Huntsville conducts propulsion research for government and industry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/redstone/units-tenants" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[army.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home Units/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army HomeUnits/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone...</span></span></span>

That concentration creates unusually fertile conditions for speculation. A researcher can publish openly at a university, take government or contractor work, obtain a security clearance and largely disappear from the scholarly literature without literally disappearing. Huntsville even has a striking real example: physicist Ning Li, whose published work on superconductors and gravity was followed by defence-funded work and decades of rumours that she had vanished. Later reporting found that she had remained in Huntsville and eventually died there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. The result is an important distinction for [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) research: Huntsville provides unusually strong evidence for **secrecy around advanced aerospace work**, but that is not the same as evidence for a successful secret [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) or UFO-reverse-engineering programme.

## Why Huntsville is a major propulsion and defence hub

The physical geography matters. Marshall Space Flight Center is not merely somewhere in the same metropolitan area as Redstone Arsenal: NASA describes Marshall as being situated **on** the Army installation. Marshall has more than 6,000 workers and remains heavily involved in propulsion, advanced concepts, materials, testing and space-transportation systems. Its propulsion capabilities include liquid and solid propulsion, thrust-vector control, component development and propulsion research laboratories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/marshall/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marshall Space Flight Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marshall Space Flight Center - NASAJanuary 27, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2026</span></span></span>

That institutional arrangement grew directly from Huntsville's missile history. In 1950, Wernher von Braun's rocket team moved to Redstone Arsenal, where it developed Army Redstone and Jupiter missiles and early launch vehicles. In 1960, the technical core of the Army Ballistic Missile Agency became Marshall Space Flight Center. NASA consequently inherited an established concentration of propulsion expertise rather than building a civilian space centre from scratch somewhere disconnected from military rocketry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://technology.nasa.gov/blog-meet-msfc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: technology.nasa.gov">[NASA Technology Transfer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">technology.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technology TransferCenter Spotlight: Meet Marshall Space Flight Center &#124; T2 PortalMarch 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The military side did not disappear when NASA arrived. Redstone today lists among its tenant organisations the Army's DEVCOM Aviation & Missile Center, Army Aviation and Missile Command, Army Space and Missile Defense Command, Missile Defense Agency, Redstone Test Center, Program Executive Office Missiles and Space and the DIA's Missile and Space Intelligence Center (MSIC), as well as Marshall itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/redstone/units-tenants" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[Army Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home Units/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army HomeUnits/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone...</span></span></span>

Some of those organisations work on subjects that naturally resemble the ingredients of a hypothetical secret-propulsion programme even when their actual missions are conventional. DEVCOM's Aviation & Missile Center describes itself as the Army's focal point for aviation and missile research, development and engineering. Its published core competencies have included missile propulsion, materials and structures, guidance and control, prototype development, modelling and systems engineering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://devcom.army.mil/centerslabs/avmc/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: devcom.army.mil">[DEVCOM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">devcom.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DEVCOMDEVCOM Aviation &amp; Missile Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DEVCOMDEVCOM Aviation &amp; Missile Center</span></span></span>

MSIC adds an intelligence dimension. The DIA says its Redstone centre analyses foreign missile systems and has facilities large enough to accommodate missile hardware for hands-on and virtual testing and evaluation. Access itself illustrates the everyday reality of secrecy in Huntsville: visitors without appropriate [credentials]({{ 'credentials/' | relative_url }}) require advance authorisation from MSIC security before entering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dia.mil/About/Organization/MSIC/lang/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dia.mil">[Defense Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dia.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Intelligence Agency MSIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Intelligence Agency MSIC</span></span></span>

None of this demonstrates unconventional propulsion. It does explain why an outsider trying to infer activities from job histories, buildings, contracts or incomplete résumés encounters an unusually difficult problem. In Huntsville, perfectly real propulsion development, foreign-technology exploitation, missile intelligence, classified engineering and civilian space research coexist within the same local professional network.

## Researchers can cross institutional boundaries without leaving Huntsville

The University of Alabama in Huntsville (UAH) is an especially important bridge between the open and restricted sides of this ecosystem. Its Propulsion Research Center conducts fundamental and applied research in liquid, solid, electric and air-breathing propulsion. Its stated customers include NASA, defence organisations, the Department of Energy, the Missile Defense Agency and aerospace companies. The centre explicitly says that it can work in International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and proprietary environments when required.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uah.edu/prc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uah.edu">[UAH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uah.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Propulsion Research Center &#124; The University of Alabama in Huntsville</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Propulsion Research Center &#124; The University of Alabama in Huntsville</span></span></span>

The technical range is broad enough to show why the word *propulsion* alone reveals little about how exotic a project really is. Current UAH research areas include solid and hybrid propellants, liquid engines, injectors, micro-thrusters, electric spacecraft propulsion, plasma-assisted combustion, nuclear-thermal propulsion systems integration and fusion propulsion and power. The centre's director describes current programmes involving hypersonic boost propulsion, centrifugal nuclear propulsion, supercritical injection and ramjets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uah.edu/prc/capabilities/plasmas-combustion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uah.edu">[UAH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uah.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uah.edu.</span></span></span>

Consequently, a career transition that looks dramatic on paper can be mundane within the Huntsville labour market. A graduate student or university researcher can acquire expertise relevant to NASA, Army laboratories, missile-defence programmes or contractors without changing cities or even abandoning the underlying engineering speciality. Conversely, someone leaving an open academic environment for [classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}) may stop producing the papers, conference presentations and detailed project descriptions by which outsiders previously followed their career.

Redstone's own administrative pages show how normal that security infrastructure is. Its security office handles clearance processing, reinvestigations, operations security, information security and industrial security for supported organisations. Installation badges can also facilitate access to secure activities requiring specific access codes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/redstone/index.php/my-fort/newcomers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[Army Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home For Newcomers:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Home For Newcomers:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone</span></span></span>

This creates an important evidential trap in unconventional-propulsion discussions. **A break in a publication record is evidence that public information stopped; it is not evidence of why it stopped.** In Huntsville, classified employment is one plausible explanation. Other ordinary explanations include a change of speciality, proprietary commercial work, management duties, retirement or personal circumstances. Establishing that a scientist subsequently held a clearance strengthens the classified-work explanation, but still does not identify what the classified project actually concerned.

## Ning Li shows how the secrecy narrative forms

The case of Ning Li is unusually valuable because it contains both the ingredients that generate a compelling conspiracy story and later evidence that corrects some of its most dramatic elements.

Li and Douglas Torr worked at UAH's Center for Space Plasma and Aeronomic Research. Their papers were not merely internet speculation: the American Physical Society published their work in *Physical Review D* in 1991 and *Physical Review B* in 1992. Those papers explored possible gravitational or gravitomagnetic effects associated with superconductors. That establishes a genuine academic research trail in Huntsville involving gravity and superconductivity. It does **not** establish experimental antigravity propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.43.457" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsEffects of a gravitomagnetic field on pure superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. D...</span></span></span>

Li later left UAH and formed AC Gravity. Huntsville Business Journal reported that the company received a US Department of Defense award of $448,970 in 2001 and that Li's subsequent public publication record largely stopped. That combination — an antigravity-associated researcher, a defence award and then an absence of published results — supplied almost the ideal raw material for claims that a breakthrough had been classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

For years, the [information vacuum]({{ 'info-vacuums/' | relative_url }}) accumulated additional stories: that Li had disappeared, that the Pentagon could no longer find her, or that she had returned to China. Yet local reporting eventually located her son, George Guangyu Men, who said his mother had not disappeared or defected. According to his account, she remained involved in Department of Defense work and had obtained a top-secret clearance that prevented her from discussing it with her family. The same report says Li was struck by a vehicle on the UAH campus in 2014, suffered permanent brain injury, never returned to work and died in 2021. Her funeral-home obituary independently records her death in Huntsville on 27 July 2021.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

This does not resolve the technical question of what AC Gravity achieved. The Huntsville Business Journal reporter said Freedom of Information Act requests for the results of Li's defence-funded work had been denied, leaving an authentic documentary gap. But the later family account substantially changes the human story: a supposed mysterious disappearance becomes a researcher moving from public academia into restricted defence work, followed years later by a serious accident and illness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

For the wider question of allegedly suspicious deaths or disappearances among unconventional-propulsion researchers, that difference is crucial. Li's case demonstrates how **loss of public visibility can be retrospectively transformed into physical disappearance**. Her eventual death does not, on the evidence available, establish that she was killed because of her research.

## Huntsville also touched genuinely speculative propulsion research

The city's connection to unusual propulsion ideas is not solely a product of later UFO culture. NASA itself pursued a Breakthrough Propulsion Physics programme beginning in the 1990s. Its published objectives included examining the scientific foundations of propulsion requiring no propellant, extremely high transit speeds and new energy sources. NASA documents explicitly mention gravity-electromagnetism coupling, vacuum-fluctuation energy, warp drives and wormholes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980201240.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span></span></span>

The organisational detail is revealing. The programme was managed by NASA's Lewis Research Center, now Glenn Research Center, but formed part of a wider Advanced Space Transportation Plan managed by Marshall. In other words, Huntsville really was connected institutionally to a NASA effort whose public documents used language that today sounds remarkably close to discussions of exotic propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980201240.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Technical Reports Server NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program</span></span></span>

Yet those same documents describe the concepts as beyond existing science and emphasise the need for credible, affordable experiments and measurable progress. NASA's later guidance on purported mechanical antigravity is similarly cautious: after receiving large numbers of proposed breakthrough devices, it warned that oscillating masses, gyroscopes and similar systems can falsely appear to produce net thrust through experimental artefacts, and recommended minimum evidential thresholds before extraordinary claims receive further attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20070004897" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Technical Reports Server Responding to Mechanical Antigravity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Technical Reports Server Responding to Mechanical Antigravity</span></span></span>

This history therefore cuts both ways. It refutes the simplistic claim that government agencies would never investigate something labelled antigravity or breakthrough propulsion. They demonstrably have. But it also refutes the reverse inference that official investigation means successful development. NASA could study gravity modification precisely because it **did not know** whether any viable mechanism existed.

The same caution applies to modern Huntsville propulsion work. Marshall openly describes advanced and alternative propulsion as part of its remit and operates facilities capable of testing propulsion components, subsystems, subscale motors and full-scale engines. UAH openly investigates electric propulsion, nuclear propulsion concepts and energetic systems. The existence of unfamiliar laboratories, restricted test activity or unusual technical terminology around Huntsville therefore has a large conventional explanation before any hidden gravity-control programme needs to be invoked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/marshall/about-marshall-space-flight-center/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Marshall Space Flight Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About Marshall Space Flight Center - NASA...</span></span></span>

## Why real secrecy amplifies extraordinary interpretations

Huntsville's unusual feature is not simply that classified projects exist. It is the proximity of several different kinds of secrecy.

A university scientist may work with a defence customer. A contractor may support several agencies. NASA engineers and Army researchers can operate inside the same enormous federal installation. Missile intelligence specialists examine foreign technology at MSIC. Army counterintelligence operates at Redstone to protect defence information and technologies from foreign intelligence services and insider threats. More than 65 tenant organisations operate on the Arsenal according to its garrison leadership.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/redstone/redstone-arsenal-field-office-army-counterintelligence-se-region-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[Army Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home Units / Tenants</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Home Units / Tenants</span></span></span>

Under those conditions, fragments of accurate information can produce an inaccurate whole. Consider a hypothetical chain of facts: a physicist publishes an unusual propulsion paper; later works for a defence customer; acquires a clearance; stops publishing; colleagues cannot discuss the project; and a Freedom of Information request produces little or nothing useful. Every step could be true without the underlying programme having produced antigravity, recovered UFO technology or any other extraordinary capability.

Compartmentalisation intensifies the ambiguity because even insiders may possess only part of the picture. A propulsion specialist may know the engineering problem but not the intelligence source behind a requirement; an intelligence analyst may know a foreign system is being exploited without knowing every technical conclusion; a contractor may know its own work package without knowing what parallel teams are doing. From outside that system, the resulting gaps can resemble deliberate concealment of a single coherent secret.

This mechanism is consistent with the broader findings of the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO). Its 2024 historical review specifically investigated allegations that government and contractors possessed recovered off-world technology and secret reverse-engineering programmes, including through research into classified and special-access programmes. AARO said it found no empirical evidence that the US Government or private companies had been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span></span></span>

[KONA BLUE]({{ 'kona-blue/' | relative_url }}) provides a particularly useful comparison. Interviewees had described it as a sensitive government compartment associated with retrieval and exploitation of non-human material. AARO subsequently found that KONA BLUE had actually been a **proposed** Special Access Program that was never approved or formally established, received no materials and received no funding. A real [classified-program]({{ 'secret-programs/' | relative_url }}) proposal had thus become evidence, in later recollections, for a much more consequential programme than the documentary record supported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Next-AARO-Home-redesign/Inactive-Parent/Next-AARO-UAP-DT-Records/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on aaro.mil.</span></span></span>

That does not prove every classified-program allegation false, nor does it make AARO's conclusions immune from criticism. It does demonstrate why programme status matters. A concept study, proposal, restricted experiment, intelligence exploitation effort and operational breakthrough programme are different things. Huntsville contains enough genuine examples of the first four categories that confusing one for the fifth is particularly easy.

## The strongest conclusion is narrower than the mythology


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Huntsville deserves its place in discussions of classified unconventional propulsion because the underlying institutional landscape is real. It has an extraordinary concentration of rocket and missile expertise; NASA and military organisations share Redstone Arsenal; UAH performs propulsion research for government and industry; security clearances and restricted projects are routine features of the local technical economy; and NASA's historical research genuinely extended into speculative subjects such as gravity-electromagnetism coupling and propellantless propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/redstone/units-tenants" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[army.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home Units/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army HomeUnits/Tenants:: Official Page: Redstone Arsenal / Team Redstone...</span></span></span>

</div>

Ning Li's history makes the ambiguity unusually concrete. She genuinely published gravity-related superconductivity research at UAH. Her later company genuinely received defence funding. Her public scientific trail genuinely became much harder to follow. Later reporting says she genuinely entered classified work. But the widely repeated claim that she mysteriously disappeared turned out to be wrong: her family said she remained in Huntsville, she was badly injured in a 2014 traffic accident, and her death in 2021 is publicly documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[aps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. BSeptember 1, 1992...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 1992</span></span></span>

That is the central lesson of Huntsville's role in secret-propulsion speculation. The city makes the **premise of secrecy** unusually credible while making the **contents of that secrecy** unusually difficult to infer. A scientist going quiet there may indeed have entered classified work. A restricted programme may indeed concern advanced propulsion, missiles, foreign technology or unusual physics. Neither fact supplies the missing evidential step needed to conclude that antigravity has been achieved, UFO technology has been reverse-engineered, or researchers have been harmed to protect such a programme.
