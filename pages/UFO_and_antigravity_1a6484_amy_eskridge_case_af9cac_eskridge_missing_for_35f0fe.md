## Introduction

Amy Eskridge died in Huntsville, Alabama, on 11 June 2022, aged 34. Public reporting in 2026 consistently describes the official conclusion as suicide by a self-inflicted gunshot wound. What the public still cannot readily examine, however, is the forensic record behind that conclusion: no publicly accessible autopsy report, toxicology report or comprehensive police investigative file has surfaced in the reporting reviewed for this case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That distinction is central. The available evidence does **not** establish an alternative cause of death, and the absence of published records is not evidence that authorities falsified their finding. But it does mean that outsiders cannot independently test the [suicide ruling]({{ 'suicide-ruling/' | relative_url }}) against the usual underlying evidence — wound characteristics, firearm and scene evidence, toxicology, post-mortem findings, witness accounts and investigative chronology. In Eskridge's case, therefore, the strongest evidence-based question is narrower than many UFO-related claims surrounding her death: **what evidence supports the official ruling, and why is so little of that evidence publicly inspectable?**

The description of the death as suicide comes instead through later reporting. Multiple 2026 accounts state that authorities determined that Eskridge died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound. Coverage has variously attributed that conclusion to local authorities or the Madison County coroner, while later compilations of the available record describe the local case as having been closed on that basis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[hindustantimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span>

The wording matters because a **cause of death** and a **manner of death** answer different questions. A gunshot wound describes the physical mechanism causing death; suicide describes the conclusion that the fatal injury was intentionally self-inflicted. Establishing the second ordinarily depends on more than establishing the first. Investigators can consider the wound and firearm evidence, scene reconstruction, accessibility of the weapon, fingerprints or other trace evidence where relevant, witness statements, communications, medical history and other circumstances.

None of that means every suicide investigation must generate a publicly released dossier. It does mean that a bare statement such as "self-inflicted gunshot wound" cannot, by itself, show an outside reader how investigators excluded accident or another person's involvement.

That is the position of Eskridge's case in the accessible record. There is a repeatedly reported official conclusion, but the primary forensic documentation that would permit outsiders to audit the reasoning behind it has not accompanied that conclusion in public reporting. Reviews specifically searching for primary documentation have likewise found the ruling repeated without locating a released police investigative file or medical-examiner autopsy report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/justice/amy-eskridge-autopsy-police-report-how-to-obtain-7bb268" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl...</span></span></span>

## Which records have not surfaced publicly?

The evidence gap becomes clearer when the missing material is separated into its component parts. As of the reporting reviewed in 2026, no accessible source identified a publicly released comprehensive police report or autopsy report for Eskridge, and searches focused specifically on those documents did not locate copies attached to the news coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/justice/amy-eskridge-autopsy-police-report-how-to-obtain-7bb268" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl...</span></span></span>

The most consequential absent records are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The autopsy or post-mortem report.** Such a document could establish whether a full autopsy was conducted and, if so, document the fatal injury, wound path, range-related findings and other physical observations relevant to determining whether a gunshot was self-inflicted.
* **The toxicology results.** These could establish what substances, if any, were detected and at what concentrations. Toxicology would not determine suicide on its own, but it could materially inform reconstruction of Eskridge's condition around the time of death.
* **The police investigative file.** Depending on what the investigation produced, this could contain the scene narrative, photographs, witness interviews, firearm information, evidence inventories, chronology and the basis for treating the death as self-inflicted rather than suspicious.
* **A detailed coroner or medical-examiner determination.** Public reporting supplies the conclusion, but an underlying document explaining the factual basis for the manner-of-death classification has not surfaced alongside it.
* **Associated forensic evidence.** The accessible reporting does not provide a sufficiently detailed account of firearm examination, trace evidence, gunshot-residue testing, fingerprints, scene reconstruction or other tests to establish which were performed and what they showed.

</div>

This list should not be misread as proof that the records do not exist. The more defensible statement is that they **have not been located in the publicly accessible record examined by journalists and researchers**. Recent source reviews explicitly caution that the available reporting neither reproduces the documents nor establishes a verified public route to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/justice/amy-eskridge-autopsy-police-report-how-to-obtain-7bb268" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report Availabl...</span></span></span>

That distinction is particularly important with the toxicology record. It is tempting to say that "no toxicology exists" because none is publicly visible. The evidence supports only the weaker conclusion: no publicly accessible toxicology report was identified. Without the autopsy file, it is not even possible for an outside reader to establish from primary documentation exactly which forensic examinations were ordered.

## Alabama's records rules complicate the gap

The absence of Eskridge's death certificate from general public circulation has a straightforward legal explanation that does not require a cover-up hypothesis. The Alabama Department of Public Health says [death certificates]({{ 'death-certificate/' | relative_url }}) are confidential for **25 years from the date of death**. During that period, certified copies are restricted to specified relatives, estate representatives and other authorised people. Eskridge died in 2022, so her certificate is not an unrestricted public record under that rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthDeath Certificates &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)March 27, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 27, 2026</span></span></span>

That is an important corrective to claims that every missing document must reflect unusual secrecy. At least one potentially useful primary record is subject to an ordinary statewide access restriction.

The death certificate would nevertheless answer only part of the question. It would ordinarily record certified cause and manner; it would not substitute for the underlying investigative evidence explaining *why* the manner was classified as suicide. For an independent review, the more important documents would be the post-mortem findings and investigative materials.

Nor should the lack of an online police file automatically be treated as exceptional. Law-enforcement investigative material can be subject to disclosure limitations, particularly where privacy, evidentiary or investigative interests apply. The correct transparency question is therefore not simply "Why isn't everything online?" It is whether the responsible agencies can identify what records were created, which can legally be disclosed, which cannot, and the legal basis for any withholding.

That more precise framing separates two issues often blurred together in discussion of Eskridge: **legal availability** and **forensic existence**. A record may exist without being publicly obtainable; another may be obtainable only by particular people; still another examination may never have been performed. Without agency documentation, outsiders cannot safely infer one category from another.

## Why Eskridge's pre-death statements do not settle the forensic question

Renewed scrutiny in 2026 has been driven partly by communications attributed to Eskridge before her death. Franc Milburn, a former British military and intelligence figure who says he communicated with her, has publicised a message in which Eskridge reportedly said that if reports appeared saying she had killed herself, that was not what had happened. Recent news coverage has repeated the message while discussing the official suicide determination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[hindustantimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span>

That communication is relevant because investigators assessing manner of death can consider a person's statements, behaviour and circumstances before death. A specific advance denial of suicidal intent is therefore not meaningless.

It is also not dispositive forensic evidence.

A person's earlier declaration that they would not die by suicide cannot establish what happened later. Intent can change, statements can have contexts that are not apparent from excerpts, and the authenticity, date and complete conversation surrounding privately retained messages matter. Conversely, investigators should not simply ignore a well-authenticated, temporally relevant statement that appears inconsistent with their eventual conclusion.

This is precisely where the missing investigative record becomes important. An accessible case file could show whether investigators knew of such statements in 2022, interviewed the relevant contacts, examined Eskridge's devices or communications, and reconciled apparently contradictory evidence with the physical scene. Without that record, the public cannot tell whether these issues were investigated and resolved, investigated but deemed irrelevant, or were unknown to the original investigators.

The same caution applies to more extraordinary allegations attributed to Eskridge concerning stalking, threats or directed-energy attacks. Their existence as claims does not establish that those events occurred, much less that they caused her death. Public reporting has not produced forensic evidence connecting such alleged attacks to the fatal gunshot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/uncovered-videos-texts-amy-eskridge-death-1793433" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ibtimes.co.uk">[International Business Times UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ibtimes.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uncovered videos texts amy eskridge death 1793433</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">uncovered videos texts amy eskridge death 1793433</span></span></span>

The evidentially sound position is therefore symmetrical: Eskridge's statements should not be transformed into proof of murder, but neither can outsiders determine how authorities evaluated them when the investigative record supporting the suicide ruling remains inaccessible.

## What would actually allow an independent review?

The most useful new evidence would not be another retrospective theory about [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) research. It would be primary documentation from the death investigation.

An autopsy or post-mortem report could establish what examination occurred and provide the physical findings behind the cause-of-death determination. Toxicology could clarify whether drugs, alcohol, medications or other substances were present. Police records could establish who discovered Eskridge, the condition of the scene, the location and ownership of the firearm, what physical evidence was collected, which witnesses were interviewed and whether investigators found indications inconsistent with suicide.

A detailed coroner's record could then connect those pieces to the ultimate manner-of-death determination.

Those documents would also make it possible to test some claims that currently circulate largely through secondary reporting. For example, assertions about the timing of any autopsy or cremation have been attributed to acquaintances rather than demonstrated through publicly posted official records. Reviews of the case have specifically warned that such claims should not be treated as established forensic facts without the relevant documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/justice/amy-eskridge-autopsy-police-report-how-to-obtain-18e2a6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report for Amy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is There an Official Autopsy or Police Report for Amy...</span></span></span>

This is why the records question has greater evidential value than speculation over individual anomalies. A complete primary record could strengthen the suicide ruling substantially. It could show a coherent scene, internally consistent forensic findings and an investigation that considered the relevant circumstances. Alternatively, it could expose unresolved contradictions or missing investigative steps. At present, public readers cannot perform either assessment.

## The evidence gap is not evidence of homicide

The most important limitation runs in both directions.

No publicly available forensic record examined here establishes that Eskridge was murdered. The reported threats, her alleged advance denial of suicide and the unusual subject matter of her research provide reasons people have questioned the official account, but they do not supply a competing forensic reconstruction of her death. Recent reporting on wider claims involving dead or missing researchers has likewise noted that authorities have not publicly established a common connection among those cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nypost.com/2026/04/17/us-news/string-of-missing-of-dead-scientists-too-coincidental-congressman-says-as-a-11th-researcher-revealed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nypost.com">[New York Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nypost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Among the cases is Air Force Maj. At the same time, saying that homicide has not been demonstrated is different from saying that the publicly available evidence independently proves suicide. The latter requires access to evidence that the public presently does not have.

Eskridge's obituary illustrates the limits of what can be established directly. It independently confirms her identity, age and date of death, but it says nothing about a gunshot, suicide, autopsy, toxicology or police findings. Those details enter the public account principally through later reports describing what authorities concluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

The responsible assessment therefore has three levels of certainty. **First**, Amy Eskridge died on 11 June 2022. **Second**, multiple sources report that authorities classified her death as suicide by self-inflicted gunshot wound. **Third**, the underlying forensic and investigative documentation necessary for an outsider to independently verify that classification has not surfaced publicly in the sources reviewed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That third point is the unresolved evidence gap. It is narrower than claims that Eskridge was killed because of antigravity or UFO research, but it is also more defensible. Until primary records become available, the suicide ruling remains the reported official conclusion, while the evidential pathway that produced it remains largely outside public view.
