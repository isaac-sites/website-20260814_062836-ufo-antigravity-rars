## Introduction

James E. McDonald’s death is often included in lists of UFO researchers allegedly silenced because of what they knew. Yet one fact materially changes the evidential balance: before his fatal gunshot wound in June 1971, McDonald had already survived a serious suicide attempt only a few months earlier. Contemporary and retrospective reporting describes both incidents as involving self-inflicted gunshots and notes, while the later death was officially ruled suicide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_chronology_test_4f29a5_mcdonald_earlier_sui_401336-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Earlier Attempt illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_chronology_test_4f29a5_mcdonald_earlier_sui_401336-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_chronology_test_4f29a5_mcdonald_earlier_sui_401336-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That earlier attempt does not logically prove that the second shooting was also self-inflicted. It does, however, create a documented alternative causal pathway that any homicide or assassination theory must explain. A theory that concentrates on McDonald’s UFO advocacy, institutional conflicts or government attention while omitting his recent suicidal crisis leaves out evidence unusually close in time and character to the death itself. The central question is therefore not whether McDonald had powerful opponents — he plainly had contentious public disputes — but whether there is affirmative evidence that somebody intervened to cause his death despite the preceding attempt.

## What is known about the earlier 1971 attempt

McDonald was not a marginal UFO enthusiast whose scientific identity rested on the subject. He was a University of Arizona atmospheric physicist with an established career in cloud physics, weather modification and related fields. The American Meteorological Society’s 1971 obituary recorded his academic career and his position as professor of atmospheric sciences at the university, while the University of Arizona archive preserves extensive papers from his scientific and UFO investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/JEMcDonald/mcdonald_obituary_bams_71.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirkmcd.princeton.edu">[Kirk McDonald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirkmcd.princeton.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kirk Mc Donald SHG</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kirk McDonaldSHG - McCarthy Dissertation...June 26, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2009</span></span></span> His appearance at the 1968 House UFO symposium likewise came with a formal introduction describing his distinguished scientific career.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/1968-07-29-house-symposium-on-ufos-committee-print-7-house-symposium-on-ufos-1968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">House of Representatives (90th Congress, 2d Sess, No. 7), U.S. House Committee on Science and Astronautics · 1968 · UFO TransparencyJuly...</span></span></span>

That standing helps explain why his death later became attractive material for suppression theories. McDonald publicly argued that the UFO problem had been inadequately investigated and challenged governmental and scientific conclusions about it. A declassified contemporary press clipping in CIA holdings even records his criticism of official efforts to dismiss UFO reports, although it also records him rejecting the idea that the CIA itself was conducting a conspiracy to conceal the truth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP75-00149R000500070001-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">STATScientist Accuses CIA of World Plot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">STATScientist Accuses CIA of World PlotMarch 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>

The crucial chronology comes in 1971. The *Arizona Daily Star*’s retrospective publication of material from the Tucson newspaper archive states that McDonald attempted suicide several months before his death by shooting himself in the head and that he left a note on that occasion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span> Later biographical accounts place the attempt in April 1971 and describe severe resulting injuries, including damage to his sight and a period of psychiatric hospitalisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos.wiki/figure/james-e-mcdonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos.wiki">[UFOs Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos.wiki</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki James E. Mc Donald</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">James E. McDonald - UFOs Wiki...</span></span></span>

The exact secondary accounts differ on some medical details — particularly the extent and permanence of his blindness — so those details should not be made to carry more weight than the evidence permits. The core event is much better supported: there was a serious, non-fatal suicide attempt shortly before the fatal episode. The Tucson archival account is especially important because it does not merely repeat a modern internet biography: it states that McDonald had attempted suicide a few months earlier and that he left notes on both occasions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span>

The interval was short. On 13 June 1971, McDonald was found dead near the Cañada del Oro wash outside Tucson. A revolver was found beside him, and the death was ruled suicide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span> The *New York Times* reported his death at the time as suicide, and later reference records continue to cite that contemporary report for both the manner of death and the gunshot injury.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://handwiki.org/wiki/Biography%3AJames_E._McDonald" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: handwiki.org">[HandWiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">handwiki.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hand Wiki Biography:James E. Mc Donald</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biography:James E. McDonald - HandWiki...</span></span></span>

This produces a sequence very different from the simplified version sometimes encountered in suspicious-death compilations:

**personal and professional crisis → serious suicide attempt → survival and medical consequences → short recovery interval → second gunshot episode with another note → [suicide ruling]({{ 'suicide-ruling/' | relative_url }}).**

That sequence is not proof beyond every conceivable doubt. It is, however, evidence that directly bears on causation.

## Why the first attempt changes the homicide question

The earlier attempt matters because causal explanations should account for evidence that precedes the event they are intended to explain. If McDonald had never previously displayed suicidal behaviour and had suddenly been found shot after receiving documented threats connected with his UFO work, homicide would begin from a substantially different evidential position. Instead, the fatal event followed a recent episode of strikingly similar self-harm.

This distinction is visible even in sources sympathetic to UFO research. A modern UFO-focused account describes McDonald as having suffered an earlier suicide attempt before his June death, while another UFO site explicitly says that no foul play was alleged and treats the case instead as an example of the potentially serious human consequences of professional stigma.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufouap.com/articles/uap-missing-dead-suspicious-pattern/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.com">[UFOUAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap missing dead suspicious pattern</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Missing and the Dead: UFO Figures Who Vanished or Died Under Suspicious Circumstances — UFOUAPMarch 3, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2026</span></span></span> In other words, accepting that McDonald faced [hostility]({{ 'hostility/' | relative_url }}) over UFOs does not require accepting that the hostility caused his death through murder.

The point becomes clearer when the evidence is separated into three categories.

* **Evidence of conflict:** McDonald challenged influential scientific and governmental positions on UFOs and became an unusually visible advocate for further investigation. His archival collection documents extensive correspondence, case investigations, [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) material and disputes over official treatment of the subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/JEMcDonald/mcdonald_arizona_archives_ms412.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirkmcd.princeton.edu">[Kirk McDonald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirkmcd.princeton.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kirk Mc Donald Arizona Archives Online</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kirk Mc Donald Arizona Archives Online</span></span></span>
* **Evidence relevant to suicide:** there was a serious earlier suicide attempt, a short interval before his death, a firearm at the later scene, another reported note and an official suicide determination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span>
* **Evidence required for homicide:** this would need to connect another person or organisation to the fatal act — for example through forensic inconsistency, credible threats tied to the death, witness evidence, documentary orders, evidence of staging or other material that makes the suicide reconstruction untenable.

The first category establishes possible enemies or sources of pressure. It does not by itself establish the third.

That is a common causal error in suspicious-death narratives. A motive attributed to a government agency can sound compelling because the person was genuinely controversial, but **motive is not mechanism**. McDonald’s prominence could conceivably supply a hypothetical motive for silencing him; his earlier suicide attempt supplies direct evidence for a competing mechanism of death. A homicide theory therefore has to do more than show that somebody might have preferred McDonald to stop campaigning.

## The strongest homicide claims remain inferential

Claims that McDonald was murdered do exist, but the accessible versions located in the historical and contemporary UFO literature tend to proceed from suspicion rather than new forensic evidence. One UFO conspiracy account, for example, describes his death as mysterious and suggests that his opponents benefited from it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.info-quest.org/documents/ufobodycnt.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info-quest.org">[Info Quest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info-quest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on info-quest.org.</span></span></span> Another modern site explicitly labels his death suspicious, arguing that the combination of professional conflict, personal collapse and death resembles a pattern of institutional “neutralisation”. Yet the same page acknowledges both the April suicide attempt and the official suicide ruling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/James_McDonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts James E. That juxtaposition reveals the central weakness. A pattern-based argument can reinterpret almost every part of the suicide evidence as part of an assassination programme: professional criticism becomes deliberate destruction, personal distress becomes engineered distress, and suicide itself becomes the final stage of neutralisation. But unless independent evidence demonstrates that those events were deliberately produced, the argument risks becoming unfalsifiable. Evidence supporting suicide is absorbed into the conspiracy theory rather than tested against it.

Some retellings go further by emphasising apparently puzzling practical details — for example, how a badly injured or visually impaired McDonald could obtain another firearm or reach the location where he died. Such questions are legitimate areas for historical verification, but repetition can transform uncertainties into evidence of murder without establishing the intermediate facts. One widely circulated conspiratorial account even describes him as driving himself to a pawnshop and then into the desert despite his disabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://9-11themotherofallblackoperations.blogspot.com/2012/07/whos-murdering-americas-ufo-researchers.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 9-11themotherofallblackoperations.blogspot.com">[9-11themotherofallblackoperations.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">9-11themotherofallblackoperations.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span> By contrast, another suspicious-death account says that he travelled by taxi and was dropped off outside Tucson.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/James_McDonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts James E. There is also evidence of federal interest in the McDonald family, but its meaning needs similar restraint. Records obtained from the US National Archives concern an FBI domestic-security investigation involving James and Betsy McDonald; a recent archival project says the material reflects, among other things, attention to Betsy McDonald’s political activism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.expandingfrontiersresearch.org/post/records-requests-update" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: expandingfrontiersresearch.org">[EFR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">expandingfrontiersresearch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EFRRecords Requests Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EFRRecords Requests Update</span></span></span> Such records are relevant to the broader history of surveillance and government interest. They are not, without additional evidence, proof that the FBI or another agency caused McDonald’s death.

Indeed, the existence of surveillance records can make a conspiracy narrative feel more concrete while leaving the decisive evidential gap untouched. “The government kept records on McDonald” and “government agents murdered McDonald” are radically different propositions. The former does not establish the latter.

## What a homicide theory would still need

A serious homicide hypothesis has to explain the evidence better than the documented suicide interpretation rather than simply showing that murder was physically possible. McDonald’s first attempt raises that threshold because it supplies a recent behavioural precedent independent of the later death scene.

The most consequential new evidence would therefore be evidence that breaks the apparent continuity between the two incidents. A contemporaneous police or coroner record showing that the fatal wound was inconsistent with self-infliction would matter greatly. So would authenticated evidence that the weapon could not have belonged to or been obtained by McDonald; reliable witness testimony placing another person at the scene under incriminating circumstances; a demonstrably forged suicide note; documented threats explicitly tied to his death; or records showing an organised operation directed against him.

The earlier attempt itself could also become relevant to a homicide theory if credible evidence showed that it had been misclassified — for example, forensic evidence indicating that the first shooting was inflicted by somebody else. But that would require evidence, not the retrospective assertion that two shootings are inherently suspicious.

What the available material instead shows is a first attempt already recognised as suicidal before the fatal event, followed within months by another shooting accompanied by circumstances contemporaneously interpreted as suicide. The Tucson newspaper archive is particularly damaging to claims that the earlier attempt is merely a later rationalisation invented to explain the death: it records the [prior attempt]({{ 'prior-attempt/' | relative_url }}) as part of the local historical account and specifically reports notes in both episodes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span>

This does not make homicide metaphysically impossible. Historical conclusions rarely work that way. It changes the comparative strength of the explanations. A murder theory must now posit not merely that McDonald had enemies, but that someone killed a man who had recently tried to kill himself, did so in a manner resembling his previous attempt, left a scene interpreted as suicide and successfully avoided leaving persuasive evidence of intervention.

## The narrower conclusion the record supports


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

McDonald’s case contains two stories that should not be collapsed into one. The first is well documented: a respected atmospheric physicist became an outspoken UFO researcher, challenged powerful scientific and governmental positions and encountered serious controversy. His surviving papers demonstrate that his UFO work was extensive and substantive rather than a minor hobby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/JEMcDonald/mcdonald_arizona_archives_ms412.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirkmcd.princeton.edu">[Kirk McDonald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirkmcd.princeton.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kirk Mc Donald Arizona Archives Online</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kirk Mc Donald Arizona Archives Online</span></span></span> The second proposition — that those conflicts culminated in his assassination — requires a separate chain of evidence.

</div>

The documented earlier suicide attempt is therefore not a peripheral biographical detail. It is one of the most important facts for evaluating the later death. Contemporary and archival reporting identifies the June 1971 death as suicide, records a revolver at the scene and reports that McDonald had attempted suicide by gunshot only months before, leaving a note on each occasion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are realMarch 23, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 23, 2012</span></span></span> Later [murder claims]({{ 'murder-claims/' | relative_url }}) establish that suspicion exists, but the sources located for those claims do not supply comparably strong forensic or documentary evidence overturning that sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.info-quest.org/documents/ufobodycnt.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info-quest.org">[Info Quest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info-quest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on info-quest.org.</span></span></span>

For the wider question of allegedly suspicious deaths among UFO and unconventional-physics researchers, McDonald is consequently a useful caution rather than a clean assassination case. His institutional conflicts are real; government interest in him and his family can be documented; and the pressures surrounding his final years deserve historical attention. But those facts cannot safely substitute for evidence about how he died. Any suppression theory that leaves out the earlier 1971 suicide attempt removes precisely the fact that its causal explanation most needs to confront.
