## Introduction

For claims that UFO, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) or unconventional-propulsion researchers were deliberately eliminated, the most important cross-case question is not whether several people worked on unusual science. It is whether the **same identifiable actor appears in more than one case in a role plausibly connected to the harmful outcome**. A recurring individual, operational unit, contractor, intermediary or tightly defined organisation would provide a linkage that shared research interests cannot.

## What would count as a meaningful recurring actor?

The useful analogy is ordinary serial-crime investigation. The FBI's Violent Criminal Apprehension Program, ViCAP, is designed to compare information from homicides, missing-person cases and other violent crimes so that apparently separate incidents can be linked. Its records can include victim and offender information, crime-scene descriptions, laboratory findings, criminal histories, court records and statements. The National Institute of Justice similarly describes forensic intelligence as integrating information across cases specifically to identify connections that would otherwise remain hidden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/department-of-justice-fbi-privacy-impact-assessments/vicap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ViCAP — FBIVICAP contains crime scene descriptions, victim and offender descriptive data, including name and other personal identifyin...</span></span></span>

Applied cautiously to allegations involving researchers, that means a recurring actor becomes important when the recurrence is **specific, independently documented and operationally relevant**. Finding that two scientists once dealt with the Department of Defense would be weak because the department encompasses an enormous number of organisations and activities. Finding that both dealt, shortly before their deaths, with the same named programme officer, security contractor, investigator or intermediary would be substantially more interesting. Showing that the same person threatened, surveilled or met both researchers would be stronger again.

The hierarchy therefore runs roughly from broad association to operational linkage:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Weak linkage:** both researchers worked in aerospace, had government funding, lived near defence facilities or dealt with large institutions such as NASA or the Department of Defense.
* **Potentially meaningful linkage:** both dealt with the same small laboratory, contractor, programme office, company or named intermediary during the relevant period.
* **Strong linkage:** the same identifiable person or team appears in independent records involving surveillance, threats, unusual approaches or other activity immediately relevant to both cases.
* **Very strong linkage:** communications, financial records, travel data, forensic evidence or credible testimony show that the recurring actor participated in both harmful events or acted under the same instructions.

</div>

This is why names matter more than categories. “Intelligence”, “the military”, “NASA” or “defence contractors” are too broad to function as perpetrators without further identification. A particular office, company, case officer, subcontractor or intermediary can be tested against records.

## Broad institutional overlap can be misleading

The Huntsville cases illustrate the problem particularly well. Ning Li unquestionably occupied a research environment containing NASA and military connections. NASA's Technical Reports Server preserves a 1999 paper on testing a proposed gravitational-force coupling in superconductors that lists Li alongside researchers associated with NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville. A later investigation by the *Huntsville Business Journal*, drawing partly on an interview with Li's son, also documents her subsequent AC Gravity work and association with the Army Aviation and Missile Command.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19990104365" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[NASA Technical Reports Server]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Robertson, Tony (NASA Marshall Space Flight Center Huntsville, AL</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerTest Status for Proposed Coupling of a Gravitational Force...by D Noever · 1999 — Li, Ning (NASA Marshall S...</span></span></span>

Those connections are worth mapping. They are not, however, evidence that those organisations harmed Li. Indeed, the later account considerably weakens the once-popular premise that Li herself mysteriously “disappeared”. Her son said she continued living in Huntsville and described a 2014 road accident that left her severely impaired; her publicly reported death came in 2021. The disappearance was principally from the public scientific record, not a demonstrated physical disappearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">solving the mystery of huntsvilles brilliant scientist disappearing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army Aviation and Missile Command. Amy Eskridge presents a superficially tempting comparison because she too lived in Huntsville and was associated with unconventional gravity research. Her obituary confirms her death on 11 June 2022, while later reporting and online discussion have attached allegations of threats and harassment to her case. But publicly accessible primary documentation of those allegations remains thin. More importantly for this particular test, the evidence located does not establish that a named person, unit or contractor associated with Li subsequently played an operational role in Eskridge's death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obits.al.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: al.com">[Alabama Obituaries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">al.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">amy eskridge obituary</span></span></span>

“Huntsville” itself cannot do the evidential work. The city contains Marshall Space Flight Center and the Redstone Arsenal research ecosystem, so overlap among aerospace researchers there is unsurprising. Geography becomes probative only when it leads to something narrower: the same programme, office, intermediary, security contact or other identifiable actor appearing independently in both researchers' records.

## Mallove shows why known perpetrators change the comparison

Eugene Mallove's murder is especially useful as a control case because investigators ultimately identified actual people alleged to have participated in his killing. Chad Schaffer pleaded guilty to first-degree manslaughter and received a 16-year sentence. Mozelle Brown was convicted of murder and conspiracy in 2014 and sentenced to 58 years in 2015; contemporary accounts described the prosecution's case as arising from a confrontation while Mallove was clearing a Norwich property after Schaffer's family had been evicted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nbcconnecticut.com/news/local/man-gets-16-years-in-prison-for-killing-scientist/1906691/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nbcconnecticut.com">[nbcconnecticut.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nbcconnecticut.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">man gets 16 years in prison for killing scientist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">man gets 16 years in prison for killing scientist</span></span></span>

That does not make every aspect of the long-running case beyond dispute. It does demonstrate what an identifiable-actor hypothesis looks like when evidence becomes concrete. There are names, relationships between defendants, a location, an immediate connection to the victim and a prosecution theory describing how the participants came into contact with him. Contemporary reporting said Schaffer and Brown were cousins and placed them together at the property.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://davidkushner.com/article/the-coldest-case/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: davidkushner.com">[David Kushner]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">davidkushner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">David Kushner The Coldest Case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">David Kushner The Coldest Case</span></span></span>

For a coordinated researcher-killing theory, the decisive next discovery would therefore not be another scientist who died unexpectedly. It would be evidence that **someone in Mallove's established perpetrator network also appeared around another researcher** — or, alternatively, that another independently identified network recurred across two different cases.

No credible evidence located in the present record makes that connection. The people identified in the Mallove investigation do not emerge as recurring actors in the documented Li or McDonald histories, and the available Eskridge material does not establish such a connection either. That absence is much more informative than the fact that Mallove advocated controversial energy research.

## A repeated organisation needs to be narrower than “the government”

Organisations require the same discipline as individuals. A recurring company can be significant because companies leave administrative traces: officers, addresses, contracts, patent assignments, litigation, payments and named representatives. Government units likewise generate procurement records, correspondence, programme documentation and organisational identifiers.

Several public systems make these claims unusually testable. SAM.gov now provides searches of federal contract-award information and states that contract data contain detailed information on reportable federal contract actions. USAspending permits searches by recipient, award identifier, agency, dates and other fields. Together, such records can help determine whether supposedly independent researchers actually received money through the same small contractor or programme office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sam.gov/contract-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sam.gov">[SAM.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sam.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Contract Data &#124; SAM.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Contract Data &#124; SAM.gov</span></span></span>

Patent records offer another route. The US Patent and Trademark Office maintains recorded assignment information, while Patent Public Search can be queried by inventor, applicant, assignee, examiner, attorney or correspondence information. Assignment records can reveal a shared assignee or representative that is invisible if researchers are considered only by subject matter. The USPTO cautions, however, that recording an assignment is a ministerial function and does not itself validate the underlying transaction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s301.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uspto.gov">[uspto.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uspto.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">301-Ownership/Assignability of Patents and Applications</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">301-Ownership/Assignability of Patents and Applications</span></span></span>

Corporate records have similar value where applicable. The Securities and Exchange Commission's EDGAR full-text system allows searches by company or individual name across more than two decades of electronic filings, although its usefulness is naturally limited to entities and people subject to SEC filing requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sec.gov.</span></span></span>

These sources change the question from “Did both scientists have defence connections?” to much sharper tests: Did they share a contractor? A corporate officer? A patent assignee? A lawyer? A programme manager? An address? A funding instrument? If so, did that connection exist at the time relevant to the alleged targeting?

## What records could actually establish recurrence?

A serious linkage investigation would work outwards from identified entities rather than backwards from a theory. For every researcher, names should be extracted from contemporaneous records and placed on a dated timeline. The aim is then to discover independently recurring entities whose presence cannot easily be explained by the normal structure of the research community.

The highest-value records would include **police and court material**, because these can identify actual suspects, witnesses, associates and [competing motives]({{ 'competing-motives/' | relative_url }}). Connecticut, for example, directs members of the public towards State Police and Judicial Branch systems for criminal records, while its Judicial Branch provides criminal case look-up facilities. Federal litigation can be searched through PACER's nationwide Case Locator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.ct.gov/dcj/programs-and-services/criminal-records" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.ct.gov">[ct.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">criminal records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">criminal records</span></span></span>

**Government contracting and programme records** could test whether researchers who appear unrelated were actually connected through the same narrow funding chain. Contract award searches can expose awarding organisations, recipients and modifications; historical programme reports and technical archives can add named participants. Li's presence in NASA's technical archive demonstrates the value of this approach: it anchors particular collaborators and institutions to a particular piece of research rather than relying on later recollection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sam.gov/contract-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sam.gov">[SAM.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sam.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Contract Data &#124; SAM.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Contract Data &#124; SAM.gov</span></span></span>

**Patent, corporate and litigation records** could reveal intermediaries that receive little attention in biographical accounts. A recurring patent attorney, assignee, company officer, consultant or small contractor across separate researchers would justify further examination, especially if the relationship clustered immediately before a death or abrupt withdrawal from research. USPTO assignment records are particularly useful because recorded documents can preserve assignor and assignee identities and associated correspondence information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uspto.gov/patents/laws/power-attorney-and-assignment/assignments-web-aotw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uspto.gov">[USPTO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uspto.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">assignments web aotw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">assignments web aotw</span></span></span>

**Archived correspondence and personal papers** can be equally important. The University of Arizona's James E. McDonald collection contains correspondence, reports, interviews and material relating to his UFO investigations from 1958 to 1971. Collections of this kind offer a way to test claims about repeated contacts: a name allegedly connected to McDonald can be checked against dated correspondence rather than inferred from later conspiracy literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.azarchivesonline.org/xtf/view?docId=ead%2Fuoa%2FUAMS412.xml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: azarchivesonline.org">[AZ Archives Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">azarchivesonline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AZ Archives Online James E</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">McDonald papers, (1904-1997)Mostly correspondence, cases, reports, interviews, and printed materials relating to James E. McDonald&#x27;s inve...</span></span></span>

Finally, **Freedom of Information Act requests** can test claims about federal involvement, although exemptions and incomplete record retention mean that a negative response cannot automatically be treated as evidence of concealment. The FBI's own FOIA guidance requires sufficiently detailed identification and, for records concerning deceased individuals, proof of death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/sample-fbi-foia-request-letter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sample FOIA Request Letter — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sample FOIA Request Letter — FBI</span></span></span>

## The recurrence must also be independent

Even finding the same name twice is not enough. The two appearances must originate independently.

Suppose a later article says that Researcher A dealt with “Agent X”, then a podcast repeats the article and a website adds Researcher B to the story by citing the podcast. Searching the web might produce dozens of references to Agent X, but there is still only one evidential chain. Repetition of a claim is not recurrence of an actor.

A much stronger result would be, for example, an archived letter from Researcher A naming an intermediary and an unrelated police interview in Researcher B's case naming the same person. Stronger still would be telephone, financial or travel records independently placing that person around both researchers. The National Institute of Justice's forensic-intelligence framework is relevant precisely because [cross-case linkage]({{ 'cross-case-links/' | relative_url }}) depends on integrating information from separate evidence streams, rather than simply noticing superficial similarities between incidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/using-forensic-intelligence-combat-serial-and-organized-violent-crimes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nij.ojp.gov">[ojp.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">using forensic intelligence combat serial and organized violent crimes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">using forensic intelligence combat serial and organized violent crimes</span></span></span>

There is also a base-rate problem. Some entities naturally recur because they are hubs. NASA Marshall, the Army's Huntsville organisations, a major university or a prominent aerospace contractor may interact with hundreds or thousands of scientists. Their recurrence therefore carries much less weight than the recurrence of a little-known consultant or small programme office.

The rarer and more operationally specific the connection, the harder coincidence becomes as an explanation.

## What discovery would genuinely change the case?

The strongest realistic breakthrough would be a **cross-case entity match followed by independent [corroboration]({{ 'corroboration/' | relative_url }})**. Imagine that records from two researchers, separated by geography and with no known personal relationship, independently identified the same obscure intermediary shortly before each death. If subsequent records showed that intermediary communicating with the same small organisation, travelling to both locations or receiving payments linked to both operations, the evidential landscape would change dramatically.

That would still not automatically prove murder or a programme targeting unconventional researchers. Investigators would have to establish what the actor was doing, whether the contacts had innocent explanations and whether the underlying deaths were actually crimes. But there would finally be something concrete linking cases that could be falsified, corroborated and investigated.

The reverse finding matters too. If named perpetrators in one homicide lead only to a local personal dispute; another researcher's records lead to ordinary university and government collaborators; another case contains no demonstrable perpetrator at all; and the names never intersect, accumulating the cases does not strengthen a [coordination]({{ 'coordination/' | relative_url }}) theory merely because all of the researchers can be placed under a broad “unconventional science” label.

That is where the presently accessible evidence leaves the recurring-actor test. Genuine institutional connections exist, particularly around Li's Huntsville research, and Mallove's death unquestionably became a criminal case with identifiable participants. But no well-supported recurring perpetrator, intermediary or narrowly defined operational organisation has yet been demonstrated across these otherwise separate cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19990104365" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrs.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrs.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Robertson, Tony (NASA Marshall Space Flight Center Huntsville, AL</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Technical Reports ServerTest Status for Proposed Coupling of a Gravitational Force...by D Noever · 1999 — Li, Ning (NASA Marshall S...</span></span></span>

For the wider question of alleged coordinated killings of [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) researchers, that is a crucial distinction. **A shared research subject supplies a theme; a recurring operational actor could supply a link.** Until the latter is established through independent records, the recurring-actor test remains not evidence for a coordinated campaign, but one of the clearest ways such a campaign could actually be demonstrated.
