## Introduction

Strong scientific credentials can establish that a UFO or unconventional-gravity researcher was a serious scientist. They cannot, by themselves, establish that the same person was later silenced, murdered, disappeared or otherwise suppressed because of that work. Those are different propositions and require different evidence.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_credentials_vs_claim_032e02-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Credentials" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

James E. McDonald and Ning Li show why the distinction matters. McDonald was unquestionably a respected atmospheric physicist who devoted substantial effort to UFO investigation. Li genuinely published unconventional gravity-related work in mainstream physics journals and later received US defence funding. Yet neither fact automatically validates later claims about what happened to them. In McDonald's case, the documented circumstances of his death weigh heavily towards suicide. In Li's case, later family reporting resolved much of the supposed mystery surrounding her "disappearance".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/mccarthy/obit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Mc Carthy Dissertation: James E. Mc Donald Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesMcCarthy Dissertation: James E. McDonald Obituary...</span></span></span>

The key question is therefore not whether these researchers were credible people. It is whether the specific suppression claim has independent evidence of its own.

## Why credentials matter to the research story

Scientific standing matters because it helps answer an important preliminary question: was the researcher actually engaged in substantial work, or has a later legend inflated a marginal association into something more impressive?

McDonald clearly passes that test. His 1971 obituary in the *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society* records a BA, an MIT master's degree, a PhD from Iowa State University, service as a naval aerologist and a long academic career at the University of Arizona's Institute of Atmospheric Physics. He was an American Meteorological Society Fellow and was remembered for substantial work in atmospheric science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/mccarthy/obit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Mc Carthy Dissertation: James E. McDonald Obituary...</span></span></span> His UFO interest was also not a casual hobby attached to his name after his death. Surviving collections contain correspondence, witness interviews and investigative material, while contemporary statements show him arguing publicly that UFO reports deserved more rigorous scientific scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufor.asn.au/archives/articles/james-e-mcdonald-ufo-researcher-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufor.asn.au">[UFOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufor.asn.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFORDr James E Mc Donald’s Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dr James E McDonald’s Archives - UFO Research (NSW) Incorporated, Sydney, AustraliaApril 15, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 15, 2016</span></span></span>

That matters because it rules out one weak form of debunking: dismissing the entire historical episode on the ground that McDonald was merely an unqualified enthusiast. He was not.

Ning Li presents a similar, though technically different, case. In 1992, Li and Douglas Torr published a paper in *Physical Review B* examining gravitational effects in relation to superconductors. The journal identifies both authors with the Center for Space Plasma and Aeronomic Research at the University of Alabama in Huntsville.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. Their measurements did **not** reproduce the large anomalous effect reported in some earlier work: changes in acceleration were below two parts in 100 million of ordinary gravity in the static configuration they tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921453497014627" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Static test for a gravitational force coupled to type II YBCO superconductors - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 1997</span></span></span>

This is important for two reasons. First, Li's [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) association was not invented retrospectively; she really worked on gravity-superconductor questions. Secondly, describing her simply as a scientist who had "proved antigravity" would be misleading. Her publications included theoretical proposals and null experimental constraints as well as speculative possibilities.

Her work was also subject to ordinary scientific criticism. In 1994, physicist Mark Kowitt published a *Physical Review B* paper specifically challenging Li and Torr's treatment of gravitomagnetic effects, arguing that their model substantially overestimated the effect because of their interpretation of magnetic permeability in superconductors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10009347" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Gravitomagnetism and magnetic permeability in superconductors</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravitomagnetism and magnetic permeability in superconductors - PubMedJanuary 1, 1994...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1994</span></span></span> That exchange is a useful reminder that peer-reviewed publication establishes that a proposal entered legitimate scientific discussion; it does not mean that its conclusions became accepted physics.

For both McDonald and Li, therefore, credentials strengthen a narrow historical claim: **these were genuine researchers doing work worth documenting accurately**. They do not settle the much harder question of what caused a later death, withdrawal or loss of public contact.

## Why credibility does not transfer to suppression claims

A common reasoning error in suspicious-death narratives is a kind of evidential transfer. The argument often runs implicitly like this: the scientist was highly qualified; the scientist worked on a controversial subject; something unusual later happened to the scientist; therefore claims that the scientist was suppressed deserve the same credibility as the scientist's professional work.

The conclusion does not follow.

Scientific qualifications are evidence about expertise within a field. A PhD, professorship, publication record or defence contract may make someone a good source on atmospheric physics, superconductivity or experimental design. Those credentials do not make later third-party allegations about homicide, intelligence operations or covert coercion more reliable. Such claims require evidence suited to the claim: police records, medical evidence, contemporaneous correspondence, witnesses, documentary links to threatening actors, unexplained financial or operational activity, or some other independent causal connection.

The distinction becomes clearer when the evidence is separated into layers:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Research credibility:** Did the person have genuine qualifications, publications or institutional roles?
* **Research-claim credibility:** Did the specific scientific hypothesis survive criticism, replication and further testing?
* **Suppression credibility:** Is there evidence that an institution deliberately restricted publication, funding, employment or access?
* **Death or disappearance credibility:** Is there evidence that suppression escalated into abduction, homicide or another covert act?

</div>

Evidence in one layer can motivate questions about another, but it cannot substitute for it.

Li's career illustrates this particularly well. The US Army awarded her company, AC Gravity, a reported $448,970 agreement in 2001 for an experimental programme concerning gravity and superconductivity. The documented technical objective was to test predictions stemming from her theoretical model.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=print&amp;name=News&amp;sid=838" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zpenergy.com.</span></span></span> The fact that no public technical results subsequently became widely available is genuinely interesting. It permits several possibilities, including unsuccessful work, proprietary restriction, defence classification or mundane archival loss. It does not, without further documentation, identify which explanation is correct.

The same rule applies even where government secrecy is known to exist. [Classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}) is not equivalent to an assassination programme. A security clearance or unpublished defence project may establish restricted dissemination; it does not establish a motive to kill the researcher, much less evidence that anybody acted on such a motive.

This evidentiary separation is especially important in UFO history because real secrecy and real stigma have sometimes coexisted with much more speculative claims. The existence of one makes the other psychologically plausible, but plausibility is not proof.

## McDonald: strong scientist, weak homicide case

James McDonald is perhaps the clearest example of why professional stature should not be allowed to overwhelm the evidence surrounding a death.

His scientific standing was substantial, and so was his commitment to UFO investigation. He challenged what he saw as inadequate [official inquiry]({{ 'official-inquiry/' | relative_url }}) and argued that some UFO cases remained scientifically unexplained. His surviving research archive demonstrates that this was sustained work rather than a fleeting public position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/mccarthy/obit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Mc Carthy Dissertation: James E. Mc Donald Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesMcCarthy Dissertation: James E. McDonald Obituary...</span></span></span>

Those facts can make later claims of suppression feel intuitively compelling. A respected scientist attacks an official consensus, becomes associated with a stigmatised subject and dies at 51: as a narrative sequence, it invites suspicion.

But the evidence about his death must be assessed independently of that narrative appeal. Contemporary reporting described his June 1971 death as an apparent suicide, and later biographical accounts record an earlier suicide attempt only weeks before his death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://handwiki.org/wiki/Biography%3AJames_E._McDonald" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: handwiki.org">[HandWiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">handwiki.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hand Wiki Biography:James E. Mc Donald</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hand Wiki Biography:James E. Mc Donald</span></span></span> The [prior attempt]({{ 'prior-attempt/' | relative_url }}) is highly significant because it provides direct, case-specific evidence of suicidal intent before the fatal event.

A suppression theory therefore needs to explain more than the fact that McDonald was controversial and died young. It must account for the prior attempt and produce affirmative evidence that the fatal shooting involved another actor. His qualifications in atmospheric physics cannot perform that evidentiary work.

There is still a valid historical issue nearby. McDonald faced scientific [hostility]({{ 'hostility/' | relative_url }}), ridicule and institutional resistance over UFOs. Those experiences help explain why his supporters may perceive his career as an example of marginalisation. But professional marginalisation and physical elimination are different claims. Evidence that a researcher was mocked, denied support or opposed by institutions may establish a climate of stigma without establishing homicide.

This is precisely where careful analysis becomes more informative than either credulity or blanket dismissal. One can simultaneously conclude that McDonald was a serious scientist, that he had legitimate criticisms of contemporary UFO investigation, and that the known evidence does not make a convincing case that he was killed for holding those views.

## Li: a real research trail behind an exaggerated disappearance

Ning Li offers the reverse pattern. Here, the strongest part of the mystery was not her death but the long gap in public information about her.

Her research credentials are easy to document. She co-authored peer-reviewed papers on gravitational and superconducting effects, worked in Huntsville's research environment and later moved into private work through AC Gravity. Her company received US defence funding for an experimental gravity-superconductor programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[aps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. B...</span></span></span>

After the early 2000s, however, Li largely disappeared from publicly visible scientific publishing. That gap became fertile ground for increasingly dramatic explanations. Online accounts proposed that she had been absorbed into a secret US programme, returned to China, disappeared involuntarily or even been killed.

The crucial lesson is that the absence of public information was real, while many conclusions drawn from it were not established.

A 2021 funeral-home obituary documented that Li had remained associated with Huntsville and died on 27 July 2021.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li/print" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span></span></span> In 2023, the *Huntsville Business Journal* interviewed her son, George Men, who described a much less sensational personal history. According to his account, Li remained in Huntsville, continued defence-related work for a period, and in 2014 was struck by a vehicle while crossing a road on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. He said the collision caused severe brain injury and that she later required long-term care before her death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

The family account does contain elements that can sustain narrower questions about secrecy. Men told the newspaper that his mother's later defence work involved a high-level security clearance and that she ceased discussing details publicly. Because those claims currently depend heavily on his testimony rather than a publicly released project file, they should be attributed rather than treated as independently demonstrated facts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

Even so, the reporting radically changes the evidential status of the old "[missing scientist]({{ 'missing-scientist/' | relative_url }})" story. A researcher becoming inaccessible to journalists is not the same as a person physically disappearing. Once direct family testimony and ordinary life events are recovered, the dramatic interpretation loses much of the vacuum that allowed it to grow.

Li's case therefore shows how genuine credentials can actually make a disappearance legend more persuasive than the underlying evidence warrants. Because the research was real, the defence funding was real and the subject sounded extraordinary, the later information gap looked meaningful. But a chain of individually true facts can still support a false overall inference if the causal links between them are assumed rather than demonstrated.

## The strongest warning sign is not an impressive CV

For readers evaluating alleged suppression cases, the most probative evidence is rarely the person's professional prestige. What matters more is whether evidence directly connects the contested research to the later event.

A genuinely stronger suppression case would contain some combination of contemporaneous threats tied specifically to the work; documented pressure from identifiable organisations; attempts to seize or destroy research materials; witnesses independently describing the same coercive behaviour; forensic evidence inconsistent with the officially recorded cause of death; demonstrable falsehoods in official records; or a documentary chain showing that people concerned about the research took action against the scientist.

By contrast, several commonly cited facts are weak on their own: a researcher held a clearance; defence agencies funded the work; papers stopped appearing; colleagues [lost contact]({{ 'lost-contact/' | relative_url }}); the topic was unconventional; government files existed; or the person died unexpectedly. Each may justify investigation. None independently demonstrates lethal suppression.

This distinction also protects the historical reputation of the scientists themselves. Treating every unexplained interval or premature death as confirmation of a conspiracy can obscure what these people actually contributed. McDonald's documented importance lies partly in his insistence that anomalous reports should be investigated rather than dismissed by reflex. Li's importance lies in having subjected unconventional gravity-superconductor ideas to theoretical analysis and experimental testing, including results that placed restrictive limits on proposed effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info-quest.org/documents/mcdonald.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info-quest.org">[Info Quest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info-quest.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Info Quest Statement by Dr. James E Mc Donald on UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Info Quest Statement by Dr. James E Mc Donald on UFOs</span></span></span> Their work deserves to be evaluated on that evidence, rather than used primarily as credentials for claims they themselves did not establish.

## A more defensible way to read these cases

The best-supported position is neither that unconventional researchers were never suppressed nor that unusual careers and deaths prove a coordinated programme. The available evidence supports a more discriminating approach.

McDonald's credentials make his scientific engagement with UFOs historically significant. They do not override evidence pointing to suicide. Li's publication record and defence funding make her gravity research real and her later withdrawal from public science worthy of explanation. They do not turn years of reduced public visibility into proof of disappearance or murder. Later reporting substantially weakened that interpretation by establishing that she remained in Huntsville and eventually died after years of serious illness following a road accident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/mccarthy/obit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Mc Carthy Dissertation: James E. Mc Donald Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesMcCarthy Dissertation: James E. McDonald Obituary...</span></span></span>

The broader lesson for claims about suspicious deaths in [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) research is therefore simple but demanding: **evaluate the scientist and the suppression allegation on separate evidentiary tracks**. A distinguished career can make a case worth examining more carefully. It cannot make an unsupported causal claim true.

That distinction is not a dismissal of anomalous research. It is what allows genuine scientific achievement, genuine institutional secrecy and genuine evidence of wrongdoing—where such evidence exists—to be distinguished from stories that derive their persuasive force mainly from the reputation of the person at their centre.

    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
