## Introduction

The US Army really did fund Ning Li’s company, AC Gravity, to investigate her unconventional superconducting-gravity theory. The surviving Department of Defense award record identifies agreement DAAH01-01-9-R001, worth **$448,970**, running from 25 April 2001 to 25 September 2002 under the US Army Aviation and Missile Command (AMCOM). Crucially, however, the record describes the project as the **first attempt to experimentally confirm** predictions of Li’s theory. It does not say that those predictions had already been confirmed, nor does it record a successful gravity-control device.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

That distinction is the key to understanding why the award matters to the Ning Li “disappearance” story. The funding establishes a genuine military connection and genuine government interest in testing the idea. It does **not** establish the much larger claim later built around it: that Li had demonstrated [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}), that the Army acquired a working technology, or that secrecy surrounding a breakthrough explains her subsequent absence from public science.

## What Did the Army Actually Fund Ning Li to Test?

The strongest surviving evidence is unusually specific. A reproduction of the Department of Defense’s FY2001 annual report on cooperative agreements and “other transactions” lists the Army award as follows: AC Gravity was the awardee; the awarding office was AMCOM; the agreement was an **“Other Transaction for Prototype”**; its title was **“Gravito - Electro Magnetic Superconductivity Experiment”**; and the federal contribution was $448,970, with no non-government contribution recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

More important than the title is the stated technical objective. The award description summarised Li’s hypothesis that a superconducting disc subjected to a particular external magnetic field could generate a significant gravitational field. It then characterised the funded work as an attempt to test theoretical predictions experimentally. That wording places the project at the **experimental-validation stage**, not at the stage of deploying a technology already known to work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

The choice of an Other Transaction for a prototype is also significant, but easily misunderstood. Under the rules applicable around this period, Department of Defense prototype Other Transactions were instruments other than ordinary procurement contracts, grants or cooperative agreements, used for prototype projects relevant to weapons or weapon systems proposed for development or acquisition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/3.3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute32 CFR § 3.3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Applicability. &#124; Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (e-CFR) &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information InstituteJune 5, 2000...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2000</span></span></span> Modern Government Accountability Office explanations make the underlying point particularly clear: prototype Other Transactions can be used to determine whether technologies can be adapted for defence purposes and can encompass proofs of concept and demonstrations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Acquisitions: DOD&#x27;s Use of Other Transactions for Prototype Projects Has Increased &#124; U.S. GAONovember 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 22, 2019</span></span></span>

In other words, “prototype” does not mean “proven machine”. It can mean building enough of an experimental system to discover whether an idea works at all.

That interpretation also fits Li’s previous experimental record. In 1997, Li and collaborators published a test involving type-II yttrium-barium-copper-oxide superconductors. The paper discussed earlier reports of anomalous weight changes around superconductors, but Li’s group found changes in gravitational acceleration of less than two parts in 108 in its static experimental configuration. The authors presented this as a limit on the proposed coupling rather than as evidence of a large gravity-shielding effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921453497014627" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Static test for a gravitational force coupled to type II YBCO superconductors - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 1997</span></span></span>

The Army award therefore represented a meaningful escalation from speculative theory towards a purpose-built experimental test. It was not government certification that Li had solved gravity control.

## Why Funding Is Not Evidence of Success

The AC Gravity award supports three relatively narrow conclusions: **the Army considered Li’s idea worth testing; it was willing to spend nearly $449,000 on that test; and it regarded the work as sufficiently defence-relevant for a prototype Other Transaction.** Those are substantial facts. None tells us the experimental result.

This matters because research funding and experimental confirmation answer different questions. An award decision asks, in effect, whether a proposed experiment is worth attempting. Successful validation asks whether the measurements subsequently support the hypothesis. A government can rationally fund an uncertain experiment precisely because it does **not** yet know the answer.

Prototype funding is especially poor evidence for treating the underlying technology as established. Government descriptions of prototype Other Transactions encompass proofs of concept, models, demonstrations of technical utility and evaluations of feasibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Acquisitions: DOD&#x27;s Use of Other Transactions for Prototype Projects Has Increased &#124; U.S. GAONovember 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 22, 2019</span></span></span> The relevant regulatory framework likewise describes these agreements as applying to prototype projects connected to possible defence systems, not only to technologies whose effectiveness has already been demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/3.3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute32 CFR § 3.3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Applicability. &#124; Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (e-CFR) &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information InstituteJune 5, 2000...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2000</span></span></span>

The wording of the AC Gravity record makes the distinction even harder to avoid. Calling the project the first experimental attempt to confirm some of Li’s theoretical predictions would make little sense if the Army already possessed conclusive experimental confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

Nor has a public technical report surfaced showing that DAAH01-01-9-R001 produced the dramatic result implied by later “antigravity breakthrough” accounts. The *Huntsville Business Journal*, reporting on Li’s later life in 2023, found that the results of the Army-funded project were never published. It also reported no public record of subsequent products from AC Gravity despite the company remaining registered for years afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

That absence creates uncertainty, but uncertainty works in both directions. An unpublished result might have been positive, negative, inconclusive, proprietary, classified or simply never developed into a publishable paper. Without the experimental report, measurements, apparatus description and independent replication, the public evidence cannot discriminate confidently among those possibilities.

The most defensible formulation is therefore narrower than either extreme: **the public record confirms an Army-funded experiment, but the surviving award record does not establish its outcome.**

## The 2003 Trail Does Not Supply the Missing Proof

There is one intriguing post-award development. Li appeared in the programme of a May 2003 gravitational-wave conference organised by MITRE with a paper titled **“Measurability of AC Gravity Fields”**, identified as HFGW-03-106. Contemporary material reproduces its abstract, which continued to explore the theoretical generation and measurement of an alternating gravitational field associated with high-temperature superconductivity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/sci.space/c/RKfc3i7Xc1g" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on google.com.</span></span></span>

That is useful evidence that Li’s research activity did not simply end when the Army agreement reached its September 2002 completion date. It is much weaker evidence for a technological breakthrough. A conference paper about the measurability and theoretical characteristics of a proposed field is not equivalent to a peer-reviewed demonstration of a propulsion system or gravity-control machine.

The *Huntsville Business Journal* also reported a private May 2003 email in which Li supposedly referred to an “11 kilowatts of output effect”. The phrase is tantalising but scientifically ambiguous: the publicly available account does not establish precisely what quantity was measured, how it was measured, what apparatus produced it, whether it represented gravitational output, or whether independent observers verified it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

That claim therefore cannot fill the evidential gap left by the unpublished Army work. A numerical fragment without the experiment’s definition, calibration, controls and reproducible results is not enough to demonstrate gravity manipulation.

## How a Real Army Award Became Conspiracy Evidence

The award became unusually powerful in later Ning Li lore because it supplied something many antigravity stories lack: a verifiable institutional anchor. Online investigators could point to an agreement number, an Army command, dates, a dollar amount and an explicitly gravity-related experimental objective. Early internet discussions reproduced precisely those details while simultaneously presenting Li as a “[missing scientist]({{ 'missing-scientist/' | relative_url }})”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.projectavalon.net/forum/showthread.php?t=1785" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectavalon.net">[Project Avalon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectavalon.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Avalon Anti Gravity Research And The Missing Dr. Ning Li</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Avalon Anti Gravity Research And The Missing Dr. Ning Li</span></span></span>

From there, a sequence that looked suggestive could easily be constructed:

**unconventional gravity theory → private company → Army prototype funding → unpublished results → reduced public visibility.**

Every step except the implied breakthrough has some documentary basis. That is exactly why the resulting story can seem stronger than an ordinary unsupported conspiracy claim.

The logical problem is the jump between the fourth and fifth elements. “The results were not published” does not mean “the results proved antigravity and were classified”. Likewise, “the Army funded a prototype experiment” does not mean “the Army obtained a successful prototype”. Those conclusions require evidence about the actual results, and the public award record does not contain it.

Freedom of Information Act activity illustrates the continuing gap. In 2022, a requester sought Department of Defense records relating specifically to AC Gravity’s $448,970 award because the project’s results had not become public. The request itself is not evidence about what the experiment found, but its existence shows that researchers were still attempting two decades later to obtain the underlying documentation rather than working from a publicly available success report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/department-of-defense-grant-for-ac-gravity-llc-2001-department-of-defense-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-and-security-135082/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on muckrock.com.</span></span></span>

The military connection should therefore neither be dismissed nor exaggerated. It is genuine and historically important. AMCOM was sufficiently interested in Li’s proposal to finance a substantial experimental test. That makes the case more consequential than a purely private antigravity claim. But the same document that establishes that connection also describes the work in cautious experimental terms.

## What the Award Actually Proves

For the wider question of Ning Li’s supposed disappearance, the AC Gravity award is best treated as a **documented fact surrounded by an undocumented outcome**.

It proves that AC Gravity obtained $448,970 in US government funding for an Army-associated superconducting-gravity experiment in 2001–02. It proves that the project was intended to test predictions from Li’s theory. It also shows that the Army regarded the possibility as sufficiently relevant to defence technology to justify a prototype Other Transaction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

It does **not** prove that the experiment generated a significant gravitational field, reduced an object’s weight, produced propulsive thrust, yielded a practical “antigravity” device or entered secret production. No publicly available result from the award establishes any of those propositions. The preceding published experiment involving Li had, if anything, placed a stringent upper limit on anomalous gravitational effects in the static configuration tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921453497014627" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Static test for a gravitational force coupled to type II YBCO superconductors - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 1997</span></span></span>

The later information gap is real as well. Public reporting says the Army-funded results were not published, while Li’s 2003 MITRE appearance shows that she continued discussing AC-gravity concepts after the formal award period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span> What cannot responsibly be inserted into that gap is a successful gravity-control experiment for which the necessary public evidence has never surfaced.

That makes the Army award both the strongest factual element in this part of the Ning Li story and one of its most frequently overinterpreted documents. It demonstrates **government interest in testing an extraordinary claim**. On the surviving public record, it does not demonstrate that the extraordinary claim passed the test.
