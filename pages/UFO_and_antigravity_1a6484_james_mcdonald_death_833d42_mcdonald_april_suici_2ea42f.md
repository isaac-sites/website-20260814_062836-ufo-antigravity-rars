## Introduction

James E. McDonald’s first shooting in April 1971 is the single most important piece of evidence for assessing later claims that his death was an assassination connected with UFO research. On 9 April, roughly two months before his death, the University of Arizona atmospheric physicist shot himself in the head in an apparent suicide attempt. He survived, but the bullet damaged his visual system severely, leaving him blind or nearly blind. Later accounts describe psychiatric hospitalisation and a gradual attempt to resume scientific work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/James_McDonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span></span></span>

That episode does not prove that every circumstance surrounding McDonald’s death on 13 June was straightforward. It does, however, transform the evidential problem. A murder theory cannot treat the June gunshot as an inexplicable first occurrence. Any alternative explanation has to account for a closely preceding, independently reported self-inflicted shooting, McDonald’s severe resulting injuries and contemporary evidence that people around him understood the April event as a suicide attempt. The April chronology therefore provides a powerful, non-conspiratorial explanation for the later death without requiring speculation about those who opposed his UFO work.

## What happened on 9 April 1971

The surviving accounts agree on the essential event. McDonald shot himself in the head on 9 April 1971 and survived. Ann Druffel’s *Firestorm: Dr. James E. McDonald’s Fight for UFO Science*, a sympathetic biography based substantially on McDonald’s papers and interviews with family, friends and colleagues, describes the bullet as missing his brain but damaging an optic nerve. A summary published in the *MUFON UFO Journal* in connection with Druffel’s research likewise gives 9 April as the date and says the wound blinded him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20May%202003.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronics and Books$3.00</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutual UFO Network UFO Journal May 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2003</span></span></span>

The date is also reported by sources openly sympathetic to suspicions surrounding McDonald’s eventual death. That agreement is significant because the existence of the April attempt is not principally a claim originating with sceptics seeking to dismiss the UFO connection. Even a website that explicitly labels McDonald’s death “suspicious” states that on 9 April he shot himself in the head, survived, suffered optic-nerve damage and was hospitalised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/James_McDonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span></span></span>

Contemporary evidence also supports the fact that a serious shooting had occurred before his death. UFO researcher Jacques Vallée recorded on 7 May 1971 that he had just learned from J. Allen Hynek that McDonald had shot himself in the head, survived and was expected to remain blind. Vallée’s entry is especially useful chronologically: it was written more than a month before McDonald died, so the April shooting cannot reasonably be dismissed as a story invented afterwards to make the June death appear suicidal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/05/vallee-on-mcdonald-final-part.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Vallee on McDonald - final part</span></span></span>

The precise personal causes of the attempt require more caution. Later biographies connect it with a severe deterioration in McDonald’s marriage, including his wife Betsy’s wish for a divorce, alongside professional pressures and public humiliation. The UFO controversy had certainly exposed him to ridicule, and his opposition to the proposed supersonic transport aircraft had brought another politically contentious scientific dispute into his life. But evidence that several pressures existed is stronger than evidence assigning a precise percentage of responsibility to any one of them.

This distinction matters. The sound historical claim is that McDonald was undergoing a serious [personal crisis]({{ 'personal-crisis/' | relative_url }}) and attempted suicide in April. The stronger assertion that one particular controversy “caused” the attempt is an interpretation, even when supported by people who knew him.

## The injuries and surviving biographical record

The April wound was catastrophic but not immediately fatal. Accounts differ slightly in how they describe its lasting effect: some say McDonald was blinded, while others describe him as almost blind or recovering limited peripheral vision. The safest conclusion is that the gunshot caused profound visual impairment. Druffel’s account says the bullet struck an optic nerve, and her later summary reports that doctors thought some vision might eventually return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20May%202003.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronics and Books$3.00</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutual UFO Network UFO Journal May 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2003</span></span></span>

This is one reason simplified retellings can become misleading. Saying only that McDonald was “blind” and then presenting his later movements as inherently mysterious can imply physical impossibilities that the underlying biographical narrative does not establish. His condition was grave, but surviving accounts describe him returning to his office and considering how he could continue productive scientific work despite his impaired sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/M/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal/MUFON%20UFO%20Journal%20-%20May%202003.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electronicsandbooks.com">[Electronics and Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electronicsandbooks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronics and Books$3.00</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutual UFO Network UFO Journal May 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2003</span></span></span>

A later account drawing on Vallée’s published diary provides another important detail. According to information Vallée recorded after a colleague spoke with Betsy McDonald, McDonald’s continued hospitalisation was not simply treatment for his damaged eyes: he was receiving psychiatric care intended to prevent another depressive crisis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2014/11/fbi-releases-its-files-on-dr-james-e.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[Bad UFOs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span> That evidence is second-hand and was recorded after the death, so it should not be treated like a hospital record. Nevertheless, it fits the independently established fact of the April attempt rather than requiring a new explanation for it.

Vallée’s contemporaneous 7 May entry is particularly valuable in separating what was known before the June death from later reconstruction. He wrote that McDonald had survived a self-inflicted head wound and that people were already discussing possible reasons for the attempt. He also noted the recent public controversy surrounding McDonald’s testimony against the supersonic transport programme and the press attention paid to his UFO advocacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/05/vallee-on-mcdonald-final-part.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Vallee on McDonald - final part</span></span></span> In other words, both the attempt and speculation about its causes existed in the record while McDonald was still alive.

McDonald’s continuing intellectual activity after the shooting is also consistent with the unusually driven scientist documented in the institutional record. The University of Arizona describes him as internationally known, continuously active in university affairs and engaged not only with UFOs but with cloud physics, weather modification, micrometeorology, the supersonic transport debate and other public scientific questions. His surviving collection contains four handwritten journals extending to 1971, extensive correspondence and research materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lib.arizona.edu/special-collections/collections/james-e-mcdonald-papers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.arizona.edu">[University of Arizona Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.arizona.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arizona.edu.</span></span></span>

The April attempt therefore should not be reduced to a caricature in either direction. McDonald was neither simply a scientist destroyed by UFO ridicule nor a man whose serious scientific work had ceased. He survived a life-threatening act, suffered devastating injuries and still tried to work. That combination makes the final weeks of his life more complicated, but not more supportive of assassination.

## Why the April attempt changes the foul-play claim

The strongest way to test a foul-play theory is to ask what it must explain that the suicide interpretation cannot. The April event substantially raises that burden.

First, there is **temporal continuity**. McDonald’s fatal shooting in June came only about nine weeks after a documented attempt to kill himself by essentially the same broad method: a gunshot to the head. The June death was therefore not an unprecedented form of violence suddenly appearing in the life of someone with no known history of self-harm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/James_McDonald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Murdering UAP Experts James E. Mc Donald &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span></span></span>

Second, there is **evidence independent of the final death scene**. Vallée knew about the earlier shooting by 7 May. His diary records information supplied through J. Allen Hynek while McDonald was alive. That is important because a hypothetical cover-up of the June death cannot easily explain away documentation that the victim had already survived an apparent suicide attempt weeks beforehand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/05/vallee-on-mcdonald-final-part.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Vallee on McDonald - final part</span></span></span>

Third, the earlier attempt was not a minor or ambiguous incident. It caused severe visual damage and was followed by psychiatric treatment according to later biographical evidence. This establishes a recent history of potentially lethal self-harm that has obvious relevance when evaluating another fatal gunshot soon afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2014/11/fbi-releases-its-files-on-dr-james-e.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[Bad UFOs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

Fourth, the April episode is acknowledged across sources with sharply different attitudes towards the UFO controversy. The *Arizona Daily Star*’s retrospective account says McDonald had attempted suicide by shooting himself in the head a few months before his death and reports that he left a note on both occasions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">His body was found near a bridge over the Canada del Oro wash near Ina Road</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily StarTales from the Morgue: UFOs are real - Arizona Daily StarJames McDonald, senior physicist at the Institute of Atmospheric...</span></span></span> UFO-oriented sources and writers sympathetic to McDonald likewise accept the earlier attempt. The dispute is therefore less about whether April happened than about what significance should be assigned to it.

None of this logically excludes homicide in June. A person who previously attempted suicide can subsequently be murdered. Nor does evidence of depression erase legitimate questions about McDonald’s conflicts, treatment by critics or the broader history of governmental interest in UFO research. But those are different propositions. Evidence that McDonald was controversial or monitored does not itself connect anyone to his death.

Indeed, the available FBI material cuts against an easy transition from “government interest” to “government assassination”. Released FBI records show that the Bureau had examined McDonald principally in connection with Cold War security concerns and contacts involving Soviet representatives. One internal source described his UFO-related contact as apparently innocuous and expressed confidence in his loyalty; the Bureau considered that interviewing him might accomplish little beyond causing embarrassment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2014/11/fbi-releases-its-files-on-dr-james-e.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[Bad UFOs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span> That does not prove that every government agency ignored him, but it illustrates the evidential gap between documented official attention and a claim of lethal action.

## The evidential weight of the April shooting

For the wider claim that UFO or unconventional aerospace researchers suffered suspicious deaths, McDonald is a useful example of why chronology matters more than a striking résumé followed by an early death. He genuinely was a prominent scientific advocate for serious UFO investigation. The University of Arizona archive documents an extensive programme of case investigation, government correspondence, [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) research, interviews, talks and handwritten journals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lib.arizona.edu/special-collections/collections/james-e-mcdonald-papers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.arizona.edu">[University of Arizona Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.arizona.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arizona.edu.</span></span></span> His controversial work was real; it need not be exaggerated to explain why later writers found his death intriguing.

But a responsible reconstruction cannot stop with “UFO scientist found shot dead”. Between those two facts lies the April attempt.

The April event changes the evidential balance because it supplies something assassination theories generally lack: a documented precursor that predicts the official explanation of the later death. Before 13 June, McDonald had already inflicted a severe gunshot wound on himself; contemporaries knew about it; his visual injuries were serious; and later biographical evidence describes psychiatric treatment following the attempt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/05/vallee-on-mcdonald-final-part.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Vallee on McDonald - final part</span></span></span>

That is considerably stronger evidence than retrospective pattern-matching between McDonald and other scientists who died under unrelated circumstances. To overturn the suicide interpretation, a foul-play theory would need positive evidence pointing towards another actor — forensic contradictions, reliable witnesses, documentary evidence of an operation, or similarly probative material. The fact that McDonald challenged official attitudes towards UFOs establishes a possible narrative motive; it does not supply that missing evidence.

The April shooting consequently does not make the June death beyond question. It does something more specific and historically important: it makes suicide a strongly evidenced explanation rather than an official label resting solely on the final scene. Any account of James E. McDonald as an allegedly silenced UFO researcher that omits 9 April 1971 removes the most important fact needed to judge that allegation fairly.
