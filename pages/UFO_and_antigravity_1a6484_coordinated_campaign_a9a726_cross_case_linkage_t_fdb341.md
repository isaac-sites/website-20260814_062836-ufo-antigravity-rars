## Introduction

A coordinated-killing claim becomes persuasive only when evidence crosses the boundaries between individual cases. It is not enough that several researchers worked on UFOs, unconventional propulsion or [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}), or that several deaths appear unusual when viewed separately. The decisive question is whether independent evidence shows that the same people, organisation, communications network, motive or operational machinery touched more than one case.

That is how genuine crime series are identified. The US National Institute of Justice describes forensic intelligence as combining evidence and situational data across incidents to uncover links; the FBI's Violent Criminal Apprehension Program similarly compares homicides and suspicious disappearances for common features. Crucially, a possible similarity is a lead, not proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/using-forensic-intelligence-combat-serial-and-organized-violent-crimes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nij.ojp.gov">[National Institute of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Institute of JusticeUsing Forensic Intelligence To Combat Serial and Organized Violent Crimes &#124; National Institute of JusticeOct...</span></span></span>

Applied to alleged deaths of [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) researchers, the publicly documented record currently lacks the kind of cross-case bridge that would transform a collection of suspicions into evidence of a [coordinated campaign]({{ 'coordination/' | relative_url }}). What would change that assessment is quite specific.

## Recurring actors would be the strongest bridge

The most important discovery would be an identifiable person or organisation appearing in operationally significant roles around two or more researchers. That means something substantially narrower than discovering that several people had worked with the US military, aerospace contractors, universities or government agencies. Those are large institutional worlds containing many thousands of employees, contractors and collaborators.

A meaningful recurring actor might instead be the same security official contacting two researchers shortly before separate deaths; the same intermediary arranging meetings with several victims; the same company obtaining their unpublished material; or the same small government unit demonstrably monitoring multiple researchers. Financial records showing payments from one sponsor to people involved in separate incidents could be still stronger. An authenticated document naming multiple targets would be stronger again.

The distinction between **association** and **operational involvement** matters particularly in this subject. Ning Li, for example, genuinely conducted unconventional gravity research and lived in Huntsville, Alabama. Her obituary identifies her as a researcher in superconductivity and antigravity and records that she died there in July 2021. Amy Eskridge also lived in Huntsville, studied at the University of Alabama in Huntsville and co-founded the Institute for Exotic Science; her 2022 obituary describes her interests in physics and other scientific fields.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; CrematoryJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

That geographical and intellectual overlap is worth documenting, but it does not by itself establish a common perpetrator. Huntsville is an unusually dense aerospace and defence research centre, so researchers interested in unconventional propulsion being found in the same scientific community is not intrinsically surprising. A genuinely probative Huntsville connection would need to become much narrower: the same threatening individual, surveillance team, restricted programme, unusual visitor, financial transaction or documented attempt to obtain research from both people.

This illustrates a central principle of crime-linkage analysis: useful similarities must be not merely consistent but sufficiently **distinctive** to discriminate a genuine series from unrelated cases. Research reviewing crime-linkage practice identifies behavioural consistency and distinctiveness as its two fundamental assumptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1002/jip.1531" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The practice of crime linkage: A review of the literature</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The practice of crime linkage: A review of the literature - Davies - 2019 - Journal of Investigative Psychology and Offender Profiling...</span></span></span>

## Communications could turn coincidence into evidence of intent

Documents and communications would be especially important because a coordinated killing is fundamentally a claim about a common plan. The ideal evidence would therefore reveal not merely that researchers felt threatened, but that the threats or instructions themselves connect separate cases.

Imagine, for example, that authenticated records from two researchers contained messages from the same sender using the same unusual terminology and referring to the same undisclosed project. That would create a testable cross-case connection. If telephone records then showed the sender communicating with people present around both deaths, the inference would become considerably stronger. An internal memorandum ordering surveillance of several named researchers would be more direct still.

The evidential hierarchy matters:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Strong linkage:** independently authenticated communications explicitly naming several researchers, a common operation or instructions concerning them.
* **Potentially significant linkage:** the same identifiable sender, telephone number, account, intermediary or unusual threat wording independently documented in separate cases.
* **Weak linkage:** different researchers reporting that unspecified people were following, intimidating or suppressing them.
* **Very weak linkage:** later commentators describing unrelated experiences using similar language such as “silenced”, “watched” or “threatened”.

</div>

This distinction becomes important when claims about Amy Eskridge are incorporated into broader [researcher-death]({{ 'missing-denominator/' | relative_url }}) theories. Recent reporting has circulated alleged messages in which Eskridge reportedly expressed fear for her safety before her 2022 death. Even if such messages were fully authenticated, however, they would primarily bear on **her individual case**. To support a coordinated researcher-killing theory, investigators would need to identify the people she feared and then connect those people, their communications or their organisation to another relevant death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[Hindustan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span>

The difference is easy to miss. Evidence that researcher A expected harm can increase suspicion about what happened to A. It does not automatically make the death of researcher B evidence of the same plot.

## Shared surveillance and forensic signatures would be harder to dismiss

Physical and digital evidence can create cross-case links without anyone leaving behind an explicit written order. Modern forensic intelligence is designed precisely to uncover connections that investigators might otherwise miss by examining incidents separately. NIJ describes the approach as combining forensic and situational information to connect evidence, suspects, cases and crime scenes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/forensics/forensic-intelligence" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nij.ojp.gov">[National Institute of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Institute of JusticeForensic Intelligence &#124; National Institute of JusticeOctober 10, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Ballistics provide an unusually clear illustration. The US Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives operates the National Integrated Ballistic Information Network (NIBIN), which compares cartridge-case markings across crimes. A potential association must still be examined and confirmed, but a genuine match can establish that the same firearm was used at otherwise apparently unrelated scenes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atf.gov/firearms/tools-services-law-enforcement/national-integrated-ballistic-information-network-nibin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atf.gov">[ATF]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atf.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">national integrated ballistic information network nibin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">national integrated ballistic information network nibin</span></span></span>

An equivalent evidential breakthrough in alleged researcher killings might involve the same weapon, DNA profile, unusual chemical substance, vehicle, surveillance equipment, forged identity or digital infrastructure appearing independently in multiple cases. Repeated access to the same anonymised communications account, for example, could be far more significant than similarities in the victims' scientific interests.

Digital evidence could be particularly revealing. Device records might establish that one operative was near multiple researchers at relevant times. Server logs could connect threatening communications sent to different researchers. Travel, hotel or vehicle records could show the same surveillance personnel repeatedly converging on victims shortly before critical incidents. Financial data could connect ostensibly independent perpetrators to a common payer.

None of those hypothetical discoveries would automatically prove a killing programme, but they would provide something presently missing from the public narrative: an evidential pathway that runs **from one case into another**.

## A common motive must be specific enough to test

“Researching antigravity” is too broad to function as a persuasive [common motive]({{ 'common-motive/' | relative_url }}). Even “working on technology that threatened powerful interests” remains a hypothesis unless investigators can identify what technology, whose interests and how each researcher came into conflict with them.

A much stronger common-motive case would look more concrete. Suppose several researchers independently possessed copies of the same restricted technical material and each received demands from the same organisation to surrender it. Or suppose internal correspondence showed that several named scientists were regarded as disclosure risks for one particular programme. If adverse events then followed identifiable disclosure attempts, the sequence could be investigated against documents, dates, witnesses and communications.

This requirement also prevents unrelated deaths from being forced into one narrative. Eugene Mallove's murder is a useful stress test. Because Mallove promoted [cold fusion]({{ 'cold-fusion/' | relative_url }}) and unconventional energy research, his 2004 killing can appear superficially compatible with a theory that controversial energy researchers were being eliminated. Yet the subsequent criminal case developed a much more specific, local chain of evidence.

A 2025 Connecticut Appellate Court decision recounting the case states that Mallove was found dead at his Norwich rental property after having recently evicted tenants. It records the investigation into people connected with that property and the eventual murder and conspiracy-to-commit-murder conviction of Chad Schaffer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on justia.com.</span></span></span>

For Mallove's death to become evidence of a broader researcher-elimination operation, therefore, proponents would need evidence connecting those participants or the circumstances behind the murder to another researcher case. Merely observing that Mallove advocated controversial energy research does not overcome the case-specific evidence developed in the criminal proceedings.

That is exactly why cross-case evidence is so valuable: it can distinguish a genuinely common motive from a theme imposed retrospectively on very different events.

## Why the cases must be tested against one another

A useful way to investigate a suspected series is to build a matrix rather than a narrative. The FBI's ViCAP system exists to compare violent crimes and suspicious missing-person cases across jurisdictions, using information about victims, offenders, crime scenes, laboratory findings and other investigative details. Its analysts conduct case-linkage analysis, timelines, mapping and crime-series comparisons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-investigate/behavioral-analysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Behavioral Analysis — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Behavioral Analysis — FBI...</span></span></span>

Applied conceptually to alleged researcher deaths, the rows would be individual cases and the columns would contain testable features: named threats, organisations, known visitors, communications identifiers, weapons, toxicology, vehicles, financial transactions, research projects, disclosure events and precise timelines.

A genuine series should begin producing intersections that are difficult to explain independently. The same obscure individual appearing around three researchers is more informative than all three having defence connections. The same encrypted account contacting two victims is more informative than both reporting unspecified surveillance. A confirmed forensic match is more informative than two deaths being labelled “mysterious” online.

Statistical crime-linkage research reinforces the danger of treating resemblance as certainty. One NIJ-supported study used Bayes factors to measure whether crimes shared a common offender; even a model capable of identifying 82 per cent of actual links at one operating point still produced a 5 per cent false-positive rate. In other words, apparent matches occur even in formal systems designed specifically to discriminate linked from unlinked offences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/statistical-approach-crime-linkage" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nij.ojp.gov">[National Institute of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ojp.gov.</span></span></span>

The practical lesson is not that similarities are worthless. It is that they are most useful as **search instructions**: if two cases share an unusual feature, investigators should look for harder evidence behind it.

## Thematic similarity is not operational linkage

The weakest versions of the coordinated-killing argument tend to begin by defining a category — UFO researchers, antigravity scientists, alternative-energy advocates or people associated with sensitive aerospace work — and then collecting deaths and disappearances within it. That procedure risks selecting cases because they already resemble the proposed pattern.

Ning Li demonstrates the problem particularly clearly. Online accounts long described her as having “disappeared” after leaving public antigravity research. Yet her funeral-home obituary records that she died in Huntsville in 2021 and was survived by her son and grandchildren. Whatever legitimate questions remain about the later history of her research, the public evidence does not support treating her as a person who simply vanished without trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; CrematoryJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Likewise, the fact that Eskridge and Li were both associated with Huntsville and unconventional gravity research establishes an interesting **research-community connection**, not a homicide connection. Mallove unquestionably was murdered, but the Connecticut judicial record supplies a specific property-related context and identified defendants. ## What discovery would materially change the picture?

The present evidential gap is therefore narrower and more concrete than the sprawling conspiracy literature sometimes suggests. Researchers would not need to prove every detail of a hypothetical programme before the coordinated-killing claim deserved serious reconsideration. They would need to find at least one robust bridge between cases and then independently corroborate it.

A particularly consequential discovery would be an authenticated record naming multiple researchers as targets; a recurring individual demonstrably involved in several incidents; communications connecting separate perpetrators to one controller; matching forensic evidence; financial transfers linking participants across cases; or surveillance records showing the same operational team repeatedly following different researchers.

Several weaker links could also become powerful if they converged independently. The same obscure intermediary, the same communications infrastructure, matching travel records and a shared specific motive would collectively be much harder to dismiss than any one similarity alone. Conversely, ten cases sharing only an interest in unconventional propulsion would remain evidentially weak because that characteristic was used to select the cases in the first place.

This is the critical distinction between a **pattern of subjects** and a **pattern of perpetrators**. The existing public record contains enough unusual histories to justify careful case-by-case scrutiny, and some individual incidents contain genuine questions or documented violence. What it has not yet produced is reliable evidence showing that the same operational machinery caused separate researcher deaths. Until such a cross-case link appears, coordinated killing remains a hypothesis rather than the conclusion established by the cases themselves.
