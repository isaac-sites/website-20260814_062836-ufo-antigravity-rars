## Introduction

Amy [Eskridge]({{ 'eskridge/' | relative_url }})'s obituary establishes an important but narrow set of facts: the Huntsville researcher died on 11 June 2022 at the age of 34, had studied at the University of Alabama in Huntsville, and was co-founder, chairwoman and president of the Institute for Exotic Science. What it does **not** establish is how she died. The notice published through AL.com and Legacy gives no medical cause, no manner of death, no account of the circumstances and no investigative finding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

## What the obituary actually verifies

The original notice identifies Amy Catherine Eskridge and says she died on Saturday, 11 June 2022. It gives her age as 34 and describes a life centred on science and space. According to the obituary, she graduated from the University of Alabama in Huntsville with a double major in chemistry and biology and pursued interests spanning electrical engineering, chemistry, physics, genetic engineering and nanotechnology. It identifies her as chairwoman and president of the Institute for Exotic Science in Huntsville, which it says she co-founded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

Those details make the obituary genuinely useful within the broader discussion of alleged suspicious deaths among UFO, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) and unconventional-technology researchers. It is contemporaneous evidence that Eskridge was not merely labelled an exotic-science researcher years after her death. Her own memorial notice publicly connected her to the Institute for Exotic Science and to an unusually broad scientific career at the time her death was announced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obits.al.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obits.al.com">[AL.com Obituaries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obits.al.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ObituariesAmy Eskridge Obituary (1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)...</span></span></span>

The notice also provides ordinary memorial details that help anchor its purpose and provenance. It names her parents and brothers, announces a celebration of life in Huntsville for 10 July 2022, asks for charitable donations instead of flowers and says her friend Sam Reid intended to commemorate her through a satellite and lunar payload. AL.com records the obituary as published from 21 to 26 June 2022.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

These are precisely the kinds of claims for which an obituary can be useful: identity, date of death, age, family connections, education, professional affiliations and the way the deceased's life was presented by those memorialising her. They do not answer the separate forensic question of why or how she died.

## The missing cause and manner of death

The obituary never identifies a cause of death. It does not say that Eskridge died from a gunshot wound, illness, accident, suicide, homicide or any other cause. Its description that she “went to her eternal rest in the Lord” is memorial language, not a medical finding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2022</span></span></span>

There is also no statement of **manner of death**. Cause and manner are distinct concepts. The Alabama Department of Public Health explains that manner is the broader classification—natural, accidental, homicide, suicide or undetermined—whereas cause is the more specific condition or injury responsible for death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/cdr/manner-and-cause.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthManner and Cause of Deaths &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)May 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2021</span></span></span> The distinction matters especially in Eskridge's case because later accounts commonly describe both a physical cause, a self-inflicted gunshot wound, and a manner, suicide. Neither appears in her obituary.

Alabama's own vital-record system shows where such information properly belongs. The Department of Public Health defines a [death certificate]({{ 'death-certificate/' | relative_url }}) as a government-issued document declaring the date, location and cause of a person's death. Alabama's Electronic Death Registration System allows physicians, coroners, medical examiners, funeral homes and other authorised providers to complete and certify the relevant portions of that record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/edrs/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthFrequently Asked Questions &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)March 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2026</span></span></span>

That makes the evidential hierarchy important. An obituary can be excellent evidence that a death occurred and useful evidence about biography. A death certificate, medical certification, autopsy or investigative record is the appropriate category of evidence for a formal cause or manner determination. The documents perform different functions.

There is a practical reason why independent online researchers may encounter the obituary more easily than Eskridge's underlying vital record. Alabama restricts access to death certificates for 25 years after death, generally limiting certified copies during that period to specified relatives, legal representatives and the informant named on the certificate. Eskridge died in 2022, so her certificate remains within that restricted period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthDeath Certificates &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)March 27, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 27, 2026</span></span></span> The ready availability of an obituary therefore should not be confused with the availability of the underlying medical certification.

## Later reports do not change what the obituary says

Recent coverage has reported information absent from the 2022 notice. In 2026, amid renewed attention to deaths and disappearances involving scientists, several outlets described Eskridge's death as having been ruled a suicide involving a self-inflicted gunshot wound.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[Hindustan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span> That is a materially different evidential claim from anything contained in her obituary.

The distinction should be maintained even where multiple later reports repeat the same account. A reader citing the obituary can safely write that Eskridge died on 11 June 2022 aged 34 and that her memorial identified her with the Institute for Exotic Science. A statement that authorities ruled the death suicide requires a source that actually reports that finding. It cannot be footnoted to an obituary which never says so.

The same rule applies in the opposite direction. Later material attributed to Eskridge has been cited by people questioning the reported suicide ruling. In 2026, reporting described messages said to have been sent before her death in which she denied intending to kill herself and expressed fears about threats or attacks. Those allegations have contributed to renewed controversy around the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/mystery-death-amy-eskridge-1795573" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ibtimes.co.uk">[International Business Times UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ibtimes.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mystery death amy eskridge 1795573</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Business Times UKAmy Eskridge&#x27;s Alleged Voice Call Recording Details Kidnapping Attempt in 2021 as Doubts on her Death Deep...</span></span></span> They may be relevant when assessing the wider dispute, but they do not turn the obituary's silence into evidence of homicide.

## Why obituary silence proves neither suicide nor murder

An omitted cause of death can easily acquire significance when a young person's death is controversial. But the inference “the obituary did not say why she died, therefore the circumstances must have been suspicious” does not follow from the document itself.

[Obituaries]({{ 'obituaries/' | relative_url }}) are memorial notices rather than comprehensive forensic reports. Families and publishers can concentrate on biography, survivors and funeral arrangements without describing medical or traumatic circumstances. Eskridge's notice follows exactly that form: it celebrates her intellectual interests, education, family and scientific ambitions before giving details of her memorial ceremony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obits.al.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obits.al.com">[AL.com Obituaries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obits.al.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ObituariesAmy Eskridge Obituary (1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)...</span></span></span> Nothing in the text explains why the cause was omitted.

Consequently, the omission cannot legitimately be converted into evidence for either side of the dispute. It does not establish suicide because suicide is never mentioned. It does not establish murder or suppression because neither is mentioned. Nor does it show that the family rejected, accepted or wished to conceal a particular official determination. Those conclusions require independent evidence.

The same caution applies to the obituary's online guest book. Later entries include statements suggesting that Eskridge was “silenced”, that the “truth” would emerge, or that her work would eventually be vindicated. Such comments demonstrate that suspicions became attached to her memory; they are not eyewitness testimony, medical findings or investigative conclusions. The Legacy memorial remains open to public entries years after her death, including numerous comments posted in 2025 and 2026.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/legacyremembers/amy-eskridge-memorial?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge memorial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">amy eskridge memorial</span></span></span> Treating those retrospective tributes as part of the original obituary would introduce another serious source error.

## What would actually settle the evidential question?

For the narrow question of cause and manner of Eskridge's death, stronger evidence would be the relevant death certificate and medical certification, an autopsy or forensic report where one exists, contemporaneous police or coroner records, or an attributable statement from the responsible investigating authority. Alabama's public-health documentation makes clear that formal death registration includes cause-of-death information and that medical certifiers participate in completing that record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/edrs/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthFrequently Asked Questions &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)March 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2026</span></span></span>

Such records would not automatically resolve every allegation surrounding the death. A formal manner-of-death determination can itself be disputed, and new evidence can sometimes prompt reassessment. Alabama public-health guidance expressly recognises “undetermined” as a manner and notes that such classifications can later be redesignated after further investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/cdr/manner-and-cause.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alabama Department of Public HealthManner and Cause of Deaths &#124; Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH)May 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2021</span></span></span> But those are forensic and investigative questions. They cannot be answered by reading additional meaning into a memorial notice.

For Amy Eskridge, therefore, the defensible conclusion from the obituary is deliberately limited. **She died; the obituary dates her death to 11 June 2022; she was 34; and it documents her connection to unconventional scientific research. It does not state her cause or manner of death.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville)June 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2022</span></span></span> Later reports of suicide and later allegations challenging that account must each be evaluated against their own evidence. The obituary's silence is not the missing proof for either claim—it is simply a boundary on what that particular source can establish.
