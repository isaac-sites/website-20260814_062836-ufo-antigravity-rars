## Introduction

Ning Li is often described in [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) and UFO-related lore as a physicist who developed extraordinary gravity-control technology and then “disappeared”. The documented record supports only the first half of that story in a limited sense: Li was a real physicist who published unconventional work on possible gravitational effects in superconductors, later received US military funding, and then largely vanished from the open scientific literature. What it does **not** support is that she became a missing person, was abducted, defected to China, or was secretly eliminated.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_ning_li_disappearanc_abd73c-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Ning Li" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

The most important corrective came from Li’s own family. In 2023, her son George Men told the *Huntsville Business Journal* that she remained in the United States, continued doing classified defence-related work, and in 2014 suffered catastrophic injuries after being struck by a vehicle on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. She never returned to work, spent her final years seriously impaired and died in Huntsville on 27 July 2021.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

Her case is therefore valuable precisely because it shows how a genuine information gap can turn into a disappearance narrative without evidence that anyone actually disappeared.

## The gravity research that made Li notable

Li did not enter antigravity folklore as an anonymous inventor with unverifiable claims. During the early 1990s, while associated with the University of Alabama in Huntsville, she and physicist Douglas Torr published papers exploring whether superconducting systems might produce unusually strong gravitomagnetic or related gravitational effects.

A 1992 paper in *Physical Review B*, for example, examined gravitational effects in the electromagnetic behaviour of superconductors and argued that gravity might have implications for superconductivity. Whatever one makes of the broader interpretation later attached to this work, the paper itself was genuine peer-reviewed physics rather than an internet-era fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on aps.org.</span></span></span>

Li and collaborators subsequently moved towards experimental tests. A 1997 study investigated claims of anomalous gravitational effects associated with high-temperature superconductors. Later summaries of that work note that Li’s group did **not** reproduce the large weight-reduction effects reported in some contemporary rotating-superconductor experiments. That detail matters because popular accounts sometimes collapse several different lines of controversial superconducting-gravity research into a single claim that Li had already demonstrated working antigravity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ning_Li_%28physicist%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikipedia.org">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikipedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The next stage gave the story its most consequential factual anchor. Li left the university environment and established AC Gravity LLC. A US Department of Defense record, subsequently reproduced in archival sources, identifies a US Army Aviation and Missile Command project titled “Gravito - Electro Magnetic Superconductivity Experiment”. The agreement, effective from April 2001 to September 2002, provided $448,970 to AC Gravity for an experimental attempt to test predictions arising from Li’s superconducting-gravity theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zpenergy.com.</span></span></span>

The military funding was therefore real. It is also easy to see why it later became central to conspiracy accounts. An unconventional physicist had moved from publishing openly to operating a private company; the Army had paid for a prototype experiment; and the results did not subsequently appear in the normal scientific literature.

But military interest is not evidence that the experiment succeeded. The surviving public award description says that the project was intended to **test** Li’s theoretical predictions. It does not document a functioning gravity-control device, much less establish that one was subsequently hidden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zpenergy.com.</span></span></span>

## How an information vacuum became a mystery

The strongest foundation for the “Ning Li disappeared” story is not evidence of disappearance. It is the sharp decline in publicly available information about her after the early 2000s.

Li was still demonstrably active in 2003. A programme from a gravitational-wave conference hosted by MITRE lists her presentation, “Measurability of AC Gravity Fields”, on 7 May 2003. Contemporary conference material likewise identifies the paper as HFGW-03-106.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integrityresearchinstitute.org/Gravity-Meeting-Schedule.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integrityresearchinstitute.org">[Integrity Research Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integrityresearchinstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on integrityresearchinstitute.org.</span></span></span>

After that period, her trail in open scientific publishing effectively stops. The *Huntsville Business Journal* found that AC Gravity continued to exist on paper for years, while no comparable stream of published experimental results emerged. The same investigation reported a private 2003 communication in which Li referred to an “11 kilowatts of output effect”, but neither the meaning nor the underlying experiment is publicly documented well enough to establish what the phrase represented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

That combination was fertile ground for speculation: real defence funding, a provocative research topic, no public experimental results and an increasingly difficult-to-contact scientist.

By 2004, people interested in gravity research were already trying to locate her. According to correspondence described by the *Huntsville Business Journal*, researcher Tim Ventura told Eugene [Podkletnov]({{ 'podkletnov/' | relative_url }}) that repeated emails to an address associated with Li appeared to be read but went unanswered. Podkletnov reportedly replied that Li was alive and working with the Department of Defense and could not discuss her work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

That exchange is important because it shows that the “[missing scientist]({{ 'missing-scientist/' | relative_url }})” framing had begun while there was already an alternative explanation circulating among people in the field: she was deliberately unavailable rather than physically missing.

The mythology grew substantially after a 2008 interview with physicist Jack Sarfatti. Sarfatti asserted that Li had disappeared, returned to China and could no longer be reached by people at the Pentagon. Those claims were repeated online and eventually became part of videos and discussions presenting Li as a possible defector, captive researcher or participant in an inaccessible black programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

Years later, her son directly contradicted the core elements of that account.

According to George Men, Li did **not** move back to China and did not defect. Chinese officials did approach her in the United States in 2008 and wanted her to return, he said, but she declined. Men also recalled that when Li later wanted to travel to China for her mother’s funeral, she was prevented from doing so.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

This is an unusually revealing example of how conspiracy narratives can form around fragments of genuine information. There apparently was contact with Chinese officials. Li genuinely became difficult for outsiders to contact. Her work did involve the US defence establishment. But those facts were combined into a much stronger claim — that she had secretly gone to China — that her family later said was false.

## Classified work explains some of the silence, but not the physics

The family account provides a plausible reason why Li stopped behaving like an academic scientist.

Men told the *Huntsville Business Journal* that his mother obtained a top-secret security clearance and that, unlike during her university career, she was no longer free to publish or discuss her work. He recalled her becoming considerably more guarded after leaving academia and refusing even to tell him what she was doing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

That testimony fits several otherwise puzzling features of the chronology: Li had a documented military-funded project; she remained associated with gravity-related research at least through the 2003 MITRE meeting; colleagues described her as continuing defence work; and then her public scientific output largely ceased.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zpenergy.com.</span></span></span>

There is an important evidentiary distinction, however. Men’s account is strong evidence about his mother’s whereabouts and family life. It is not independent verification that her classified research produced successful antigravity technology.

Nor does the absence of released grant results establish success. Freedom of Information Act requests relating to the AC Gravity project have been made, and records surrounding the request process are publicly visible. The *Huntsville Business Journal* reporter also said his requests for grant results were denied. That leaves a genuine documentary gap, but a gap is compatible with many possibilities: classified reporting, administrative restrictions, inconclusive work, archived technical material or results that never developed into a viable technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/department-of-defense-grant-for-ac-gravity-llc-2001-department-of-defense-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-and-security-135082/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on muckrock.com.</span></span></span>

Consequently, two separate questions are often mistakenly treated as one.

The first is: **Why did Ning Li stop publishing publicly?** Her son’s account gives a credible answer — classified defence work followed eventually by serious injury.

The second is: **Did that hidden work demonstrate controllable antigravity?** The publicly accessible evidence does not answer that question.

Keeping those propositions separate removes much of the apparent mystery without pretending that every aspect of her government-funded research is now known.

## The family account of her final years

The decisive event in Li’s actual disappearance from professional life came much later than internet retellings usually imply.

According to George Men, Li was struck by a vehicle in 2014 while crossing a street on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. The collision caused permanent brain damage. Men said she was subsequently diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease and never returned to work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

The accident also devastated the family in another way. Li’s husband witnessed it and suffered a heart attack at the scene, according to Men. He died the following year. Men then brought his mother home and cared for her through roughly the final six years of her life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

These details were not public during the years in which online writers were asking where Li had gone. That absence of information allowed professional silence caused by [classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}) and, later, profound disability to be interpreted as a single continuous “disappearance”.

Li died in Huntsville on 27 July 2021. Berryhill Funeral Home published an obituary giving the same date and identifying her surviving son and grandchildren. The funeral service and burial were also held locally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/guestbook/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[berryhillfh.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

The obituary alone already posed a substantial problem for claims that Li had permanently defected to China or vanished into an unknown foreign programme. The subsequent interview with her son provided the missing chronology: she had continued living and working in the United States, suffered a disabling accident and then spent years under family care.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

## Why “disappeared” is the wrong description

The phrase “Ning Li disappeared” mixes three different events that should be distinguished.

**She disappeared from academic publishing.** This is substantially true. Her visible publication record and public research activity fell away after the early 2000s.

**She became difficult for scientific acquaintances and outside researchers to contact.** This is also supported by contemporary attempts to reach her and by reports that she was involved in restricted defence work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

**She physically disappeared or became a missing person.** The available evidence does not support this. The *Huntsville Business Journal* investigation explicitly found that she had never been reported missing in any official sense, and her son described her continued life in Huntsville in considerable detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

That distinction is particularly important in discussions of alleged suspicious deaths and disappearances among UFO or unconventional-propulsion researchers. Li’s case can look ominous when reduced to a few chronological points — antigravity papers, Army money, classified work, silence, death. Once the missing family history is restored, however, the claim changes substantially.

There remains an unresolved secrecy question surrounding portions of her defence research. There remains a legitimate scientific question about what AC Gravity actually achieved. Neither unresolved point constitutes evidence that Li herself was secretly removed.

## What remains genuinely unresolved

Correcting the disappearance story does not require pretending that Li’s entire professional record is transparent.

The publicly available documentation still leaves important unanswered questions about the technical outcome of the 2001–02 Army-funded experiment. The award’s existence and objective can be documented, and Li’s 2003 conference participation can be documented, but a public final technical report demonstrating what the project actually produced has not emerged from the sources reviewed here.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zpenergy.com.</span></span></span>

Likewise, the reported “11 kilowatts of output effect” remains too poorly documented to treat as evidence of a breakthrough. Without experimental configuration, measurement methods, controls, raw data and independent replication, the phrase cannot bear the technological significance later attached to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

Those gaps may continue to attract interest because secrecy itself forms part of Li’s documented story. But the uncertainty belongs primarily to **what she researched**, not **whether she mysteriously vanished**.

On the latter question, the record has become considerably clearer. Ning Li moved from open academic work into a much less public defence environment. In 2014 she suffered devastating injuries in a traffic accident, never resumed her career, was cared for by her son and died in Huntsville in 2021. The celebrated “missing antigravity scientist” story arose because outsiders lacked that information for years. Once her family supplied it, the supposed disappearance largely ceased to be a mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>
