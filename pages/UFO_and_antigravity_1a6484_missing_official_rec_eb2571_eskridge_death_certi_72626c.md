## Introduction

Amy Eskridge’s death certificate is not presently a public record in Alabama. That fact has a straightforward legal explanation: Alabama restricts public access to death certificates for 25 years after the date of death. Eskridge died on 11 June 2022, according to her published obituary, so her certificate falls squarely inside that statutory confidentiality period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LegacyAmy Eskridge Obituary (1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because Eskridge’s death has become part of wider speculation about researchers associated with unconventional propulsion and UFO-related subjects. The inability of an ordinary member of the public to obtain her death certificate cannot reasonably be treated as evidence that the document is missing, suppressed or specially sealed. It is the expected consequence of a statewide rule applying to recent Alabama deaths. At the same time, the restriction means that independent researchers cannot presently use the certificate itself to verify reported statements about her certified cause and manner of death.

## What a death certificate could establish

Eskridge’s obituary provides a firm contemporary anchor for the basic chronology. Published by AL.com through Legacy in June 2022, it identifies her as Amy Catherine Eskridge, records that she died on Saturday, 11 June 2022, aged 34, and describes her as chairwoman and president of the Huntsville-based Institute for Exotic Science. It does not state a cause or manner of death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LegacyAmy Eskridge Obituary (1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville</span></span></span>

That omission should not be confused with what an Alabama death certificate would contain. The certificate belongs to the state’s vital-records system and includes the formal medical certification associated with the death. The Alabama Department of Public Health (ADPH) expressly notes that changes can be made by the certifier to the medical certification and that errors can be corrected through an amendment process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alabamapublichealth.gov.</span></span></span>

For the Eskridge case, access to the certificate would therefore be useful principally because it could establish what was formally certified in the state record. Recent reporting has described her death as a suicide involving a self-inflicted gunshot wound, but that reporting is not the same thing as inspecting the underlying certificate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[Hindustan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span>

A death certificate also has important limits. It is a vital record, not a substitute for a complete death investigation. Even if a certified copy became available and confirmed a particular cause and manner, it would not ordinarily provide all the scene evidence, witness interviews, forensic reasoning or investigative material necessary to reconstruct how authorities reached their conclusion. Consequently, the certificate could resolve one important documentary question — what Alabama’s official vital record says — without necessarily resolving every dispute about the circumstances of the death.

## Alabama's 25-year confidentiality rule

The central access issue is unusually clear. ADPH states that Alabama death certificates are confidential records with restricted access for **25 years from the date of death**. Once a certificate is more than 25 years old, anyone may obtain it after providing sufficient identifying information and paying the required fee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alabamapublichealth.gov.</span></span></span>

This is not merely an agency website policy. Alabama Code § 22-9A-21 governs disclosure of vital-record information. The statute generally prohibits inspection, disclosure or copying except as authorised by law, administrative rules or a court order. It further provides that, after 25 years have elapsed from a death, the record becomes a “nonrestricted public record” available to any person who supplies sufficient information to locate it and pays the required fee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2012/title-22/title-1/section-22-9a-21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawSection 22-9A-21 - Disclosure of information from vital records.:: 2012 Code of Alabama:: US Codes and Statutes:: US Law...</span></span></span>

The Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press reaches the same conclusion in its guide to Alabama open-government law: death certificates do not become nonrestricted public records until 25 years after the death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/alabama/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rcfp.org">[Reporters Committee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rcfp.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reporters Committee Open Government Guide Alabama</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reporters CommitteeOpen Government Guide Alabama - Reporters CommitteeJuly 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>

Applied to Eskridge, whose recorded date of death is 11 June 2022, the consequence is straightforward. Under the current rule, the 25-year period does not expire until **11 June 2047**. The certificate therefore should not be expected to be available to an unrelated member of the public in 2026 simply because the case has attracted public interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/huntsville/name/amy-eskridge-obituary?id=35311909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amy eskridge obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LegacyAmy Eskridge Obituary (1987 - 2022) - Huntsville, AL - AL.com (Huntsville</span></span></span>

The restriction is also broader than this individual case. ADPH says Alabama began filing death certificates statewide in 1908 and applies the same 25-year restriction to recent certificates generally. The department has explained that access to birth and death certificates is restricted in part because certified vital records can be misused for identity theft and other fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alabamapublichealth.gov.</span></span></span>

## Who can obtain a recent certificate?

“Confidential” does not mean that nobody can obtain the document. It means that access during the restricted period depends on the applicant’s legal entitlement.

ADPH currently lists parents, spouses, sons or daughters, brothers or sisters, qualifying grandchildren, legal representatives of the family or estate, and the informant named on the certificate among those who may obtain a death certificate less than 25 years old. Identification is required.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alabamapublichealth.gov.</span></span></span>

Alabama’s administrative rules add detail. They define immediate family for vital-record purposes and specifically provide that the informant named on a death certificate and the executor or administrator of the deceased person’s estate may obtain certified copies. Government representatives may obtain vital records needed for official duties, while an entitled person can authorise another individual to collect a copy. Applicants for death certificates less than 25 years old must provide valid identification, and the State Registrar can require additional documentation demonstrating entitlement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/rule/420-7-1-.22" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: admincode.legislature.state.al.us">[Alabama Administrative Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">admincode.legislature.state.al.us</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">abama Administrative CodeRule 420-7-1-.22 Who May Obtain Certified Copies Of Vital Records...</span></span></span>

The underlying statute is somewhat broader still. It permits immediate family members and their legal representatives to obtain copies and provides a route for others who can demonstrate that information from a vital record is necessary to determine or protect a personal or property right. It also allows disclosure to government agencies for official duties and permits certain statistical or legitimate research uses under conditions imposed by the State Registrar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2012/title-22/title-1/section-22-9a-21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawSection 22-9A-21 - Disclosure of information from vital records.:: 2012 Code of Alabama:: US Codes and Statutes:: US Law...</span></span></span>

Those exceptions are important when interpreting unsuccessful public searches. A certificate may legally exist and be accessible to relatives or another authorised applicant while remaining unavailable to journalists, UFO researchers and unrelated members of the public.

## What non-access does and does not imply

In discussions surrounding Eskridge, the most important distinction is between **a record that cannot currently be obtained by the general public** and **a record whose existence or contents have been deliberately concealed in this particular case**. Alabama’s rules provide affirmative evidence for the first proposition; they do not, by themselves, provide evidence for the second.

The ordinary public cannot presently retrieve Eskridge’s certificate because the same legal restriction applies to recent Alabama death certificates generally. ADPH even requires applicants for restricted certificates to identify their relationship to the deceased and supply identification. Its administrative rules authorise the registrar to refuse a certificate when an applicant cannot establish entitlement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/vitalrecords/death-certificates.html?iframed=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alabamapublichealth.gov">[Alabama Department of Public Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alabamapublichealth.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alabamapublichealth.gov.</span></span></span>

That changes the evidential meaning of a failed search. Statements such as “her death certificate cannot be found online” or “the public has not seen the certificate” may be literally true, but they do not establish an unusual records anomaly. Under Alabama law, public non-access is precisely what should be expected for a 2022 death.

There is an equally important limit in the opposite direction. The confidentiality rule does **not** independently confirm whatever cause or manner of death has been reported in secondary sources. Recent media accounts describe Eskridge’s death as a self-inflicted gunshot and say it was ruled a suicide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/amy-eskridge-update-ufo-scientists-old-texts-cast-doubt-on-cause-of-death-would-never-kill-myself-101776897906208.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hindustantimes.com">[Hindustan Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hindustantimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hindustan TimesAmy Eskridge update: UFO scientist&#x27;s old texts cast doubt on cause of death; ‘would never kill myself’ &#124; Hindustan TimesAp...</span></span></span> Without access to the certificate, however, an unrelated researcher cannot point to the certificate itself as independent primary-document verification of those reports.

## The documentary gap is ordinary, not proof of concealment

For a case already surrounded by claims about UFOs, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) research and possible suppression, an inaccessible official document can easily acquire more significance than the evidence warrants. Here, the governance mechanism is unusually mundane: Alabama deliberately gives recent death certificates a long confidentiality period.

That does not require readers to accept every secondary account of Eskridge’s death uncritically. It requires separating two different questions. **Why can the public not obtain her death certificate?** Alabama’s 25-year rule provides a strong, documented answer. **What exactly does her certificate say?** For an unauthorised member of the public, that remains unverified from the certificate itself.

Unless an authorised holder releases a genuine copy, a court orders disclosure, another lawful access route applies, or the governing law changes, the normal public-access milestone under the current statute is June 2047. Until then, the absence of Eskridge’s death certificate from the public domain is best understood as a legally predictable records limitation — an obstacle to independent verification, but not evidence in itself of exceptional concealment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2012/title-22/title-1/section-22-9a-21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawSection 22-9A-21 - Disclosure of information from vital records.:: 2012 Code of Alabama:: US Codes and Statutes:: US Law...</span></span></span>
