## Introduction

When an obituary for a UFO, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) or unconventional-propulsion researcher gives no cause of death, the omission is an evidential gap — not evidence that the cause was sinister or deliberately suppressed. [Obituaries]({{ 'obituaries/' | relative_url }}) are memorial and biographical notices, not medical certificates, and families routinely decide how much information about a death to make public. Funeral guidance explicitly treats disclosure of the cause as optional.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.funeral-directory.co.uk/guides/writing-a-death-notice-or-obituary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: funeral-directory.co.uk">[Funeral Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">funeral-directory.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">writing a death notice or obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Funeral DirectoryHow to Write a Death Notice or Obituary &#124; NAFD Guide 2026 &#124; NAFD Funeral DirectoryMarch 17, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2026</span></span></span>

That distinction becomes especially important when a researcher already has an aura of secrecy around their work. A terse notice can be transformed retrospectively into a clue: *Why did they not say what killed this person?* But the inference reverses the burden of proof. Silence establishes only that the obituary does not supply the answer. The appropriate next step is to look for records created specifically to establish cause and manner of death — death certification, coroner or medical-examiner findings, police records and reliable contemporary reporting — rather than treating the obituary's silence as a substitute for them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/writing-cause-of-death-statements.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NVSS - Writing Cause-of-Death Statements...</span></span></span>

## Why obituaries often omit cause of death

The first problem with interpreting obituary silence is that disclosure is not the normal function of an obituary. FamilySearch describes obituaries as brief biographies that can contain dates, relatives, biographical information, burial details and funeral arrangements; the amount of information varies substantially.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/find-obituaries-on-familysearch" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: familysearch.org">[FamilySearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">familysearch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Family Search Find obituaries on Family Search • Family Search</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Find obituaries on FamilySearch • FamilySearchJuly 13, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 13, 2026</span></span></span> UK funeral guidance similarly says there is no obligation to include a cause of death and notes that families may instead use phrases such as “after a short illness” or simply say that someone died peacefully.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.funeral-directory.co.uk/guides/writing-a-death-notice-or-obituary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: funeral-directory.co.uk">[Funeral Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">funeral-directory.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">writing a death notice or obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Funeral DirectoryHow to Write a Death Notice or Obituary &#124; NAFD Guide 2026 &#124; NAFD Funeral DirectoryMarch 17, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2026</span></span></span>

There are several ordinary reasons for silence. A family may regard medical information as private. The circumstances may be painful or sensitive. The cause may not yet have been established when the notice is written. Or the writers may simply regard an explanation of death as irrelevant to a text intended to commemorate the person's life and announce funeral arrangements. Guidance on obituary writing explicitly identifies privacy, uncertainty and sensitivity as reasons to omit the cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://officialobituary.com/blog/how-to-write-a-short-obituary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: officialobituary.com">[Official Obituary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">officialobituary.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Official Obituary How to Write a Short Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Official ObituaryHow to Write a Short ObituaryMay 28, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2026</span></span></span>

Timing is particularly easy to overlook. An obituary can appear shortly after death, while a death requiring investigation may take longer to resolve. In England and Wales, for example, a coroner may investigate when the cause remains unknown or the death may have been violent or unnatural.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/when-a-death-is-reported-to-a-coroner" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">What to do after someone dies: When a death is reported to a coroner</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What to do after someone dies: When a death is reported to a coroner</span></span></span> The public memorial notice and the official determination therefore operate on different schedules as well as serving different purposes.

The distinction is even clearer when an obituary is compared with an actual [death certificate]({{ 'death-certificate/' | relative_url }}). In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's death-certification guidance requires the certifier — typically a physician, medical examiner or coroner, depending on jurisdiction — to determine and report the causes of death on the death certificate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/writing-cause-of-death-statements.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NVSS - Writing Cause-of-Death Statements...</span></span></span> In England and Wales, medical cause-of-death certification likewise produces the information used in the legal registration of death; a certified copy of the register entry reproduces the cause supplied through that process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-certificate-of-cause-of-death-mccd-guidance-for-medical-practitioners/guidance-for-medical-practitioners-completing-medical-certificates-of-cause-of-death-in-england-and-wales" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gov.uk.</span></span></span>

An obituary has no equivalent forensic obligation. Expecting it to contain everything known officially about a death therefore mistakes one kind of document for another.

## How silence turns into a suspicious-death claim

In disputed [researcher-death]({{ 'missing-denominator/' | relative_url }}) narratives, obituary silence can acquire significance through a simple chain of inference. A researcher worked on an unusual or allegedly sensitive subject; the researcher died; the obituary does not explain the death; therefore, the omission is treated as another instance of secrecy surrounding the research.

The difficulty is that the final step adds information that is not actually present in the source. “No cause stated” becomes “cause withheld”; “withheld” becomes “deliberately concealed”; and deliberate concealment is then offered as indirect support for homicide, intelligence involvement or suppression. Each transition requires evidence of its own.

The mechanism is especially persuasive when several genuine unknowns are placed next to one another. Classified research, missing technical records, a period out of public view and an obituary without a cause of death can look like mutually reinforcing anomalies. Yet those gaps may have entirely different explanations. A research record can be inaccessible because work was classified, while a cause of death can be absent from an obituary simply because the family did not publish it. One silence does not establish the cause of another.

This also creates a problem of unfalsifiability. If an obituary names a conventional cause, a suspicious-death theory can claim that the stated cause is a cover story; if it names no cause, the omission can be called evidence of suppression. A method that interprets both disclosure and non-disclosure as supporting the same conclusion has stopped allowing the source to test the hypothesis.

The safer evidential rule is narrower: an obituary can support what it actually says. Its silence should normally remain silence until another source fills the gap.

## Ning Li shows how an information gap can shrink

Physicist Ning Li provides an unusually clear example within antigravity-related narratives. Li had published research concerning gravitational effects and superconductors and later became difficult for outsiders to trace. Her long absence from public scientific life generated speculation about what had happened to her. When a funeral-home obituary eventually surfaced, it established that she had died in Huntsville on 27 July 2021 and identified her as a scientist associated with superconductivity and antigravity research. It listed her son, grandchildren and funeral arrangements. It did **not** state a medical cause of death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; CrematoryJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Read alone, that notice leaves a real question unanswered. But it does not say that Li was killed, died unexpectedly or that anyone concealed the circumstances. Its language says that she “passed peacefully away”; beyond that, the cause is simply outside the document's scope.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; CrematoryJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

The important development came from reporting that followed the obituary's family information rather than treating its omission as a clue. In 2023, the *Huntsville Business Journal* located Li's son, George Men, and interviewed him. Men said that in 2014 his mother had been struck by a vehicle while crossing a street on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. According to his account, she suffered serious brain injury, subsequently developed Alzheimer's disease and required years of care before her death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

That does not mean every question about Li's later research is resolved. The same reporting describes secrecy surrounding work that Men said involved a top-secret clearance, while attempts to obtain information concerning the research had encountered government restrictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span> But this illustrates precisely why different unknowns should not be merged. Unanswered questions about what Li researched are not evidence that the circumstances of her death were correspondingly mysterious.

The obituary's silence was therefore useful mainly as a prompt for further investigation. It was not itself evidence explaining what happened.

## James McDonald shows the limits of a professional obituary

The case of atmospheric physicist and prominent UFO advocate James E. McDonald demonstrates the same mechanism from a different direction. His 1971 professional obituary in the *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society* records his death on 13 June 1971 and gives extensive information about his education, wartime service and University of Arizona career. The available text does not explain how he died.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/JEMcDonald/mcdonald_obituary_bams_71.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirkmcd.princeton.edu">[Kirk McDonald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirkmcd.princeton.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kirk Mc Donald SHG</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kirk Mc Donald SHG</span></span></span>

If that memorial were the only document consulted, “cause not stated” would be the correct conclusion. It would not be legitimate to convert the omission into evidence that McDonald's death was unexplained.

Other sources address the circumstances directly. An *Arizona Daily Star* archival retrospective reports that McDonald's body was found near a bridge over the Cañada del Oro wash, that a revolver was beside him and that his death was ruled suicide. It also reports an earlier suicide attempt and says that he left notes on both occasions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span></span></span>

The contrast is instructive because the two sources are not genuinely contradictory. The professional obituary was describing McDonald's scientific life and career; reporting on his death addressed circumstances that the memorial did not. The absence of those details from the professional notice cannot reasonably outweigh a separate evidential record dealing directly with the death.

McDonald's case therefore demonstrates a useful rule for disputed researcher deaths: **the specificity of the question should determine the specificity of the source**. A professional obituary may be excellent evidence for employment, scientific standing and date of death while being almost useless for determining manner of death.

## Which records can actually resolve the question?

Once an obituary has been exhausted, stronger evidence comes from records whose purpose includes establishing what caused the death. The precise system differs by jurisdiction, but the hierarchy is conceptually straightforward.

**Death certificates and medical certification** are fundamentally different from memorial notices. US death certificates contain a medical certification section for diseases, injuries, poisoning and, where applicable, circumstances of accident or violence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nvss/manuals/2025/2a-2025.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2a 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Instructions for Classifying the Underlying Cause-of-Death, ICD-10, 2025...</span></span></span> In England and Wales, the medical certificate of cause of death is part of the formal registration process, with medical-examiner scrutiny applying to non-coronial deaths under reforms introduced in September 2024.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-certificate-of-cause-of-death-mccd-guidance-for-medical-practitioners" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Medical certificate of cause of death (MCCD): guidance for medical practitioners</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Medical certificate of cause of death (MCCD): guidance for medical practitioners</span></span></span>

**Coroner or medical-examiner findings** matter particularly where homicide, suicide, accident or another unnatural death is alleged. In England and Wales, a coroner must hold an inquest in circumstances including an unknown cause or a possible violent or unnatural death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/when-a-death-is-reported-to-a-coroner" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">What to do after someone dies: When a death is reported to a coroner</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What to do after someone dies: When a death is reported to a coroner</span></span></span> Similar functions are performed by coroners and medical examiners under varying US state and local systems. The existence, absence or result of such an investigation is much more probative than whether a funeral notice explained the death.

**Police and court records** can address allegations of foul play. If a suspicious-death narrative proposes an assault, poisoning, staged accident or homicide, the relevant questions concern whether there was an investigation, what physical or testimonial evidence existed and what investigators concluded. An obituary cannot answer those questions.

**Contemporary reporting** can bridge gaps when primary records are inaccessible, particularly if journalists quote investigators, relatives, witnesses or official documents. The McDonald example illustrates the value of local archival reporting; the Li example shows how a later interview with a close family member can supply information absent from the funeral notice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tucson.com/news/blogs/morgue-tales/tales-from-the-morgue-ufos-are-real/article_6593476e-745e-11e1-838d-001871e3ce6c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tucson.com">[Arizona Daily Star]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tucson.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arizona Daily Star Tales from the Morgue: UFOs are real</span></span></span> Such reporting still needs to be weighed according to its sources rather than treated as equivalent to a death certificate or investigative finding.

Access also varies. In England and Wales, copies of registered death certificates can be ordered through the General Register Office, and government guidance confirms that the certificate contains the registered cause-of-death information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/order-copy-birth-death-marriage-certificate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Order a birth, death, marriage or civil partnership certificate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order a birth, death, marriage or civil partnership certificate</span></span></span> In the United States, availability of death certificates, autopsy reports and investigative files depends considerably on state and local law. That practical difficulty can leave genuine gaps, but inability to obtain a record is still different from evidence that the record contains something sinister.

## When an omission really would become significant

None of this means obituary omissions can never matter. They can become evidentially interesting when combined with independent evidence showing that the omission was deliberate and unusual in a relevant way.

For example, the assessment would change if documentary evidence showed that an obituary originally contained a cause and somebody ordered its removal; if a family publicly said authorities had forbidden them to disclose the circumstances; if multiple independent official records contradicted the public notice; or if a death officially remained unexplained after a competent investigation. Those would be affirmative facts requiring explanation.

Even then, the inference must remain proportionate. Evidence that information was withheld would establish withholding; it would not automatically establish murder. Evidence of a disputed medical finding would establish a dispute; it would not automatically identify who caused the death. The farther the proposed conclusion moves from the documented fact, the more independent evidence it needs.

This is why the distinction between **absence of evidence** and **evidence of suppression** is central to evaluating alleged suspicious deaths among [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) researchers. An obituary without a cause of death creates a question mark. It does not determine what belongs inside it.

## The practical evidential test

For a disputed researcher death, the most useful way to read obituary silence is to separate three propositions that are often inadvertently collapsed into one:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **The obituary does not state a cause of death.** This can usually be established directly from the notice.
2. **The cause of death was deliberately withheld from the obituary.** This requires additional evidence about the author's or family's decision.
3. **The cause was withheld because the death involved foul play or suppression connected to the researcher's work.** This requires substantially stronger independent evidence linking the omission, the death and the research.

</div>

Only the first proposition follows from silence alone.

That discipline matters because unusual researchers often leave unusual documentary trails. [Classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}), abandoned companies, discontinued publications, institutional disputes and speculative claims about UFOs or unconventional propulsion can make an otherwise ordinary obituary look more mysterious in retrospect. But the evidential standard should move in the opposite direction: the more consequential the allegation, the less weight should be placed on what a memorial notice happens not to say.

Ning Li's obituary demonstrates how a missing explanation can later be supplemented by family testimony; James McDonald's professional obituary demonstrates how a memorial can omit circumstances already addressed elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[berryhillfh.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; CrematoryJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span> In both situations, the decisive evidence lies outside the obituary. That is the central limitation of obituary silence in suspicious-death claims: it identifies something still to investigate, not a conclusion already proved.
