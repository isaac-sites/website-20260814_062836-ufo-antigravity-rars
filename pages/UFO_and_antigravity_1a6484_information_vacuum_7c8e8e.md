## Introduction

In stories about [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) researchers, the word “disappeared” can conceal a much weaker underlying fact: a person stopped publishing papers, stopped giving interviews or became difficult for outsiders to contact. Those are genuine information gaps, but they are not equivalent to a missing-person case. The difference matters because once a researcher is already associated with secretive technology, military funding or disputed physics, ordinary public silence can be reinterpreted as evidence that someone deliberately removed them.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/UFO_and_antigravity_1a6484_information_vacuum_7c8e8e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Info Vacuums" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

Physicist Ning Li is one of the clearest examples. Her peer-reviewed work on superconductors and gravity was real, her company really received US military funding, and her public scientific output then became sparse. Yet later reporting found that she had not vanished in the ordinary sense at all: she remained in Huntsville, continued working, suffered disabling injuries in a 2014 road accident and died there in 2021. The “disappearance” arose largely inside the gap between what outsiders could see and what was actually happening in her life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aps.org">[aps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PhysRevB.46.5489</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Physical Review JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. ## Why abrupt silence attracts suspicion

A researcher who gradually retires from an ordinary field rarely becomes the subject of disappearance rumours. The situation changes when three conditions occur together: the work sounds revolutionary, government or defence institutions appear in the paper trail, and the public record suddenly becomes thin.

Li's career contained all three ingredients. During the 1990s she published technical work with Douglas Torr on possible gravitational effects in superconductors. One 1992 paper appeared in *Physical Review B*. A later 1997 experiment, co-authored with researchers associated with the University of Alabama in Huntsville and NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, tested claimed gravitational anomalies around superconductors. Importantly, that experiment did not report a dramatic [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) success: its measurements placed new limits on a proposed static gravity-superconductor effect. The historical record is therefore more complicated than later summaries suggesting that Li simply “discovered antigravity”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aps.org">[Physical Review Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PhysRevB.46.5489</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Physical Review JournalsGravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. BSeptember 1, 1992</span></span></span>

The public trajectory nevertheless became unusually easy to narrate as a mystery. Li left the University of Alabama in Huntsville and established AC Gravity. A US Department of Defense annual-report entry recorded a $448,970 Army Aviation and Missile Command agreement for a “Gravito-Electro Magnetic Superconductivity Experiment”, running from April 2001 to September 2002. Later accounts could therefore put two accurate facts side by side — military money and disappearing publications — and allow the reader to infer a third, much less certain proposition: that secrecy had swallowed the scientist herself. Contemporary reproductions and archived references preserve the agreement number, dates and amount even though the original departmental document has become difficult to access on the current web.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.projectavalon.net/forum/showthread.php?t=1785" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Government award records">[projectavalon.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Government award records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on projectavalon.net.</span></span></span>

That is the crucial mechanism. **Absence of new public work is evidence about the public record, not automatically evidence about the person's physical whereabouts.** A scientist can stop publishing because of commercial restrictions, classified employment, retirement, illness, family responsibilities, loss of funding, a change of research direction or simply a decision to withdraw from publicity. Unless independent evidence establishes that relatives, employers or authorities also cannot locate the person, “disappeared from the literature” and “disappeared” should remain separate claims.

## Ning Li and the escalation from silence to disappearance

Li's case shows how the wording changed as the information vacuum lengthened. A 2003 MITRE conference paper attributed to Li and James Bradas, “Measurability of AC Gravity Fields”, indicates that she remained professionally active after the military agreement. In other words, the beginning of the supposed disappearance was not a clean moment at which she abruptly ceased to exist publicly; identifiable traces continued after the transition away from conventional academic publishing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/sci.space/c/RKfc3i7Xc1g" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RKfc3i7Xc1g</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsMitre Corporation Conference related to Exotic WMDJune 10, 2003</span></span></span>

By 2004, however, people interested in unconventional propulsion were already trying to track her down. Reporting later reconstructed correspondence in which attempts to reach Li by email produced no reply. Eugene [Podkletnov]({{ 'podkletnov/' | relative_url }}) reportedly told an intermediary that she was alive and still working with the Department of Defense but unable to discuss that work, while also conceding that he no longer possessed working contact details for her. This is exactly the sort of ambiguous evidence from which an information vacuum grows: someone cannot reach a researcher, but another person says she is alive; neither circumstance independently establishes where she is or why she is silent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Corporate records">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Corporate records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

A more dramatic claim appeared in 2008, when physicist Jack Sarfatti stated that Li had disappeared and apparently returned to China. That claim supplied the vacuum with a story: Chinese-born scientist, antigravity research, Pentagon interest, sudden silence and alleged return to China. Later reporting based on an interview with Li's son, George Guangyu Men, contradicted the central point. Men said his mother had remained in the United States and had rejected efforts to persuade her to return to China.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Corporate records">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Corporate records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

The distinction between those stages is important:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The publication trail became sparse:** well supported.
* **Outsiders had difficulty contacting Li:** supported by contemporary correspondence described in later reporting.
* **Her work involved the US defence establishment:** supported by the documented military agreement.
* **She had physically vanished:** not established.
* **She had defected or secretly returned to China:** contradicted by her family's later account.
* **Her silence proves that successful antigravity technology was suppressed:** unsupported by the available public evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.projectavalon.net/forum/showthread.php?t=1785" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Government award records">[projectavalon.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Government award records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on projectavalon.net.</span></span></span>

</div>

The narrative nevertheless became stronger than the evidence because each uncertainty appeared to reinforce the next. Lack of publications made government involvement look more secretive; government involvement made unanswered messages look more ominous; unanswered messages made an unverified China story sound plausible; and repeated retellings could then cite the existence of the “mystery” itself as though it were additional evidence.

## The private life missing from the public story

The most consequential correction arrived only after Li's death. Berryhill Funeral Home published an obituary recording that she died in Huntsville on 27 July 2021 and listing her son and grandchildren. Whatever remained unknown about her research, this basic record was incompatible with the idea that she had permanently vanished into China or disappeared without trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral HomeNing Li Obituary July 27, 2021 - Berryhill Funeral Home &amp; Crematory...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

In 2023, the *Huntsville Business Journal* located and interviewed George Men. He said his mother had continued working in the United States and described her post-university work as classified. The journalist also reported filing Freedom of Information Act requests concerning Li's military-funded work and receiving denials. Those details should be characterised carefully: the son's account is strong evidence about his mother's personal whereabouts and family circumstances, while claims about the precise classification status and content of her later research remain substantially less transparent to outsiders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Corporate records">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Corporate records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

The same interview supplied an ordinary but tragic explanation for Li's final withdrawal from professional life. According to Men, she was struck by a vehicle while crossing a street on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus in 2014. The accident caused permanent brain damage; she never returned to work, and her son cared for her for the following years. The account says her husband witnessed the collision, suffered a heart attack and died the next year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Corporate records">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Corporate records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

Seen chronologically, the contrast is striking. Internet audiences encountered a scientist whose papers seemed to stop and whose work had touched military funding. Her family experienced something completely different: a woman living locally, working outside public view, and later becoming severely disabled. The information vacuum existed because those private facts were not part of the searchable public narrative, not because no facts existed.

## How an information vacuum becomes self-reinforcing

Once a disappearance story has taken hold, later searches can unintentionally strengthen it. Search engines and discussion forums preferentially surface pages that explicitly ask “Where did she go?” or describe someone as “missing”. Ordinary evidence — a local address, a company registration, family life, medical incapacity — may be harder to locate because it was never written for a national audience.

Li's case demonstrates several recurring distortions.

**Publication silence becomes personal silence.** A bibliographic database can show that a scientist has stopped publishing. It cannot show that the scientist has stopped speaking to family, neighbours, colleagues or employers.

**Restricted information becomes evidence of a successful breakthrough.** A military agreement can explain why a project attracts [secrecy claims]({{ 'secrecy-claims/' | relative_url }}), but the existence of defence funding does not establish that the research succeeded. Li's earlier published experimental work actually included a stringent null result for the static gravitational effect being tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921453497014627" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Static test for a gravitational force coupled to type II YBCO superconductors - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

**Unreturned contact attempts become proof of disappearance.** They show only that the particular correspondent could not establish communication. Email addresses expire, professional relationships end and people decline unsolicited contact.

**Hearsay fills the missing chronology.** Sarfatti's claim that Li had returned to China gave the story a concrete destination at precisely the point where documentary evidence was weak. Once repeated, a claim of that sort can be mistaken for an independent fact even when later first-hand testimony contradicts it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Corporate records">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Corporate records</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on huntsvillebusinessjournal.com.</span></span></span>

**Later records are treated as mysterious “reappearances”.** An obituary discovered years after speculation began can be framed as though the person suddenly resurfaced. In reality, an obituary usually establishes something much narrower: the individual had an ordinary enough social existence for relatives and a funeral home to document their death.

This feedback loop helps explain why alleged disappearances in UFO and antigravity research can remain compelling even after their literal premise weakens. The mystery migrates. First the question is “Where is the scientist?” Once that is answered, it becomes “What was the scientist doing?” If some work remains unavailable, the unresolved second question can preserve the emotional force of the original disappearance narrative even though the disappearance itself has largely dissolved.

## What can actually resolve an information vacuum?

A responsible assessment works outward from records closest to the person rather than treating the absence of internet material as the starting proof. No single record is decisive in every case, but several independent forms of evidence can sharply distinguish public silence from genuine disappearance.

The highest-value checks are often mundane:

1. **Publication and conference records** establish when public scientific activity actually declined. They can also reveal appearances after the date someone was supposedly “last seen”. Li's 2003 conference contribution is significant for exactly that reason. [Google Groups](https://groups.google.com/g/sci.space/c/RKfc3i7Xc1g)

2. **Government award records** can verify that defence involvement existed without assuming what happened after the award. In Li's case, the Army agreement is a documented fact; the success or ultimate fate of the research is a different question. [projectavalon.net](https://www.projectavalon.net/forum/showthread.php?t=1785)

3. **Corporate records** can show whether a research company continued to exist after its founder allegedly vanished. Later reporting found that AC Gravity's registration continued to be renewed for years, which is inconsistent with a simple narrative of instantaneous disappearance even though it reveals little about actual research activity. [Huntsville Business Journal](https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/)

5. **Police, court, medical, probate or contemporaneous local reporting**, where legally and ethically accessible, can test dramatic claims about [accidents]({{ 'accidents/' | relative_url }}), deaths or missing-person status. Their absence should not itself be treated as proof of concealment, but their presence can decisively change the factual picture.
6. **Freedom of Information responses** can clarify what an agency will or will not release, although a denial is not proof that the withheld material validates the theory motivating the request. Classification can conceal mundane, unsuccessful and strategically sensitive work just as readily as extraordinary results.

The evidential threshold should also match the strength of the claim. Showing that somebody ceased publishing requires only a reliable publication chronology. Calling that person “uncontactable” requires evidence from people actually attempting contact. Calling them “missing” requires considerably more: credible evidence that people reasonably expected to know their whereabouts could not locate them. Claiming they were abducted, killed or deliberately erased requires stronger evidence still.

## Silence is a clue, not a conclusion

Ning Li remains an interesting figure precisely because part of her professional history is still difficult to reconstruct. Her peer-reviewed research is documented; US military funding is documented; later public output is sparse; and important details about subsequent work remain unavailable. None of those facts needs to be minimised. [Physical Review Journals+2ScienceDirect](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.5489)

But the case also demonstrates why information gaps must not be allowed to change grammatical form unnoticed. “There are few public records of what Li was doing” became “nobody knew where Li was”, which became “Li disappeared”, which in some retellings became a premise for theories of defection, suppression or secret antigravity breakthroughs. The 2021 obituary and the 2023 interview with her son substantially collapse the literal disappearance claim while leaving narrower questions about her later professional work unresolved. [Berryhill Funeral Home](https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6)

For alleged UFO and antigravity researcher disappearances more broadly, that distinction is the central safeguard. A gap in the archive is worth investigating. An unanswered email is worth noting. A classified contract can legitimately limit what outsiders know. But until evidence moves from missing information about a person to evidence that the person themselves was missing, public silence should be described for what it is: an information vacuum, not a disappearance.

    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();

        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
