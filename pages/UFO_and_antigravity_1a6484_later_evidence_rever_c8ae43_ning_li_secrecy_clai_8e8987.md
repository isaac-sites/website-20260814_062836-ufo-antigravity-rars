## Introduction

Ning Li's later secrecy is one of the strongest parts of her story, but also one of the easiest to overinterpret. Her son, George Guangyu Men, told the *Huntsville Business Journal* in 2023 that Li obtained a top-secret security clearance and consequently stopped discussing or publishing her work. The same investigation reported that requests under the US Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) for records of her Department of Defense-funded research had been denied. Those details provide a plausible explanation for why a previously publishing scientist largely disappeared from the open literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

What they do **not** establish is that Li successfully created [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) technology, that such a breakthrough was hidden, or that secrecy surrounding her work explains suspicious deaths or disappearances. The distinction matters because later revelations have overturned one part of the Ning Li legend — she had not literally vanished — while leaving another part, the content of her post-academic research, genuinely unresolved. The public evidence supports an information gap. It does not tell us what belongs inside that gap.

## The son's claim of a top-secret clearance

The most important evidence about Li's secrecy comes from Men, interviewed by Huntsville journalist Noah Logan in 2023. Men described a marked change after his mother moved from university research into defence-related work. At university, he said, she had enjoyed publishing her findings. After receiving what he described as a top-secret clearance, however, she could no longer share her work and became noticeably more guarded. When he once tried asking her directly about it, her response was essentially that he knew nothing and should forget anything he thought he knew.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

That testimony deserves weight because Men had direct access to Li's private life that internet investigators did not. It also fits several independently visible features of the chronology. Li's earlier research was openly published: she and Douglas Torr produced a 1991 *Physical Review D* paper on gravitomagnetic effects in superconductors, while Li and NASA-associated collaborators published experimental work in *Physica C* in 1997. NASA's technical archive still catalogues the latter paper as publicly distributed research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.43.457" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Phys Rev D.43.457</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsEffects of a gravitomagnetic field on pure superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. DJanuary 15, 1991...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 15, 1991</span></span></span>

The public trail then changed character. AC Gravity received $448,970 from US Army Aviation and Missile Command in 2001 for a "Gravito-Electro Magnetic Superconductivity Experiment". The surviving description says that the effort was intended to provide a first experimental test of predictions associated with Li's theory. The project ran from April 2001 to September 2002, but its technical results did not subsequently appear in the open scientific literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

The *Huntsville Business Journal* found indications that Li nevertheless remained professionally active. It reported evidence of a 2003 MITRE conference presentation entitled "Measurability of AC Gravity Fields", given in conjunction with an official associated with US Army Aviation and Missile Command. Men further said that Li continued working at Redstone Arsenal until the road accident that incapacitated her in 2014. Taken together, these details make "she stopped doing research" a poor explanation for her disappearance from scientific publishing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

There is nevertheless an evidential limitation that should remain explicit. The public evidence located for the clearance itself is **Men's testimony**, not a released security-clearance document identifying its level, date and programme. Security-clearance records are not normally the sort of material from which outsiders can reconstruct a classified career in detail. The son's account is therefore significant testimony consistent with the known chronology, but it should not be silently upgraded into documentary proof of every claim subsequently attached to Li's secret work.

## What the missing project records actually tell us

The second important strand is the difficulty of obtaining the Army-funded project's records. A publicly viewable 2022 FOIA request sought paperwork relating specifically to AC Gravity's 2001 grant and noted that the research results had never been made public. Separate requests were directed to multiple Department of Defense components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/department-of-defense-grant-for-ac-gravity-llc-2001-department-of-defense-under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-and-security-135082/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Defense Grant for AC Gravity LLC (2001) (Department of Defense, Under Secretary Of Defense For Intelligence And Sec...</span></span></span>

There is also an official documentary trace showing that interest in these records was real rather than retrospective folklore. A Department of Defense Office of Inspector General FOIA log records a July 2022 request for declassified material concerning AC Gravity and its gravity grant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2025/Mar/20/2003673003/-1/-1/1/FOIA%20CASE%20LOG%20FISCAL%20YEAR%202022%20%28REDACTED%29.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOIA CASE LOG FISCAL YEAR 2022 (REDACTED</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarFreedom of Information Act Case Log Fiscal Year 2022 (Redacted)April 10, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 10, 2023</span></span></span>

Logan reported in 2023 that his own FOIA requests for the results of the 2001 grant "came back denied", which he said was consistent with earlier attempts. This strengthens the case that researchers encountered an actual barrier to obtaining the material rather than simply failing to search for it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

But a denied or unfulfilled records request needs careful interpretation. FOIA outcomes are evidence about **access to records**, not experimental outcomes. Without the underlying agency response identifying exactly what was located and the statutory basis on which each document was withheld, "FOIA denial" cannot safely be translated into "the experiment succeeded and therefore had to be classified". Records can be withheld, transferred, unavailable, outside the receiving office's holdings, covered by exemptions or otherwise inaccessible for reasons that say little about whether the underlying science worked.

That distinction becomes particularly important in Li's case because the original project description was explicitly experimental. The Army was paying to find out whether the predicted phenomenon could be demonstrated. Funding an experiment is not the same thing as validating its hypothesis. The surviving project description even framed the research as a test capable of determining whether this line of investigation merited further work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

The correct evidential chain is therefore modest but meaningful: **the Army funded a serious test; the resulting technical record is not publicly available; Li ceased normal open publication; her son attributed that silence to [classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}); and later attempts to obtain records encountered access barriers.** None of those propositions requires an antigravity breakthrough to be true.

## Secrecy cannot substitute for experimental evidence

The temptation to infer success from secrecy is especially strong because Li's earlier research involved extraordinary possibilities. Her theoretical work examined proposed relationships between superconductivity and gravitomagnetism, and the later Army project was explicitly interested in whether significant gravitational forces might be generated experimentally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.43.457" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.aps.org">[APS Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Phys Rev D.43.457</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APS JournalsEffects of a gravitomagnetic field on pure superconductors &#124; Phys. Rev. DJanuary 15, 1991...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 15, 1991</span></span></span>

Yet Li's last readily accessible peer-reviewed experimental evidence points towards caution rather than a demonstrated breakthrough. In the 1997 *Physica C* experiment, Li and colleagues tested stationary type-II YBCO superconductors with a sensitive gravimeter. They measured changes in acceleration of less than two parts in 100 million of normal gravitational acceleration. The authors presented that result as a new limit on the proposed coupling between static superconductors and gravity — not as evidence of usable antigravity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921453497014627" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sciencedirect.com.</span></span></span>

This produces an important asymmetry in the historical record. The public can examine the earlier null or limiting result because its method and conclusion were published. For the later defence-funded work, the public cannot perform the same evaluation because the relevant technical results have not been released. That makes the later period **less knowable**, not automatically **more successful**.

One intriguing fragment illustrates the problem. Logan reported a May 2003 private email in which Li referred to an "11 kilowatts of output effect". Without the apparatus description, measurement protocol, definition of "output", calibration information, controls, error analysis and independent replication, however, that isolated number cannot establish what physical phenomenon was being measured. It certainly cannot by itself demonstrate gravity control or a propulsion system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

Scientific proof normally becomes stronger as experimental details, measurements and replication accumulate. In conspiracy-oriented retellings of classified research, the evidential logic can accidentally run backwards: the fewer data that are available, the more revolutionary the missing data are imagined to be. Ning Li's case is a particularly clear example of why that reasoning is unsafe.

## Where reasonable inference ends

The available evidence supports several conclusions with different levels of confidence. It is reasonable to conclude that Li did not simply abandon her research when her publications stopped. The Army award is documented, evidence places her research activity beyond the end of the grant, and her son described years of defence work at Redstone Arsenal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[ZPEnergy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

It is also reasonable to regard classification or other defence secrecy as a credible explanation for at least some of the public silence. Men's account directly says so, while the inaccessible project results and abrupt contrast between Li's earlier publishing career and later silence fit that explanation. That conclusion resolves an important historical question: the absence of papers after 2002 need not mean that Li herself had disappeared.

The evidence becomes much weaker when the claim changes from **"Li did classified research"** to **"Li's research succeeded."** Nothing in a security clearance demonstrates experimental validity. Nothing about an unpublished defence contract proves that its technical objective was achieved. And a refusal or failure to release records does not disclose what those records would have said.

The further claim that the government concealed a functioning antigravity technology requires another evidential leap. To establish that proposition convincingly would require material such as released technical reports describing a repeatable anomalous force, independently assessable experimental data, authenticated documentation of a working device, corroborated testimony from technically knowledgeable participants, or later technology demonstrably derived from Li's work. The publicly accessible record reviewed here supplies none of those things.

Nor does Li's secrecy provide evidence that her eventual incapacity or death was connected to suppression of her research. According to Men, she remained in Huntsville and continued working at Redstone Arsenal until 2014, when she was struck by a vehicle while crossing a street on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. The accident caused severe brain damage; she subsequently developed Alzheimer's disease and died in 2021 after years in her son's care. That later family account undermines rather than strengthens the older picture of a scientist who mysteriously vanished after discovering something dangerous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

## The revelation changes the mystery rather than solving the science

This is where Ning Li's case becomes particularly useful for understanding how later revelations can overturn entrenched [UFO and antigravity]({{ 'ufo-and-antigravity/' | relative_url }}) lore. Before Men spoke publicly, outsiders were trying to explain two absences at once: **where was Ning Li, and where were her research results?** With little reliable information about either, the two questions became fused. Her missing publications helped make the scientist herself seem "missing", while her supposed disappearance made the unpublished research seem more ominous.

The 2023 account separates those questions. Li's personal whereabouts are no longer much of a mystery: according to her son, she remained in Huntsville, continued defence-related work and was later incapacitated by an ordinary but catastrophic road accident. An earlier internet claim that she had disappeared or returned to China therefore cannot be treated as an established fact merely because it circulated for years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business JournalJuly 30...</span></span></span>

The research question is different. There **is** a genuine gap in the public record surrounding AC Gravity's later work. The documented Army award, disappearance of open publications, reported security clearance, later defence employment and difficulty obtaining project records make that gap worthy of historical interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

But the gap has boundaries. It can support the proposition that some information about Li's later work was unavailable to the public. It may support the inference that classification contributed to that absence. It cannot tell us, without additional evidence, whether the withheld work produced a null result, an interesting anomaly, a defence-relevant but conventional finding, an unresolved experiment or the revolutionary technology later stories imagine.

That is the central lesson of Ning Li's secrecy claims. **Secrecy can explain why evidence is missing; it cannot become the missing evidence.** Later testimony makes the history of her public silence substantially more intelligible, while simultaneously giving less reason to describe her as a mysteriously vanished scientist. What remains unresolved is narrower, and more defensible: what exactly did Li and AC Gravity find during their inaccessible defence-funded work? Until technical records capable of answering that question emerge, classification is evidence of an information barrier — not proof of antigravity behind it.
