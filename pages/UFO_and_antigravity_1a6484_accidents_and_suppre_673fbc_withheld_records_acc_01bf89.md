## Introduction

Withheld records can make an already unusual story feel more suspicious, but in Ning Li’s case the public-record trail does not establish that secrecy surrounding her defence-funded gravity research had anything to do with the traffic accident that ended her career. The strongest conclusion is narrower: there is a genuine information gap around what AC Gravity produced for the US Department of Defense, while the evidence publicly available about Li’s 2014 accident does not demonstrate targeted harm.

## What the unreleased defence material leaves unknown

There is good evidence that Li’s company, AC Gravity LLC, received US defence funding. Contemporary descriptions identify an agreement numbered DAAH01-01-9-R001, titled “Gravito - Electro Magnetic Superconductivity Experiment”, awarded through the US Army Aviation and Missile Command. The reported government contribution was $448,970, with an effective date of 25 April 2001 and completion scheduled for 25 September 2002. Its stated purpose was experimental testing of predictions arising from Li’s ideas about superconductors and gravitational effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZP Energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

What is conspicuously absent from the public scientific literature is an ordinary published account of what that project ultimately found. The 2023 Huntsville Business Journal investigation reported that Li produced no further published papers after 2002, although evidence placed her at a 2003 gravitational-wave conference discussing the “Measurability of AC Gravity Fields”. The same investigation, based substantially on an interview with her son George Men, reported that Li subsequently held a top-secret clearance and continued defence work around Redstone Arsenal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

Those circumstances create legitimate unanswered questions. Did the funded experiment produce a final technical report? Were any results negative, inconclusive or promising? Which organisation ultimately retained the experimental data? Were later results classified, restricted for another reason, or simply never turned into a conventional publication? Publicly available material does not resolve those questions.

There is also evidence that researchers have tried to use FOIA to reconstruct this history. A Defense Technical Information Center FOIA log records a 2019 request for a search on “Ning Li or AC Gravity, LLC” and says material was “Sent to requester”. That entry is significant because it shows that the records story is not simply one of an impenetrable wall: at least one request was processed with something sent back, although the log alone does not tell us what was supplied or whether it included substantive project results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.governmentattic.org/38docs/DTICfoiaLog_2010-2020.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: governmentattic.org">[governmentattic.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">governmentattic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Description of document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

A later, publicly traceable MuckRock request sought “any paperwork” relating to the 2001 AC Gravity award. It was submitted to several Department of Defense components in October 2022. The surviving correspondence shows the case being closed and, in one component’s later explanation, administratively closed after the requester did not respond to a five-day letter. The Department offered to let the requester reformulate the request under a new tracking number.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/department-of-defense-grant-for-ac-gravity-llc-2001-department-of-defense-defense-security-cooperation-agency-135084/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Defense Grant for AC Gravity LLC (2001) (Department of Defense, Defense Security Cooperation Agency) • MuckRockOcto...</span></span></span>

That detail complicates the widespread description of the episode as a straightforward national-security “denial”. A Department of Defense FOIA log lists case 23-F-0043 under an “Other Reasons” disposition associated with records not being reasonably described. In other words, the accessible administrative record does not establish that this particular request located Li’s files and then withheld them under the FOIA exemption for classified national-defence information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/936346104/FOID-FOIA-Log-FY23" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOIA Requests</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FOIA Requests Summary and Details &#124; PDF &#124; Firearms &#124; Freedom Of Information Act (United States)...</span></span></span>

The distinction is not semantic. Under FOIA, a request must “reasonably describe” the records sought before the agency’s disclosure obligations operate in the normal way. Department of Justice guidance explains that the description must allow an employee familiar with the subject to locate responsive records with a reasonable amount of effort; agencies are encouraged to help requesters reformulate deficient requests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/oip/oip-guidance/guidance-agency-foia-regulations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on justice.gov.</span></span></span>

The Huntsville Business Journal nevertheless reported that its own FOIA attempts concerning Li’s research had been denied and described her later work as classified, drawing in part on her son’s account of her security clearance. That is useful evidence that access to the later research has been difficult, but it should not be expanded into the stronger claim that every missing AC Gravity document is known to have been withheld specifically because it contains successful [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

## Why classification can have several explanations

National-security secrecy is compatible with many scenarios, only some of which are dramatic. FOIA itself expressly allows properly classified national-defence and foreign-policy information to be withheld under Exemption 1. Other exemptions can protect trade secrets or confidential commercial information, internal deliberative material, personal information and certain law-enforcement records. The existence of a withholding therefore does not, without the agency’s stated exemption and the underlying record, reveal why a document is sensitive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/oip/freedom-information-act-amended-1974-public-law" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">freedom information act amended 1974 public law</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">freedom information act amended 1974 public law</span></span></span>

More importantly, defence classification is evidence about the sensitivity of information, not evidence that the underlying scientific proposition worked. An experimental programme could generate information considered sensitive because of equipment, military applications, associated programmes or technical capabilities even if its central hypothesis ultimately failed. Conversely, a project can leave a poor public paper trail without being classified at all: records can reside with another component, be inadequately indexed, fall outside a request’s wording, be retained under different programme names, or never have existed in the form a requester expects.

Li’s case therefore contains two claims of very different strength. It is reasonable to say that the public cannot presently reconstruct her later defence work from the material readily available. It is much harder to say why every missing record is missing.

Her family’s account does strengthen the proposition that secrecy became an important part of her professional life. Men told the Huntsville Business Journal that his mother had loved publishing at the university but stopped sharing her work after receiving a top-secret clearance. He also described her as continuing to work at Redstone Arsenal until 2014. Those are important recollections from a close family member, but they are not themselves declassification records identifying the programme, its classification guide, its results or the exemptions applicable to particular documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

The appropriate description is therefore **documented defence involvement plus an unresolved public-record gap**, rather than proven suppression of a successful technology.

## Does the records gap make the 2014 accident suspicious?

This is where the evidentiary standard has to change. According to Men’s account, Li continued working until 2014, when a vehicle struck her as she crossed a street on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. He said the collision caused permanent brain damage and that she subsequently developed Alzheimer’s disease; he cared for her during the final years of her life. Her husband, who witnessed the collision, reportedly suffered a heart attack and died the following year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

Li died on 27 July 2021. Her funeral-home obituary independently establishes her death, her Huntsville connection and her identity as a researcher associated with superconductivity and “anti-gravity”, although it says nothing about the circumstances of the 2014 collision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituary/ning-li?lud=4CF765EE88E7526FCBA619C30101F7E6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berryhill Funeral Home Ning Li Obituary</span></span></span>

The accident story is understandably striking when placed beside the secrecy surrounding her work. A scientist conducts unusual defence-funded research, stops publishing, works for years outside public view and is then catastrophically injured by a vehicle. That sequence supplies the raw material for a suspicious interpretation. It does not supply evidence that the events were causally connected.

The accessible sources located for the accident do not establish the features that would be needed to support such a connection: an identified motive, evidence that Li was being monitored or threatened, suspicious conduct by the driver, evidence that the collision was deliberately engineered, a documented attempt to conceal the crash, or investigative findings linking anyone involved to her research. The detailed public account currently rests principally on her son’s recollections reported in 2023, rather than a publicly available police collision file or court record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

That limitation cuts both ways. It means the open record is not detailed enough to reconstruct every circumstance of the collision independently. But missing detail is not affirmative evidence of assassination or sabotage. The proposition “the complete accident file is not readily available online” is fundamentally different from “the accident was deliberately caused”.

Some later websites explicitly turn the combination of missing research and the collision into a theory of researcher suppression. Their argument is essentially cumulative: Li had sensitive research, the research disappeared from public view, and the accident permanently prevented her from explaining it. Such sources demonstrate that the suspicious-death narrative exists, but they do not provide independent evidence establishing the crucial causal step between the defence programme and the vehicle collision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapmurders.com/uaps/Details/Ning_Li/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapmurders.com">[Murdering UAP Experts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapmurders.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Murdering UAP Experts Ning Li &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Murdering UAP Experts Ning Li &#124; Murdering UAP Experts</span></span></span>

## The gap between secrecy and targeted harm

The most useful way to assess the case is to separate what the evidence supports from what would have to be added by inference.

**The defence-research gap is real.** AC Gravity received documented military funding for an experimental gravity-related project, while a conventional public final report explaining the outcome has not emerged in the material examined here. Li also stopped publishing publicly and, according to her son, worked under security restrictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zpenergy.com/modules.php?file=article&amp;mode=flat&amp;name=News&amp;order=0&amp;sid=838&amp;thold=-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zpenergy.com">[zpenergy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zpenergy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZP Energy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gravity-Waves &amp; Missing Scientists at American Antigravity.ComJuly 19, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 19, 2004</span></span></span>

**The FOIA story does not prove successful research was classified.** The 2022 request most easily inspected in public records encountered procedural and description problems, including an administrative closure and an invitation to reformulate it. FOIA law separately permits properly classified material to be withheld, but the existence of that legal mechanism cannot be treated as proof that it was the reason particular AC Gravity records were unavailable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/department-of-defense-grant-for-ac-gravity-llc-2001-department-of-defense-defense-security-cooperation-agency-135084/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[muckrock.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Defense Grant for AC Gravity LLC (2001) (Department of Defense, Defense Security Cooperation Agency) • MuckRockOcto...</span></span></span>

**The accident had an extraordinary consequence without being demonstrably extraordinary in cause.** The collision reportedly destroyed Li’s ability to continue her scientific work and contributed to years of severe cognitive decline. That consequence makes the accident important to the history of her research. It does not establish that ending her research was the purpose of the collision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huntsville Business JournalUncovering The Mystery Of Huntsville’s Brilliant Anti-gravity Scientist - Huntsville Business Journal...</span></span></span>

**A stronger suspicious-accident claim would require new evidence.** A police report showing unexplained circumstances, witness evidence contradicting an ordinary collision, records identifying the driver and demonstrating a relevant connection, prior documented threats, intelligence or security records linking Li to a protective or coercive operation, or credible investigative findings of deliberate targeting would materially change the assessment. Without evidence of that kind, secrecy about the research and uncertainty about the accident remain two unresolved facts rather than a demonstrated chain of events.

This is the central governance lesson in Ning Li’s case. Government secrecy can create a vacuum in which researchers, journalists and the public cannot readily distinguish failed experiments, sensitive but conventional defence work, successful classified research and mundane records-management problems. That vacuum is a legitimate transparency issue. It also makes causal discipline more important, not less.

The unreleased material leaves a meaningful question — **what happened to the results of Li’s defence-funded work?** The currently available evidence does not answer a much more serious one in the affirmative — **was the 2014 collision arranged because of that work?** Until evidence bridges those two questions, the withheld records make Li’s scientific history incomplete, but they do not make targeted harm an established explanation for her accident.
