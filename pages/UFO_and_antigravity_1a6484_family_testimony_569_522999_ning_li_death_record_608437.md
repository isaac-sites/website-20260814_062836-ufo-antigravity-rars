## Introduction

The strongest documentary anchor for Ning Li’s later-life chronology is not a defence file or a speculative account of her research. It is a much more ordinary set of [local records]({{ 'local-records/' | relative_url }}): a 2021 funeral-home obituary and a contemporaneous Alabama death notice. Together, they establish that Li died on 27 July 2021 while associated with the [Huntsville]({{ 'huntsville/' | relative_url }})–Madison area, identify George Men as her son, name the same grandchildren, and give matching funeral and burial arrangements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

That matters because Li’s long absence from public research had generated claims that she had physically “disappeared”, including a prominent allegation that she had returned to China. The death notices do not reconstruct the missing years or explain her defence-related work. What they do provide is an independently published endpoint to the family chronology later described by Men. The distinction is important: the records corroborate who the family was, where Li’s final public life was rooted and when it ended, without proving every detail of what happened between her last public research activity and her death.

## What the funeral-home notice establishes

Berryhill Funeral Home’s obituary for Ning Li<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[berryhillfh.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span> records her dates as 14 January 1943 to 27 July 2021 and associates her with Madison while describing her as being “of Huntsville, AL”. More importantly for identifying the deceased, the notice does not merely contain the common name Ning Li. It describes her as a scientist associated with superconductivity and anti-gravity research and says that she had constructed a 12-inch high-temperature superconducting disc in the late 1990s. Those details make the identification with the Huntsville physicist unusually strong.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

The obituary also establishes a family link that later became crucial to resolving the disappearance narrative. It names “Dr. George Men” as Li’s surviving son and identifies Vivian YingYing Men and Andy YangYang Men as her grandchildren. The same notice gives concrete funeral arrangements: services through Berryhill Funeral Home on 1 August 2021, visitation that morning, a chapel service officiated by the Rev Tim Cole and burial at Faith Memorial Park.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

Those details give the record greater evidential value than an isolated online statement saying simply that Li died. They tie together a named deceased person, a specific family, a local funeral provider, a service date and a burial destination. They also provide information that predates the 2023 interview in which Men supplied the much fuller account of his mother’s later years.

There is, however, a small internal problem worth preserving rather than smoothing over. The Berryhill page gives Li’s birth date as 14 January 1943 but describes her as 79 when she died on 27 July 2021. Those dates calculate to an age of 78. A separate Alabama notice published by the *Quad Cities Daily* on 29 July 2021 gives her age as 78.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span> The discrepancy looks like an age error in the funeral-home prose rather than evidence of two different people, because the surrounding identifiers agree.

## The Alabama obituary reinforces the chronology

The useful corroboration comes from a second local publication. The *Quad Cities Daily* published a short death notice on 29 July 2021, two days after Li’s reported death. It identifies “Ning Li, 78, of Madison, AL”, gives the same death date of 27 July and names George Men, Vivian Ying Ying Men and Andy Ying Yang Men as her surviving son and grandchildren. It also reproduces the same 1 August visitation, Berryhill funeral service and subsequent burial at Faith Memorial Park.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://quadcitiesdaily.com/ning-li/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quadcitiesdaily.com">[Quad Cities Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quadcitiesdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Quad Cities Daily Ning Li – Quad Cities Daily</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Quad Cities Daily Ning Li – Quad Cities Daily</span></span></span>

The overlap is significant because the records contain several matching data points rather than merely the same name:

The *Quad Cities Daily* notice may ultimately derive its information from the funeral home or family, so it should not be treated as a wholly independent investigation. Its value is narrower: it shows that the death and funeral information entered the local public record contemporaneously in July 2021, rather than first appearing after the disappearance controversy was revisited in 2023.

That timing becomes especially important when compared with the later reporting. In July 2023, the *Huntsville Business Journal* described Berryhill’s obituary as the key verifiable update it found while investigating what had happened to Li. The obituary supplied George Guangyu Men’s name; the reporter subsequently located and interviewed him. The death record was already public in 2021. The journalist found the family through that record in 2023, and the resulting interview supplied additional chronology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

## Why this changes the meaning of “disappeared”

The records do not show that Li remained continuously in Alabama throughout every year after her public scientific career faded from view. They do, however, provide a firm endpoint inconsistent with treating her fate as simply unknown.

The *Huntsville Business Journal* documents the earlier source of the confusion. In 2004, physicist Eugene [Podkletnov]({{ 'podkletnov/' | relative_url }}) reportedly told a correspondent that Li was alive and working with the US Department of Defense but unable to discuss her work. By 2008, physicist Jack Sarfatti was publicly claiming that she had disappeared and returned to China. That latter account became part of the mythology surrounding Li because outsiders had little visible information with which to test it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ning Li passed away on July 27, 2021. She was 79 years old. The obituary described her as “one of the world&#x27;s leading scientists in super...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Against that backdrop, the Alabama death records supply something mundane but powerful: evidence of a normal local end-of-life process. A Huntsville-area funeral home recorded Li’s death, identified her son and grandchildren, scheduled visitation and a chapel service, and named a local burial site. A regional publication carried matching information two days after her death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

The 2023 family interview then fits onto that independently existing framework. The *Huntsville Business Journal* reports that Men said his mother had remained in the United States rather than returning to China. It also reports his account that she was struck by a vehicle on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus in 2014, suffered permanent brain damage and was cared for by him during her final years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ning Li passed away on July 27, 2021. She was 79 years old. The obituary described her as “one of the world&#x27;s leading scientists in super...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

The resulting evidence is stronger than either component by itself. Men provides the narrative connecting the missing years; the 2021 records independently confirm his relationship to Li and the endpoint of that narrative. They therefore make it much harder to sustain the strongest version of the disappearance claim — that Li's physical whereabouts and ultimate fate simply remained unknown.

## What these records cannot verify

The evidential limits are just as important as the corroboration. A funeral-home obituary is a memorial and service record, not a police report, medical record or government investigation. Berryhill does **not** state a medical cause of death, document the circumstances of Li’s 2014 accident or establish whether a driver was identified. It also does not verify Men’s statements about [classified work]({{ 'classified-work/' | relative_url }}), security clearance or approaches from Chinese officials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span>

Nor does the *Quad Cities Daily* notice fill those gaps. Its entry is essentially a brief local death announcement containing family and funeral information. It corroborates the date, Alabama location, relatives and arrangements but offers no independent account of Li’s health or the events preceding her death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://quadcitiesdaily.com/ning-li/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quadcitiesdaily.com">[Quad Cities Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quadcitiesdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Quad Cities Daily Ning Li – Quad Cities Daily</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Quad Cities Daily Ning Li – Quad Cities Daily</span></span></span> Claims elsewhere on the web that assign a specific medical cause or an official ruling therefore should not be attributed to these obituary records unless supported by a separate authoritative document.

The records likewise cannot establish where Li was during every year between her last widely documented research activity and 2021. A Huntsville-area death and funeral do not, by themselves, prove uninterrupted residence there for two decades. The stronger evidence for her intervening life comes from Men’s testimony, while the death records provide corroboration at the end of that chronology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ning Li passed away on July 27, 2021. She was 79 years old. The obituary described her as “one of the world&#x27;s leading scientists in super...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Even the age discrepancy illustrates why these sources should be used precisely. Berryhill says 79, whereas its own displayed birth and death dates imply 78; the contemporaneous regional notice gives 78.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span> That minor inconsistency does not undermine the convergence of the identifying details, but it cautions against treating an obituary as an error-free vital record.

## The records anchor the family timeline, not the research mystery

For assessing claims surrounding allegedly disappeared UFO or [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) researchers, Ning Li’s case demonstrates the importance of separating different questions. Her withdrawal from visible scientific publication is real. The public record of what happened to her research after the early 2000s remains incomplete. Those gaps can legitimately invite historical investigation. They do not, however, make her eventual fate undocumented.

By July 2021, local records placed Ning Li’s death in Alabama, linked her directly to George Men and recorded a conventional funeral and burial process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.berryhillfh.com/obituaries/ning-li" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: berryhillfh.com">[Berryhill Funeral Home]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">berryhillfh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on berryhillfh.com.</span></span></span> Two years later, those records became the bridge by which a Huntsville journalist located Men and obtained the [family testimony]({{ 'family-accounts/' | relative_url }}) that addressed the much older disappearance narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huntsvillebusinessjournal.com/news/2023/07/30/solving-the-mystery-of-huntsvilles-brilliant-scientist-disappearing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huntsvillebusinessjournal.com">[Huntsville Business Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huntsvillebusinessjournal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ning Li passed away on July 27, 2021. She was 79 years old. The obituary described her as “one of the world&#x27;s leading scientists in super...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

That is the narrow but consequential contribution of the death records. They cannot tell readers what Li discovered, what classified work she may have performed, or precisely what caused every event in her later life. They **can** independently anchor the identity, family relationship, Alabama setting and 27 July 2021 endpoint around which the fuller family chronology is built. In a case where an absence from public science had gradually been transformed into a story of physical disappearance, that documentary anchor materially changes what the available evidence supports.
