## Introduction

Eugene Mallove’s death on 14 May 2004 was a homicide. That point is not speculative: Connecticut authorities formally treated his death as murder, and later criminal proceedings developed extensive evidence about an assault at the Norwich rental property he was cleaning. What the homicide finding does **not** establish is why Mallove was killed — and, specifically, it does not show that his advocacy of [cold fusion]({{ 'cold-fusion/' | relative_url }}) or unconventional energy caused his death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.ct.gov/despp/press-releases/2009/50000-reward-being-offered-from-homicide-investigation-norwich-2004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.ct.gov">[CT.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">50000 reward being offered from homicide investigation norwich 2004</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT.gov50000 Reward Being Offered from Homicide Investigation Norwich 2004...</span></span></span>

That distinction is crucial because Mallove was unusually well placed to attract a suppression narrative. He was a former MIT science writer, an outspoken critic of the scientific establishment’s treatment of cold fusion, and an energetic promoter of unconventional-energy research. Yet the strongest surviving investigative and court evidence instead connects the killing to people associated with recently evicted tenants and to a confrontation at the property. Even the later overturning of Mozzelle Brown’s convictions does not convert that evidence gap into proof of a research-related motive: the [2025 ruling]({{ '2025-ruling/' | relative_url }}) concerned the prosecution’s disclosure obligations regarding a cooperating witness, not evidence of scientific suppression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa3.org/archive/asa/200403/0244.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa3.org">[American Scientific Affiliation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa3.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Scientific Affiliation ASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Scientific AffiliationASA - March 2004: Credibility for cold fusion?March 25, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2004</span></span></span>

## What the homicide finding actually establishes

The narrowest firmly established fact is straightforward. A Connecticut State Police notice issued in 2009 states that Mallove was discovered dead in the driveway of 119 Salem Turnpike in Norwich at about 11 p.m. on 14 May 2004 and that his death “was ruled a Homicide”. Police, the New London State’s Attorney’s Office and Connecticut State Police subsequently operated a joint homicide task force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.ct.gov/despp/press-releases/2009/50000-reward-being-offered-from-homicide-investigation-norwich-2004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.ct.gov">[CT.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">50000 reward being offered from homicide investigation norwich 2004</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT.gov50000 Reward Being Offered from Homicide Investigation Norwich 2004...</span></span></span>

This matters because discussions of allegedly suppressed unconventional-energy researchers sometimes use phrases such as “mysterious death” for cases where even the manner of death is disputed. Mallove is different. There is no need to infer foul play from coincidence, unusual circumstances or his professional activities. He really was violently killed.

The later court record supplies considerably more context. In its January 2025 decision in *Brown v. Commissioner of Correction*, the Connecticut Appellate Court recounted that Mallove had recently evicted Roy and Patricia Anderson from the Salem Turnpike property and had travelled from New Hampshire to clean it. Patricia Anderson’s son, Chad Schaffer, and Schaffer’s girlfriend, Candace Foster, had also lived there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. Commissioner of Correction:: 2025:: Connecticut Appellate Court Decisions:: Connecticut Case Law:: Connecticut Law...</span></span></span>

The same record describes how the investigation changed direction. Two other men, Gary McAvoy and Joseph Reilly, were arrested in 2005, but investigators subsequently eliminated them as sources of relevant DNA profiles and the state dropped the charges. In 2009, new witnesses directed investigators towards Schaffer, Foster and people connected with the former tenants. One witness reported seeing Schaffer’s bloody clothing; another told police that Schaffer had admitted assaulting Mallove and taking his possessions to make the incident appear to be a robbery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. Commissioner of Correction:: 2025:: Connecticut Appellate Court Decisions:: Connecticut Case Law:: Connecticut Law...</span></span></span>

That history is important for two reasons. First, it demonstrates that the case was genuinely difficult and that early investigative conclusions could be wrong. Second, it shows why the strongest claims should be separated carefully. “Mallove was murdered” is an established fact. “Every later allegation about precisely who participated and how” has a more complicated evidential history. Neither proposition, however, establishes that cold fusion was the motive.

## Why homicide does not establish a research motive

A ruling that a death was a homicide answers a forensic question: the death resulted from another person’s actions rather than accident, suicide or natural causes. It does not identify the killer’s ultimate purpose. In Mallove’s case, a research-suppression hypothesis therefore requires an additional evidential bridge between the violence and his scientific activities.

The known circumstances provide a competing, much more direct chain of events. Mallove was killed at a property from which tenants had recently been evicted, while he was clearing that property. The later investigation centred on the evicted household. The Connecticut Appellate Court record says witnesses described bloody clothing, admissions concerning the assault, disposal of evidence and an attempt to stage the crime as a robbery. Schaffer ultimately pleaded guilty to first-degree manslaughter and third-degree robbery in 2012.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. Commissioner of Correction:: 2025:: Connecticut Appellate Court Decisions:: Connecticut Case Law:: Connecticut Law...</span></span></span>

Contemporary and later reporting described the same basic property-dispute setting. Associated Press coverage before Schaffer’s trial noted that Mallove had been cleaning the rental after Schaffer’s parents were evicted. Reporting after Mozelle Brown’s 2014 conviction likewise said police alleged that Brown and Schaffer attacked Mallove outside the property as he prepared it for rental following the eviction of Schaffer’s mother and stepfather.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhregister.com/news/article/Norwich-man-to-go-on-trial-in-New-Hampshire-11540611.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhregister.com">[New Haven Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhregister.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Haven RegisterNorwich man to go on trial in New Hampshire scientist&#x27;s killingApril 9, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2012</span></span></span>

A detailed account of the cold-case investigation provides an even more specific alleged trigger. According to Schaffer’s account to detectives, he learnt that Mallove was disposing of belongings left at the house and went there with Brown to retrieve property. Schaffer eventually admitted striking Mallove while alleging that Brown inflicted the fatal violence. Investigators believed Mallove’s possessions and vehicle were then taken in an effort to make the killing look like a robbery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://davidkushner.com/article/the-coldest-case/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: davidkushner.com">[David Kushner]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">davidkushner.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">David Kushner The Coldest Case – David Kushner</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">David KushnerThe Coldest Case – David KushnerJuly 7, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2016</span></span></span>

None of this means every detail of that reconstruction is beyond challenge. Witness accounts changed, the investigation initially targeted the [wrong suspects]({{ 'wrong-suspects/' | relative_url }}), and subsequent litigation exposed a serious disclosure problem. But it does mean that a research-motive theory has to compete with a documented local connection between Mallove, the location of his death and people associated with the recently evicted tenants. A suppression theory cannot become the default explanation merely because Mallove’s scientific career was controversial.

## Why the suppression theory nevertheless took hold

The suspicion did not arise from nothing. Mallove was not simply a scientist who happened to have an eccentric private interest. He was one of the most visible American advocates of cold fusion and had publicly argued that mainstream institutions had treated the subject unfairly.

The timing was particularly striking. Only months before his death, the US Department of Energy agreed to conduct a new review of cold-fusion evidence. Contemporary coverage quoted Mallove responding enthusiastically to that development, saying that a major new window on physics had opened. The review represented a noteworthy moment for a field that had been largely rejected by mainstream physics following the Fleischmann-Pons controversy of 1989.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa3.org/archive/asa/200403/0244.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa3.org">[American Scientific Affiliation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa3.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Scientific Affiliation ASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Scientific AffiliationASA - March 2004: Credibility for cold fusion?March 25, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2004</span></span></span>

Mallove’s collaborators also regarded his work as consequential. Material preserved on a website devoted to his writings describes him as the founder of the New Energy Foundation, editor of *Infinite Energy* and an advocate not only of cold fusion but of other unconventional-energy concepts. After his death, collaborators explicitly linked the loss to the future of alternative-energy research and complained about the effects on that community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eugenemallove.com/em_investigation.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eugenemallove.com">[eugenemallove.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eugenemallove.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Press Releases on Mallove Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 24, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2005</span></span></span>

Those facts explain why a murder that might otherwise have been understood primarily as a violent property dispute acquired a second life as a scientific-suppression case. Alternative-energy accounts emphasised the conjunction: an outspoken researcher was promoting potentially disruptive technologies, institutional interest in cold fusion appeared to be reviving, and he was suddenly murdered. Some explicitly presented his death within a broader narrative of suppression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectcamelot.org/mallove.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectcamelot.org">[projectcamelot.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectcamelot.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Camelot &#124; In Tribute &#124; Eugene Mallove</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Camelot &#124; In Tribute &#124; Eugene Mallove</span></span></span>

But this is an argument from **timing, significance and possible incentive**, rather than evidence identifying the motive of the actual attackers. It establishes why supporters might have suspected a connection. It does not establish that such a connection existed.

## The 2025 court ruling changes the case — but not in that way

There is an important complication that older summaries of Mallove’s murder now miss. Mozelle Brown was convicted in 2014 of murder and conspiracy to commit murder and received a 58-year prison sentence. In January 2025, however, the Connecticut Appellate Court ordered those convictions vacated and directed that Brown receive a new trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jud.ct.gov/lawlib/lawlibnews/Posts/Post.aspx?Id=6178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jud.ct.gov">[Connecticut Judicial Branch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jud.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Connecticut Judicial Branch Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Connecticut Judicial BranchHome - CT Judicial Law Libraries&#x27; NewsLogJanuary 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2025</span></span></span>

The reason matters. The court found a due-process violation involving the prosecution’s failure to disclose information about an informal understanding with cooperating witness Candace Foster. The court concluded that statements concerning the state’s practice of providing consideration to cooperating witnesses were material and that there was a reasonable probability the trial result could have differed had the information been disclosed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. Commissioner of Correction:: 2025:: Connecticut Appellate Court Decisions:: Connecticut Case Law:: Connecticut Law...</span></span></span>

That is significant evidence about the reliability and fairness of Brown’s prosecution. It means Brown’s 2014 conviction should no longer be presented as an uncomplicated final confirmation of his guilt.

It does **not**, however, amount to judicial evidence for a cold-fusion assassination theory. The appellate decision did not uncover communications with an energy company, government programme, intelligence organisation or scientific rival. It did not find that Mallove had been targeted because of his research. Nor did it reject the documented connection between the crime scene and the former tenants. Its holding concerned undisclosed information bearing on a cooperating witness and the fairness of Brown’s trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. Commissioner of Correction:: 2025:: Connecticut Appellate Court Decisions:: Connecticut Case Law:: Connecticut Law...</span></span></span>

This creates an important evidential distinction. The 2025 decision weakens reliance on **Brown’s conviction as proof of Brown’s individual criminal responsibility**. It does not strengthen the separate proposition that **Mallove was murdered to suppress unconventional-energy research**. Those are different questions.

## What a research-suppression claim would need

For a research motive to move from possibility to a well-supported explanation, evidence would need to connect the people responsible for Mallove’s death — or somebody directing them — to his scientific work. The existing public record instead offers extensive evidence of a local property connection while leaving the alleged research connection largely inferential.

Several kinds of evidence could materially change that assessment: communications showing that Mallove had been threatened specifically over his research; payments or instructions connecting an attacker to an outside sponsor; credible evidence that somebody sought particular notebooks, prototypes or research files rather than ordinary possessions; documented surveillance tied to his scientific activities; admissions by a participant identifying suppression as the objective; or independently corroborated evidence that a person or organisation with a demonstrable interest in stopping Mallove arranged the attack.

The distinction is especially important because some facts that sound suspicious in isolation have limited discriminatory value. Mallove being active in cold fusion shortly before his death shows that he had potentially controversial work underway. His criticism of established scientific institutions demonstrates conflict. The Department of Energy’s renewed review shows that cold fusion was receiving fresh attention. None identifies who attacked him or explains their intentions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa3.org/archive/asa/200403/0244.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa3.org">[American Scientific Affiliation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa3.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Scientific Affiliation ASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Scientific AffiliationASA - March 2004: Credibility for cold fusion?March 25, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2004</span></span></span>

Conversely, the property-dispute evidence has the advantage of proximity. It places people connected with a recent eviction in the narrative of the crime itself and is supported by witness statements, admissions described in court records and subsequent pleas and prosecutions. Even after allowing for the weaknesses exposed in Brown’s case, that is a qualitatively different kind of evidence from reasoning that Mallove’s research *could* have threatened powerful interests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/connecticut/court-of-appeals/2025/ac46658.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia LawBrown v. ## What can responsibly be concluded


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Mallove’s case deserves a more precise description than either “mysterious scientist death” or “cold-fusion assassination”. He was unquestionably the victim of homicide, and the violence abruptly removed an unusually energetic advocate for unconventional-energy research. His death therefore had real consequences for the community he helped organise and understandably generated suspicion among colleagues already convinced that cold fusion faced institutional resistance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.ct.gov/despp/press-releases/2009/50000-reward-being-offered-from-homicide-investigation-norwich-2004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.ct.gov">[CT.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">50000 reward being offered from homicide investigation norwich 2004</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT.gov50000 Reward Being Offered from Homicide Investigation Norwich 2004...</span></span></span>

</div>

The public evidential record, however, does not demonstrate that scientific suppression motivated the killing. The documented investigative trail leads instead towards a dispute surrounding the recently vacated rental property, while the strongest arguments for a research connection rely principally on Mallove’s prominence, the potentially disruptive implications attributed to his work and the timing of his death.

The 2025 reversal of Brown’s convictions adds genuine uncertainty about one defendant’s prosecution and warns against treating every element of the state’s case as settled. But it does not fill the central evidential gap in the suppression theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jud.ct.gov/lawlib/lawlibnews/Posts/Post.aspx?Id=6178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jud.ct.gov">[Connecticut Judicial Branch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jud.ct.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Connecticut Judicial Branch Home</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Connecticut Judicial BranchHome - CT Judicial Law Libraries&#x27; NewsLogJanuary 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2025</span></span></span>

For the broader question of suspicious deaths associated with UFO, [antigravity]({{ 'antigravity/' | relative_url }}) and unconventional-energy research, Mallove is therefore a useful test case precisely because the homicide itself is so clear. **Proof that a researcher was murdered is not proof that the research was the reason.** Establishing that second proposition requires independent evidence of motive, and in Mallove’s case the publicly documented evidence has not established that link.
